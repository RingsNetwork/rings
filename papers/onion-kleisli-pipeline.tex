\noindent
The Rings onion data plane interprets two layers, \emph{relay} and
\emph{exit}, and answers along the reversed path through relays that keep
per-circuit return state and forward one client-bound ciphertext
byte-identical.  We generalise the circuit to a term over a static
signature $\Sigma$ of operation symbols: nodes are partial
$\Sigma$-algebras that register symbols, never applications; a circuit is a
client-sealed loop $\cl\to g\to\cdots\to g\to\cl$ that evaluates one
application per hop and composes them in the Kleisli category of the hop
effect monad, with $\relay$ as the identity: an onion Kleisli pipeline,
not a monad.  Each segment holds $s=2$
relays, the guard counting in both end segments; every hop parses one
uniform layer of a fixed-length Sphinx-style header; values travel in a carry slot of one width per loop, enciphered with AEZ, one
length-preserving layer per relay and one authenticated layer per
consumer; replies to world-facing sessions ride single-use reply blocks
under credit flow control.  We state eleven laws, among them
noninterference over hop views, position hiding, client path authority
(which admits Arrow composition and excludes monadic bind), byte
unlinkability, and
single-use admission, list the residual leakage, and map each law to a
test obligation.\par\vspace{0.6\baselineskip}}}

\section{Introduction}

Today's circuit is the term $\relay^{k}\then(\mathit{svc},\mathit{body})$
evaluated on the forward path and answered on its reverse
(\S\ref{sec:current}).  This paper states the general structure, the
onion Kleisli pipeline, of which today's circuit is the degenerate
instance, as definitions D1--D9 and laws L1--L10 and L5$'$ of issue \#834,
numbered identically.  Design lineage: Sphinx headers and
wide-block payloads~\cite{Danezis2009}, reply blocks~\cite{Chaum1981,
Danezis2003}, loops~\cite{Piotrowska2017}, AEZ~\cite{Hoang2015}, Kleisli
semantics~\cite{Moggi1991}, generic effects~\cite{Plotkin2003,
Plotkin2009}, and the Arrow/Selective hierarchy~\cite{Hughes2000,
Lindley2011,Mokhov2019}.

\section{Signature and Algebras}\label{sec:sig}

\begin{defD}{D1}{signature}
$\Sigma$ is a finite, statically defined table of symbols; each $f\in\Sigma$
has
\begin{multline*}
\spec(f)=(\mathit{name},\Args_f,\In_f,\Out_f,\\
W(f),L(f),\pos(f),\quota(f))
\end{multline*}
with $\mathit{name}$ identifying the backend (e.g.\ \code{jev.typesafe}),
$\Args_f$ client constants, $\In_f,\Out_f$ carried types, $W(f)\in\mathbb{N}$
the exact output width in bytes of a fixed-width symbol, $L(f)$ the exact
latency class, $\pos(f)\subseteq\{\Int,\WF\}$, and $\quota(f)$ the
admission budget.  $W,L$ are protocol data.  The identity symbol is
\[
\relay:X\to X,\qquad L(\relay)=0 ;
\]
$\relay$ has no $W$: it preserves the slot width $C$ (L3).  A stream
symbol has no $W$ either: its frames are bounded by the slot of their
loop (L3), which the client chooses.  $\Sigma$ is
closed: a name outside $\Sigma$ is rejected at configuration, at
descriptor decode, and at route construction; nothing falls back to
\code{tcp}, and operator-named services are not accepted.  An operator
exposing TCP targets registers \code{tcp} and restricts targets by policy.
\end{defD}

\begin{defD}{D2}{registration}
Node $n$ is a partial $\Sigma$-algebra: a set $\Sigma_n\subseteq\Sigma$ and,
for each $f\in\Sigma_n$, a generic effect~\cite{Plotkin2003}
\[
\sem{f}_n:\Args_f\times\In_f\to M\,\Out_f ,
\]
$M$ the hop effect monad (failure, delay, world I/O).  $n$ publishes
$f\in\Sigma_n$, never an application.  Every published symbol,
$\relay$ included, carries $n$'s process epoch $e_n$, drawn afresh at each
process start: $\relay$ in \code{OnlineNodeDescriptor::capabilities}
extended with $e_n$, every other $f$ in one signed descriptor per symbol
with $e_n$ and $\quota(f)$.
Registration includes $\relay$: $\Sigma_n\ne\emptyset\Rightarrow\relay\in
\Sigma_n$, so a neighbour's registry entry does not reveal whether a relay
is adjacent to a symbol hop.
\end{defD}

A node is a handler~\cite{Plotkin2009} for the operations it registers;
$\Int$ symbols are one-shot.

\begin{defD}{D2$'$}{session symbol}
A $\WF$ session symbol (\code{tcp}, \code{https}) is a stream symbol:
$\Args_f\ni(\sid,\mathit{target})$ with a session id
$\sid\in\{0,1\}^{128}$ drawn by $\cl$ per session, and
$\In_f=\Out_f=\mathrm{Frame}$,
\[
\enc(\mathrm{data}(w))=\mathtt{00}\,\|\,w,\qquad
\enc(\mathrm{fin})=\mathtt{01},
\]
with $|w|\le C_0-2$ (L3).  $h$ opens $\sid$ at its first forward loop,
binding it to $\mathit{target}$, and rejects a later loop of $\sid$ with
another target.  $\mathrm{data}(\epsilon)$ is a credit-only loop.
$\mathrm{fin}$ closes one direction; $h$ drops $\sid$ once both directions
are closed, or once no forward loop of $\sid$ has arrived for $V$.
For \code{https} the frames carry the HTTPS request/response encoding;
TLS tunnels use \code{tcp}.
\end{defD}

\begin{defD}{D3}{application}
An application is $(f,\bar a)$, $\bar a\in\Args_f$, typed
$(f,\bar a):\In_f\rightsquigarrow\Out_f$; it is sealed in the layer of the
hop that evaluates it and visible to no other hop.
\end{defD}

\section{Pipelines}\label{sec:pipeline}

\begin{defD}{D4a}{pipeline}
Pipelines are the morphisms of the free category $\Free(G_\Sigma)$ on
the graph $G_\Sigma$ with types as vertices and applications as edges;
composition is
\[
\frac{\theta:X\rightsquigarrow Y\qquad \vartheta:Y\rightsquigarrow Z}
     {\theta\then\vartheta:X\rightsquigarrow Z}.
\]
$P=(f_1,\bar a_1)\then\cdots\then(f_n,\bar a_n)$ is well formed iff
\begin{gather*}
1\le n\le n_{\max}=4,\qquad\forall k<n:\ \Out_{f_k}=\In_{f_{k+1}},\\
\forall k<n:\ \Int\in\pos(f_k),\\
f_n\text{ is a }\WF\text{ session symbol}\ \Rightarrow\ n=1 .
\end{gather*}
Violations are rejected at construction.  The bound $n_{\max}$ is
\code{MAX\_ONION\_LOOP\_SYMBOLS}.
\end{defD}

\begin{defD}{D4b}{Kleisli denotation}
For $\phi:X\to MY$, $\psi:Y\to MZ$ in $\Kl(M)$~\cite{Moggi1991},
$\phi\then\psi=\mu_Z\circ M\psi\circ\phi$.  A placed application
$(f,\bar a,h)$ with $f\in\Sigma_h$ is an edge of the graph $G^N_\Sigma$
of placed applications, $N$ the nodes; the unique functor
$\sem{-}:\Free(G^N_\Sigma)\to\Kl(M)$ sending it to
$\sem{f}_h(\bar a,-)$ gives, for $P$ placed on $h_1..h_n$,
\[
\sem{P}(v_0)=\bigl(\sem{f_1}_{h_1}(\bar a_1,-)\then\cdots\then
\sem{f_n}_{h_n}(\bar a_n,-)\bigr)(v_0),
\]
and $\sem{P\then P'}=\sem{P}\then\sem{P'}$.  The client consumes
$\sem{P}(v_0)$.
\end{defD}

\begin{lawL}{L1}{identity}
$\sem{\relay}=\eta$, hence $\relay\then f=f=f\then\relay$ on values.  The
loop builder factors through $\nf$, which deletes $\relay$ applications:
$\LoopOf(P)=\LoopOf(\nf P)$, so $\LoopOf(\relay\then P)\eqd\LoopOf(P)$ and every
hop's view is unchanged.
\end{lawL}

\begin{lawL}{L2}{associativity}
$(f\then g)\then h=f\then(g\then h)$ on values; immediate from the monad
laws, and $\Free(G_\Sigma)$ has no bracketing.
\end{lawL}

$\nf$ and L1 on views apply from Phase~2a.  Phase~1 admits only
$\relay^{k}\then(f,\bar a)$ with $\WF\in\pos(f)$, which
is today's circuit (\S\ref{sec:current}).

\section{The Loop}\label{sec:loop}

\begin{defD}{D4c, D5}{loop, segment}
Let $\cl$ be the client and $g$ its entry guard.  Segment $k\in[0,n]$
is $s=2$ relays $r_{k,1}..r_{k,s}$ (\code{ONION\_SEGMENT\_RELAYS}) from producer $p_k$ to consumer $c_k$,
\begin{gather*}
p_0=\cl,\quad p_k=h_k,\quad c_k=h_{k+1},\quad c_n=\cl,\\
\LoopOf(P):\ \cl\to r_{0,1..s}\to h_1\to r_{1,1..s}\to\cdots\\
\to h_n\to r_{n,1..s}\to\cl .
\end{gather*}
Positions are $i\in[1,H]$; position $i$ evaluates $f_i$,
which is $\relay$ on every $r_{k,j}$.  $\LoopOf(\nf P)$ is the symbol word
$\relay^{s}f_1\relay^{s}\cdots f_n\relay^{s}$.
\end{defD}

\begin{lawL}{L7}{guard closure}
$r_{0,1}=r_{n,s}=g$: the guard is relay $1$ of segment $0$ and relay $s$
of segment $n$.  The other $H-2$ positions carry pairwise distinct DIDs,
distinct from $g$.  $g$ is the only hop adjacent to $\cl$.
\end{lawL}

\begin{proposition}[Loop size]\label{prop:size}
With $n$ symbol hops and $n+1$ segments of $s$ relays,
\begin{gather*}
H(n,s)=n+(n+1)s=(s+1)n+s,\\
\#\mathrm{DID}=H-1,\qquad E=H+1\ \text{cells},\\
\Hm=H(n_{\max},s)=(s+1)\cdot4+s=14 .
\end{gather*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Segments contribute $(n+1)s$ positions; L7 repeats exactly one DID; the
$H$ positions lie on a path from $\cl$ to $\cl$ of $H+1$ edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Segment length]\label{prop:s}
$s=2$ is the least $s$ at which no single node is adjacent to both $\cl$
and a symbol hop.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$s=0$ violates L7.  At $s=1$, $r_{0,1}=r_{0,s}=r_{n,1}=r_{n,s}=g$, so $g$
alone is adjacent to $\cl$, $h_1$ and $h_n$, and for $n=1$ links $\cl$ to
its world-facing $h_1$.  At $s=2$, $g$ neighbours only $\cl$, $r_{0,2}$,
$r_{n,1}$.
\end{proof}

Relays do not mix ($L(\relay)=0$), so a timing-correlating coalition skips
middle relays: $s\ge3$ adds $n+1$ edges of latency per relay and no
anonymity.  At $(s,n)=(2,1)$, $E=6$ and the forward prefix
$g,r_{0,2},h_1$ is Tor's three-hop circuit~\cite{Dingledine2004}.

Route validation checks $H=(s+1)n+s$ with exactly $s$ relays between
consecutive symbol hops, and admits $g$ exactly twice, at positions $1$
and $H$ (\code{has\_duplicate\_dids}); selection fails closed when fewer
than $H-1$ distinct eligible DIDs exist.  \code{MAX\_ONION\_LOOP\_HOPS}
$=\Hm$ is derived from $s$ and $n_{\max}$ and replaces
\code{MAX\_ONION\_CIRCUIT\_HOPS}~$=8$; \code{DEFAULT\_ONION\_ROUTE\_HOPS}
and \code{OnionRouteRequest::\{hop\_count, allow\_short\_paths\}} are
removed, since a short path is a distinguishable segment length (D5).

\section{Uniform Layer and Timing}\label{sec:layer}

\begin{defD}{D6}{uniform layer}
Every position $i$ has one layer shape
\[
\lambda_i=(f_i,\bar a_i,\nxt_i,e_i,x,\nu_i,\kappa_i,\gamma_{i+1},
\alpha_{i+1})
\]
of $\ell$ bytes, with $\bar a_i$ padded to $A$ bytes: epoch $e_i$, one
expiry per loop,
\begin{gather*}
x=\lceil t_{\mathrm{build}}/Q\rceil\,Q+X_0,\\
Q=30\,\mathrm{s},\qquad X_0=V-Q=120\,\mathrm{s},
\end{gather*}
a function of the build quantum only ($X_0$ is today's
\code{ONION\_FORWARD\_PAYLOAD\_TTL\_MS}), replay nonce
$\nu_i$, carry keys $\kappa_i=(k^{\mathrm{in}}_i;k^{\mathrm{out}}_{i,1..s+1})$,
and the next hop's MAC $\gamma_{i+1}$ and key transport $\alpha_{i+1}$
(Def.~\ref{def:header}).  A relay has $\bar a_i=()$ and random
$k^{\mathrm{out}}$.  Each loop has one class $b\in\mathfrak B$, today's
cell buckets above 4\,KiB ($16$\,KiB to $12$\,MiB), chosen by $\cl$; the
cell on every edge of the loop, SURB replies included, is
\[
(\chi_{i+1},y_i),\quad \chi=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),\quad
|\beta|=\Hm\ell,\quad |y_i|=C_b ,
\]
padded to $b$, with $C_b=b-|\chi|-F$ and $F$ the cell framing.
\end{defD}

With today's encodings (239-byte ElGamal-AEAD envelope as $\alpha$, 43-byte
DIDs, 52-byte public keys), $48$-byte AEZ keys and a $16$-byte $\gamma$,
$\ell(s,A)=409+A+48(s+2)$, so $\ell(2,64)=665$\,B and $|\beta|=9310$\,B:
every loop cell uses at least the 16\,KiB bucket.

\begin{definition}[Fixed-length header]\label{def:header}
Hop $i$ derives $K_i$ from $\alpha_i$ and its secret key and expands
$\rho_i=\PRG(K_i)\in\{0,1\}^{(\Hm+1)\ell}$; $\rho[a,b)$ is a byte range in
units of $\ell$.  It checks the $16$-byte $\gamma_i=\MAC_{K_i}(\beta_i)$ and peels
\[
(\beta_i\,\|\,0^{\ell})\oplus\rho_i=\lambda_i\,\|\,\beta_{i+1}.
\]
The client builds, for $1\le i<H$, with $R$ uniform,
\begin{gather*}
\phi_0=\epsilon,\quad
\phi_i=(\phi_{i-1}\|0^{\ell})\oplus\rho_i[\Hm{-}i{+}1,\Hm{+}1),\\
\beta_H=\bigl((\lambda_H\|R)\oplus\rho_H[0,\Hm{-}H{+}1)\bigr)\,\|\,
\phi_{H-1},\\
\beta_i=\bigl(\lambda_i\,\|\,\beta_{i+1}[0,\Hm{-}1)\bigr)\oplus
\rho_i[0,\Hm),\quad \gamma_i=\MAC_{K_i}(\beta_i).
\end{gather*}
\end{definition}

This is Sphinx's header~\cite{Danezis2009}: per-hop PRG filler with MACs
the client precomputes.  $\alpha_{i+1}$ is either a per-layer envelope
carried in $\lambda_i$ (as above) or a blinding of $\alpha_i$ (Sphinx's
single group element, roughly halving $\ell$); L5$'$ holds for both.

\begin{proposition}[Header correctness]\label{prop:header}
For $H\le\Hm$ and $1\le i<H$, peeling $\beta_i$ under $K_i$ yields
$(\lambda_i,\beta_{i+1})$, peeling $\beta_H$ yields $\lambda_H$, and every
$\gamma_i$ verifies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By induction down from $H$, the last $i-1$ blocks of $\beta_i$ are
$\phi_{i-1}$: true at $H$ by construction, and expanding $\beta_i$ and
$\phi_i$ carries it from $i+1$ to $i$.  So the first $\Hm$ blocks of
$(\beta_i\|0^\ell)\oplus\rho_i$ are $\lambda_i\|\beta_{i+1}[0,\Hm{-}1)$,
and its last block $\rho_i[\Hm,\Hm{+}1)$ is the last block of $\phi_i$,
hence of $\beta_{i+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Header view]\label{lem:header}
For $H\le\Hm$, hop $i$'s view of $(\chi_i,\chi_{i+1})$ is, up to
$\Hm\,\epsilon_{\PRG}+\epsilon_{\MAC}+\epsilon_{\alpha}$, a function of
$\lambda_i$ and fresh coins alone, independent of $H$, $i$, and every
other layer.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$|\chi|$ is constant.  Hop $i$ unmasks $\lambda_i$ and $\beta_{i+1}$, and
reads $\alpha_{i+1},\gamma_{i+1}$ from $\lambda_i$.  Every block of
$\beta_{i+1}$ but the last is masked by some $\rho_j$, $j\ne i$: by
$\rho_{i+1}$ when $i+1<H$, and by $\rho_H$ or an upstream term of
$\phi_{H-1}$ when $i+1=H$.  The last block is $\rho_i[\Hm,\Hm{+}1)$,
which $i$ computes itself.  Hybrids replacing the $\rho_j$, $j\ne i$, one
key at a time by uniform strings make $\beta_{i+1}$ uniform apart from
that block; $\gamma_{i+1}$ is then a $\MAC$ under an unseen key.
\end{proof}

\begin{lawL}{L3}{width}
The carry slot has width $C=C_b$ on every edge of a loop of class $b$.
With $C_0=C-\tau$, for fixed-width symbols the client picks the least
$b$ with $|\enc(v_0)|<C_0$ and $W(f_k)<C_0$ for all $k$; for a stream
symbol, whose frames carry up to $C_0-2$ bytes, $b$ is the client's choice
between throughput and leakage (Table~\ref{tab:leak}).  For a fixed-width
symbol $W$ factors through the output type ($W(f)=w(\Out_f)$, so the
width a consumer unpads is fixed by its own $\In$); a stream symbol
(D2$'$) carries one frame per loop or reply, and larger bodies span
several loops.  Further,
\[
\pad(b)=b\,\|\,\mathtt{80}\,\|\,\mathtt{00}^{C_0-|b|-1},\qquad
|\enc_f(w)|=W(f);
\]
an output with $|\enc_f(w)|>W(f)$, or a frame with $|\enc(w)|\ge C_0$,
fails closed, never truncated.
\end{lawL}

\begin{lawL}{L4}{timing}
$\dep_i=\arr_i+L(f_i)$ exactly.  An evaluation unfinished at $\dep_i$ is
dropped, never released late.  The one exception is a reply of a $\WF$
session symbol (Algorithm~\ref{alg:reply}): it leaves when world bytes are
ready, at any time before the SURB's $x$.
\end{lawL}

\section{Carry}\label{sec:carry}

Write $\Enc{\tau}{k},\Dec{\tau}{k}$ for AEZ v5~\cite{AEZv5,Hoang2015}
encryption and decryption with empty nonce and associated data;
$|\Enc{\tau}{k}(m)|=|m|+\tau$, and $\Dec{\tau}{k}$ returns $\bot$ unless
the deciphered block ends in $\tau$ zero bytes.

\begin{defD}{D7}{carry}
For segment $k$ the client draws fresh keys
$k_{r_{k,1}},..,k_{r_{k,s}},k_{c_k}$ and places them
\[
\kappa_{p_k}^{\mathrm{out}}=(k_{r_{k,1}},..,k_{r_{k,s}},k_{c_k}),\quad
\kappa_{r_{k,j}}^{\mathrm{in}}=k_{r_{k,j}},\quad
\kappa_{c_k}^{\mathrm{in}}=k_{c_k}.
\]
With $b_k=\pad(\enc(v_k))$ and $\tau=16$:
\begin{gather*}
y_{k,0}=\Enc{0}{k_{r_{k,1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\Enc{0}{k_{r_{k,s}}}
\circ\Enc{\tau}{k_{c_k}}(b_k),\\
y_{k,j}=\Dec{0}{k_{r_{k,j}}}(y_{k,j-1}),\qquad
b_k=\Dec{\tau}{k_{c_k}}(y_{k,s}).
\end{gather*}
A value of type $()$ is encoded as $\epsilon$, so every consumer
authenticates $\pad(\epsilon)$ and processing is uniform.
\end{defD}

\begin{lemma}[Relay carry]\label{lem:carry}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:prim} and L9, at $i=r_{k,j}$ the strings
$y_{k,j-1}$ and $y_{k,j}$ are uniform on $\{0,1\}^C$ and independent of
$v_k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$y_{k,j}=\Dec{0}{k_{r_{k,j}}}(y_{k,j-1})$ is $\Pi_k$ applied to one
input, for $k=k_{r_{k,j+1}}$ ($\tau=0$) or $k=k_{c_k}$ ($\tau=16$, input
$b_k\|0^{16}$): a fresh key not held by $i$ and used once by L9
(Prop.~\ref{prop:onekey}), hence a uniform string.  $y_{k,j-1}$ is its
image under a permutation keyed by $i$'s own key.
\end{proof}

Since $s$ is constant, $|\kappa_i|$ is constant and reveals no position.
AEZ v5 is implemented in-repo on RustCrypto \code{aes::hazmat}
(constant-time, \code{wasm32}); keys are raw $48$-byte strings, a key with
a zero subkey $I$, $J$ or $L$ is rejected, and $\mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$
doubling is branch-free.  $C\ge32$ keeps every call on the AEZ-core
path, never AEZ-tiny.

\begin{proposition}[One value per key]\label{prop:onekey}
Under L9 every $k_{r_{k,j}}$ and $k_{c_k}$ is applied to exactly one
$C$-byte value: enciphered once by $p_k$, deciphered once by its holder.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Keys are fresh per layer (D7).  $p_k$ enciphers its single output once
under each key of $\kappa^{\mathrm{out}}_{p_k}$.  The holder of $k$
deciphers only a cell whose layer names $k$, and L9 admits that layer
once, across restarts by the process epoch.  Without L9 a replayed cell
with a modified carry makes the holder a chosen-ciphertext oracle under a
fixed key: L9 is load-bearing for AEZ.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\mathrm{Hop}_i(\frm,\chi,y,\arr)$: one position, all symbols}
\label{alg:hop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $(\lambda,\chi')\gets\peel(\mathit{sk}_i,\chi)$;\ \textbf{if}
  $\gamma$ fails \textbf{then} drop
\If{$x\notin(\arr,\arr{+}V]\vee e\ne\mathrm{epoch}_i\vee\nu\in R_i
  \vee R_i^{\frm}\text{ full}$} \State drop
  \Comment{L9} \EndIf
\State $R_i\gets R_i\cup\{(\nu,\arr+V)\}$
\If{$f=\relay$} \State $y'\gets\Dec{0}{k^{\mathrm{in}}}(y)$
\Else
  \State $b\gets\Dec{\tau}{k^{\mathrm{in}}}(y)$;\ \textbf{if} $b=\bot$
    \textbf{then} drop \Comment{L8}
  \If{$f$ is a session symbol, $\bar a=(\sid,\mathit{target})$}
    \State $Q_{h,\sid}\gets Q_{h,\sid}\cup\{(\nxt,\chi',\kappa^{\mathrm{out}},x)\}$
      \Comment{D8}
    \State apply $\enc^{-1}\pad^{-1}(b)$ to session $\sid$ (D2$'$);\
      \Return
  \EndIf
  \State $w\gets\sem{f}(\bar a,\enc^{-1}\pad^{-1}(b))$ by $\arr+L(f)$
    \Comment{L4}
  \If{late $\vee\ |\enc_f(w)|>W(f)$} \State drop \Comment{L3} \EndIf
  \State $y'\gets\Enc{0}{k^{\mathrm{out}}_1}\circ\cdots\circ
    \Enc{0}{k^{\mathrm{out}}_s}\circ\Enc{\tau}{k^{\mathrm{out}}_{s+1}}
    (\pad\,\enc_f(w))$
\EndIf
\State release $(\chi',y')$ to $\nxt$ at $\arr+L(f)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\mathrm{Reply}_{h,\sid}(t_0)$: world bytes or EOF ready at
session $\sid$ of $h$}
\label{alg:reply}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $Q_{h,\sid}\gets\{\upsilon\in Q_{h,\sid}: t_0<x_\upsilon\}$
  \Comment{expired SURBs discarded}
\If{$Q_{h,\sid}=\emptyset$} \State pause reading;\ \Return
  \Comment{Inv.~\ref{inv:credit}}
\EndIf
\State $\upsilon=(\nxt,\chi,k_1..k_{s+1},x)\gets$ the element of least $x$
\State $Q_{h,\sid}\gets Q_{h,\sid}\setminus\{\upsilon\}$
  \Comment{single use}
\State $w\gets\mathrm{data}(\le C_0-2$ world bytes$)$, or $\mathrm{fin}$ at EOF
\State $y'\gets\Enc{0}{k_1}\circ\cdots\circ\Enc{0}{k_s}\circ
  \Enc{\tau}{k_{s+1}}(\pad\,\enc(w))$
\State release $(\chi,y')$ to $\nxt$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{defD}{D6$'$}{client position}
$\cl$ is position $H+1$.  $\lambda_H$ carries, in place of
$\alpha_{H+1}$, a fresh tag $t_\cl\in\{0,1\}^{128}$ padded to $|\alpha|$;
the guard sends $(\chi_{H+1},y_H)$ with
$\chi_{H+1}=(t_\cl,\beta_{H+1},\gamma_{H+1})$, so $|\chi_{H+1}|=|\chi|$
and the cell is padded to $b$ like every other.  $\nxt_H$ is the DID of
$\cl$, whose connection $g$ already holds; $\gamma_{H+1}$ is uniform and
ignored, since $k_{c_n}$ authenticates the carry.  With blinded key
transport, where $\lambda_H$ has no $\alpha_{H+1}$ field, $t_\cl$ takes a
$16$-byte tag field present in every layer (uniform elsewhere), so $\ell$
stays uniform and $\nxt_H$ still names $\cl$.
$\cl$ keeps $T:t_\cl\mapsto(k_{c_n},\text{loop})$, removes the entry on
first use or at $x$, and drops a cell with an unknown tag.  A SURB is the
suffix of a forward loop and ends in that loop's tag.
\end{defD}

$t_\cl$ is uniform and seen only by $g$ at position $H$ and by $\cl$, so it
links nothing the guard cannot already link by timing.

\section{Reply Blocks}\label{sec:surb}

\begin{defD}{D8}{SURB}
A single-use reply block for session $\sid$ at $h$ is the client-sealed
loop suffix after $h$:
\[
\upsilon=(\nxt,\chi_\upsilon,k_{r_1},..,k_{r_s},k_{\cl},x_\upsilon).
\]
$h$ keeps the suffix of every forward loop of $\sid$ that reaches it
($\chi_{i+1}$, $\kappa^{\mathrm{out}}_i$, $x$) as one SURB in
$Q_{h,\sid}$, and spends it only on a reply of $\sid$; no SURB rides in a
carry value.
\end{defD}

\begin{proposition}[No SURB in the carry]\label{prop:nosurb}
A carried SURB forces $C>\Hm\ell$ on every edge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
L5$'$ fixes $|\chi_\upsilon|=|\chi|>|\beta|=\Hm\ell$ (a shorter reply
header would name the reply path), and L3 fixes one $C$ for every edge of a loop and its
replies.
\end{proof}

At $s=2$ that would at least double every cell ($9310$\,B of $\beta$ in
the carry besides the header's own), so credit arrives only as forward
loops: one forward cell per reply cell, data optional.

\begin{invariant}[Credit]\label{inv:credit}
For each session $\sid$ at $h$ let $Q_{h,\sid}$ be its unexpired SURBs
($t_\upsilon$ the arrival of $\upsilon$), $u(t)$ its forward loops received
and $r(t)$ its replies emitted by time $t$.  Then
\begin{gather*}
r(t)\le u(t),\qquad \upsilon\in Q_{h,\sid}\Rightarrow t<x_\upsilon\le
t_\upsilon+V,\\
|Q_{h,\sid}|=0\Rightarrow\text{\code{tcp} stops reading its socket.}
\end{gather*}
Each reply removes one $\upsilon$ from $Q_{h,\sid}$
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:reply}), produced as segment $n$ of D7 with
$p_n=h$; an expired $\upsilon$ is discarded unused; a new forward loop
resumes reading.  By the rule for $x$, a SURB lives at most $X_0+Q=V$,
and a session idle for longer than $V$ holds no credit.
\end{invariant}

\section{Security Laws}\label{sec:laws}

\begin{assumption}[Primitives]\label{as:prim}
AEZ is an ideal enciphering scheme~\cite{Hoang2015}: under a fresh key
a hop does not hold, $\Enc{\tau}{k}(m)=\Pi_k(m\,\|\,0^{\tau})$ for a
uniformly random permutation $\Pi_k$ of $\{0,1\}^{|m|+\tau}$, for
$\tau=0$ and $\tau=16$ alike; $\PRG$ and $\MAC$ are secure, and key transport
reveals $K_j$ to hop $j$ only.  Network delays $\delta$ are fixed per edge.
$\cl$ departs at $t_{\mathrm{build}}$, whose phase
$t_{\mathrm{build}}\bmod Q$ is uniform and independent of the loop; no hop
other than $g$ observes $t_{\mathrm{build}}$ beyond what $x$ reveals.
\end{assumption}

\begin{defD}{D9}{view}
Over client coins $\rho$ (keys, nonces, header randomness) and delays
$\delta$,
\begin{multline*}
\view_i=(f_i,\bar a_i,\vh i,\frm_i,\nxt_i,e_i,x,b,\\
\arr_i,\dep_i,\chi_i,y_{i-1},\chi_{i+1},y_i),
\end{multline*}
where $\vh i$ is the value position $i$ consumes: $\vh i=v_{k-1}$ (D7's
segment value, $v_0$ the client's input) when $i$ is the symbol hop $h_k$,
and $\vh i=\bot$ when $f_i=\relay$.
\end{defD}

\begin{lawL}{L5}{noninterference}
For loops $P,P'$ let
\begin{gather*}
\pi_i(P)=(f_i,\bar a_i,\vh i,\frm_i,\nxt_i,e_i,x,b),\\
\sigma(P)=f_1\cdots f_H,\\
\pi_i(P)=\pi_i(P')\ \wedge\ \sigma(P)=\sigma(P')\\
\Rightarrow\ \view_i(P)\eqc\view_i(P') .
\end{gather*}
Symbols may leak; $\bar a_j$ for positions $j\ne i$ and every segment
value $v_k$ other than $\vh i$ may not~\cite{Goguen1982}.  On the reply path of a session
symbol, arrival times follow the world (L4's exception) and are excluded
from $\view_i$ there; that residual is Table~\ref{tab:leak}'s session
reply row.
\end{lawL}

\begin{proposition}[L5 from D6, D7, L3, L4]\label{prop:ni}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:prim}, every loop built by
D4c--D7 satisfies L5.  Equality in distribution holds for the carry
alone; the header makes L5 computational (Lemma~\ref{lem:header}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lengths: $|\chi_i|$ and $|y_i|=C$ are constant (D6, L3).  Times:
$\arr_i=\sum_{j<i}(L(f_j)+\delta_j)$ by L4, a function of $\sigma,\delta$,
except on a session reply path, where L5 excludes them.
Headers: $\chi_i,\chi_{i+1}$ reveal only $\lambda_i$, fixed by $\pi_i$ up
to fresh coins (Lemma~\ref{lem:header}).  Carry: uniform at a relay
position (Lemma~\ref{lem:carry}).  If $i=c_k$, it learns $\vh i=v_k$,
which $\pi_i$ fixes.  If $i=p_k$, its output is computed from $\vh i$
and $\bar a_i$.  No component depends on $\bar a_j$ or $v_k$ otherwise.
\end{proof}

L5's premise fixes $\sigma$, hence $H$ and every index, so it says nothing
about length or position; L5$'$ does.

\begin{lawL}{L5$'$}{position hiding}
For loops $P,P'$ with $H(P),H(P')\le\Hm$ and positions $i$ of $P$, $i'$
of $P'$,
\begin{gather*}
\pi^{\circ}_i(P)=(f_i,\bar a_i,\vh i,\frm_i,\nxt_i,x,b),\\
\pi^{\circ}_i(P)=\pi^{\circ}_{i'}(P')\ \Rightarrow\
\mathrm{Adv}_i\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{hdr}}+\frac{|D_i-D'_{i'}|}{Q},
\end{gather*}
where $\mathrm{Adv}_i$ is the advantage in telling $\view_i(P)$ from
$\view_{i'}(P')$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{hdr}}=\Hm\,\epsilon_{\PRG}
+\epsilon_{\MAC}+\epsilon_{\alpha}$, and
$D_i=\sum_{j<i}(L(f_j)+\delta_j)$ is the delay from the client to
position $i$.  No hop learns its position, $H$, or the symbols elsewhere
in the loop beyond that bound, except the guard: $\frm=\cl$ or $\nxt=\cl$
names position $1$ or $H$, and $\pi^{\circ}$ records it.
\end{lawL}

\begin{proposition}[L5$'$ from Def.~\ref{def:header}]\label{prop:pos}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:prim}, every loop built by D4c--D7 satisfies
L5$'$, and the timing term is tight.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$|\chi|$ and $C$ do not depend on $H$ or $i$; $e_i$ is the hop's own, and
$x\in\pi^{\circ}_i$.  The carry is uniform at a relay position
(Lemma~\ref{lem:carry}) and fixed by $\vh i\in\pi^{\circ}_i$ at a
consumer.  The header view depends on $\lambda_i$, fixed by
$\pi^{\circ}_i$ up to fresh coins, and on nothing else
(Lemma~\ref{lem:header}); this costs $\epsilon_{\mathrm{hdr}}$.  Time:
$x$ fixes $t_{\mathrm{build}}\in(x-X_0-Q,\,x-X_0]$ (D6), so
$\arr_i-(x-X_0-Q)=u+D_i$ with $u$ uniform on $[0,Q)$
(Assumption~\ref{as:prim}), and the statistical distance between
$u+D_i$ and $u+D'_{i'}$ is $\min(1,|D_i-D'_{i'}|/Q)$, attained by a
threshold test.
\end{proof}

A uniform departure delay does not remove the term: any bounded uniform
offset leaves a shift of $D_i-D'_{i'}$ visible to within its width.  At
$Q=30$\,s a hop one second further from $\cl$ is told apart with
advantage about $3\%$; this is a Table~\ref{tab:leak} row.

\begin{lawL}{L6}{path authority}
$\nxt_i$ is a field of $\lambda_i$, fixed by $\cl$ at construction:
$\nxt_i$ is independent of every $v_k$.  No symbol chooses a hop.
\end{lawL}

\begin{proposition}[Admissible combinators]\label{prop:arrow}
L6 admits the composition fragment ($\then$, without $\mathit{arr}$ or
$\mathit{first}$) of Arrows~\cite{Hughes2000} and excludes monadic bind.
Selective branching~\cite{Mokhov2019} over client-sealed branches needs
L6 weakened to $\nxt_i\in N_i$, a finite set sealed by $\cl$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\then$ fixes every hop before any value exists.
$\mathit{select}:F(A+B)\to F(A\to B)\to F\,B$ fixes the possible effects,
so $\cl$ can seal both branches, but the selecting hop picks $\nxt_i$ by
$v_i$ and learns the branch taken; its law is deferred with Selective
itself.  Bind $m\gg\!\!=k$, $k:A\to M\,B$, chooses the
continuation, and so its hops, from a run-time value: either a hop
constructs $\nxt$ (violating L6) or $\cl$ seals $|A|$ continuations.  This
is the arrows-meticulous, monads-promiscuous separation of
\cite{Lindley2011}.
\end{proof}

Hence the name: a circuit is an onion Kleisli pipeline, a static
composite in $\Kl(M)$.  $M$ is the effect inside one hop; across hops
no bind is exposed, so the structure is not a monad.

\begin{lawL}{L8}{unlinkability}
On consecutive edges $\chi_i\ne\chi_{i+1}$ and $y_{i-1}\ne y_i$ (one layer
peeled, one AEZ layer removed per hop).  If any $y_{k,j}$ is modified to
$\tilde y\ne y_{k,j}$,
\[
\Pr\bigl[\Dec{\tau}{k_{c_k}}(\tilde y_{k,s})\ne\bot\bigr]\le2^{-8\tau}
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{AEZ}},
\]
and a rejected value yields nothing; no bit of a tag survives.  L8 is a
byte-level law; colluders still link by timing (Table~\ref{tab:leak}).
\end{lawL}

Under Assumption~\ref{as:prim} consecutive $y$ are independent uniform
strings to any hop lacking both keys.  A modification propagates through
every later wide-block decipherment, so, unlike counter-mode relay
cryptography~\cite{Degabriele2018}, a flipped bit is never observed in
place; this is Sphinx's wide-block payload~\cite{Danezis2009,Anderson1996}
with AEZ in place of LIONESS.

\begin{lawL}{L9}{replay}
With $V=150$\,s (\code{ONION\_FORWARD\_MAX\_VALIDITY\_MS}) and per-sender
capacity $B=4096$ over at most $64$ senders, hop $i$ admits a layer at
$\arr$ iff
\[
\arr<x\le\arr+V\ \wedge\ \nu\notin R_i\ \wedge\ |R_i^{\frm}|<B,
\]
then retains $\nu$ in $R_i$ until $\arr+V$.  A full $R_i^{\frm}$ rejects;
no tag leaves $R_i$ before its retention ends.  A sender beyond the 64th
takes the quota of the least recently active partition, whose tags stay in
$R_i$ until their retention ends; admission rejects while $|R_i|=64B$.  A SURB is a layer, hence
single use; L9 also bounds each AEZ key to one carry value
(Prop.~\ref{prop:onekey}).
\end{lawL}

\begin{proposition}[Replay bounds]\label{prop:replay}
(i) No admitted layer outlives its tag: $x\le\arr+V$.  (ii) $|R_i|\le64B$.
(iii) Hop $i$ admits at most $B$ layers per sender per $V$, i.e.\
$B/V\approx27$ cells\,s$^{-1}$ per neighbour.  (iv) A SURB is usable
only within $[x_\upsilon-V,x_\upsilon)$, so SURB lifetime is at most $V$.
(v) Hop $i$ admits at most $64B/V\approx1748$ cells\,s$^{-1}$ in total.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) is the admission window; (ii) the capacity guard; (iii) each admitted
tag occupies a slot for $V$; (iv) every relay of the reply path applies
(i) at the reply's arrival; (v) follows from (ii).
\end{proof}

(iii) bounds per-neighbour loop throughput, including SURB replies; $B$
is a tuning constant, not a law.  Every loop of one client enters $g$ from
$\cl$, so (iii) also caps a client's total forward rate at $B/V$ cells per
second and, by Inv.~\ref{inv:credit}, its total reply rate across all its
sessions at $B\,C_b/V$ bytes per second, about $180$\,KB\,s$^{-1}$ at
$b=16$\,KiB.  This client-level cap, not relay-to-relay traffic, is what
constrains $B$.

\begin{lawL}{L10}{wire}
Phase~1 preserves today's bytes (golden tests); Phase~2 replaces them with
a new pinned golden set in one cutover, without versioning.
\end{lawL}

\section{The Current Design}\label{sec:current}

\begin{proposition}[Backward linkability]\label{prop:current}
Today the exit seals its answer once,
$c=\mathrm{AEAD}_{\mathit{pk}_\cl}(\cdot)$
(\code{encrypt\_client\_payload\_at\_sequence} in
\code{circuit/crypto.rs}), and each relay re-emits it as
$\mathit{payload}=\mathit{frame.payload}$ (\code{advance\_backward} in
\code{circuit/reducer.rs}), rewriting only the edge circuit id and the
outer hop seal.  Every backward relay therefore sees the
same $c$: any two colluding relays, and the exit, link their positions by
equality, independently of route length.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Nested layers leak position]\label{prop:nested}
\code{build\_forward\_layers\_with\_ids} nests one envelope per hop, so
hop $i$ decrypts $\mathit{inner}_i$ with
$|\mathit{inner}_i|=\sum_{j>i}\ell_j+|\lambda_H|$, measured at $404$\,B per
downstream hop.  A loop extended after $i$ changes $|\mathit{inner}_i|$,
so $\view_i$ distinguishes it with probability $1$: L5$'$ fails.
\end{proposition}

Cell buckets pad the cell before decryption, not the layer; padding the
innermost layer to a constant total only moves the leak from $H-i$ to $i$.

blinded element), raw DID and key encodings ($\ell(2,64)\approx547$\,B),

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Tor~\cite{Dingledine2004} answers along the circuit it built, as Rings does
today; Degabriele and Stam~\cite{Degabriele2018} formalise the tagging that
its counter-mode relay cryptography admits.  Chaum's untraceable return
addresses~\cite{Chaum1981} and Mixminion's SURBs~\cite{Danezis2003} are the
source of D8; Sphinx~\cite{Danezis2009} gives the fixed-length header and a
LIONESS~\cite{Anderson1996} payload whose zero-block check L8 inherits.
Loopix~\cite{Piotrowska2017} routes traffic as loops for cover; here the
loop is the circuit.  AEZ~\cite{Hoang2015,AEZv5} supplies both the
length-preserving relay layer and robust AE in one primitive.  On the
semantic side, Moggi's Kleisli semantics~\cite{Moggi1991}, generic effects
and handlers~\cite{Plotkin2003,Plotkin2009}, and the
Arrow/Selective/Monad hierarchy~\cite{Hughes2000,Lindley2011,Mokhov2019}
fix which compositions a client can seal in advance.
