\centering\bfseries Abstract\\[0.5\baselineskip]%
\normalfont
DRanking is a reputation and admission protocol for open structured
peer-to-peer networks.  Its purpose is to convert locally observed peer
behavior into a globally agreed, verifiable service weight that can gate
admission to protocol roles, culminating in committee selection for Byzantine
fault tolerant coordination.  The protocol is designed around one negative
result and one restated assumption.  The negative result, due to Douceur, is
that no internal mechanism can bound the \emph{number} of adversarial
identities in an open network; identity creation is free.  The restated
assumption is therefore not ``$2/3$ of nodes are honest'' but ``$2/3$ of
accumulated, trust-weighted service is honest.''  Every component of DRanking
exists to make that weight expensive to forge: unforgeable signed service
receipts replace unsigned counters; a seed-anchored trust propagation with
conservation, attenuation, and revocation laws bounds the influence any
colluding cluster can extract; verifiable random sortition replaces oracle-based
sampling; a minimal proof-of-authority ledger records epoch commitments while
being trusted only for ordering, never for truth; and the ledger's state
transition is a circuit-friendly pure function folded by an incrementally
verifiable computation scheme, so that a browser node verifies the entire
history at constant cost.  Rank is soulbound and slashable; rewards are a
separate transferable asset.  We state the trust algebra as explicit laws and
invariants, prove a bound on Sybil influence, and give an economic transition
path from authority-anchored to stake-anchored operation.}}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}

An open structured peer-to-peer network admits any participant who can
generate a key pair.  This is a feature for reachability and a hazard for
every protocol layer that must allocate a scarce resource: storage quota,
relay bandwidth, committee membership, or a vote.  The network needs a notion
of \emph{standing} --- which participants have served it, and how much ---
that is robust against participants who manufacture identities, testimony, or
both.

The 2023 draft of this protocol proposed local behavioral scoring, global
aggregation by random sampling, and a median-guessing incentive game.  Three
years of implementation and analysis exposed a common root flaw in all three
components: each assumed that honesty could be counted \emph{per identity},
while identities cost nothing.  Statistical validation of sample placement
cannot detect an adversary who places free identities uniformly; a median
game rewards whichever coalition controls half the sample; unsigned local
counters aggregate into unverifiable hearsay.  The present rewrite keeps the
problem decomposition of the draft --- local evidence, global aggregation,
sampling, incentives, cold start, punishment --- and replaces the adversarial
assumption underneath every component.

\subsection{Design Position}

Douceur's Sybil result~\cite{Douceur2002} shows that without an external
authority or a resource cost, an open system cannot bound the fraction of
adversarial identities.  DRanking therefore does not attempt to count
honest \emph{nodes}.  It prices honest \emph{weight}:

\begin{assumption}[Honest weight]\label{asm:honest-weight}
Let $t \in [0,1]^{V}$ be the global trust vector defined in
Section~\ref{sec:trust}.  DRanking assumes that identities controlled by the
adversary hold strictly less than $1/3$ of total trust mass:
\[
\sum_{v \in \mathsf{Adv}} t_v \;<\; \tfrac{1}{3}\sum_{v \in V} t_v .
\]
No assumption is made about the number of adversarial identities, which is
unbounded.
\end{assumption}

Every mechanism in this paper is an instrument for making
Assumption~\ref{asm:honest-weight} \emph{purchasable at a known price}: the
protocol composes a one-time admission cost, a continuous service cost with
decay, an objective punishment channel, and (in the final phase) an external
economic anchor, and the security claim is the explicit cost function of
acquiring $1/3$ of trust mass.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Verifiable receipts} (Section~\ref{sec:receipts}): local
  measurement is upgraded from unsigned counters to counterparty-signed
  service receipts, making all aggregated evidence third-party checkable and
  reducing the forgery surface to collusive self-dealing.
\item \emph{A trust algebra} (Section~\ref{sec:trust}): seed-anchored
  propagation with conservation, attenuation, and revocation laws; a proved
  upper bound on the trust mass any Sybil region can hold; soulbound,
  slashable rank.
\item \emph{Oracle-free sampling} (Section~\ref{sec:sampling}): verifiable
  random sortition weighted by trust, replacing the draft's decentralized
  random oracle and its statistical validation, with an explicit account of
  why the latter cannot detect Sybil density.
\item \emph{A minimally trusted ledger} (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}): a small
  proof-of-authority BFT chain whose validators are trusted for ordering and
  availability only; all recorded content is self-certifying, and
  equivocation proofs are first-class slash objects.
\item \emph{A succinct chain} (Section~\ref{sec:ivc}): the ledger state
  transition is a pure, circuit-friendly function; the trust fixpoint is
  unrolled into one power-iteration step per epoch, so the whole chain folds
  under incrementally verifiable computation and a browser verifies it at
  constant cost.
\item \emph{A two-asset economy} (Section~\ref{sec:economics}): rank is
  soulbound; reward is transferable; we show why a payable rank destroys the
  Sybil cost anchor, and give a security-budget criterion for the
  authority-to-stake transition.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Model and Adversary}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Network substrate]
The substrate is a structured overlay in the Chord
family~\cite{Chord,ZaveChordCorrect}: identities are self-certifying
decentralized identifiers (DIDs) derived from public keys, placed on the
circular carrier $R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}$; links are authenticated
pairwise transports; every message is signed by its origin.  Establishing a
link is cheap but not free (signaling plus session setup); creating an
identity is free.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Epoch]
Protocol time is divided into epochs $k=0,1,2,\dots$ delimited by the ledger
of Section~\ref{sec:ledger}.  All aggregation, trust updates, sortition, and
reward issuance are per-epoch.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Adversary]\label{asm:adversary}
The adversary controls an unbounded set of identities; may generate
identifiers adaptively (grinding); may deviate arbitrarily from the protocol
(Byzantine); may collude across all its identities, including exchanging
valid signatures among them; and may participate honestly for arbitrarily
long before deviating (patience).  The adversary cannot forge signatures of
identities it does not control and cannot break the verifiable random
function of Section~\ref{sec:sampling}.
\end{assumption}

The patience clause matters: any reputation earned by behavior can be farmed
by an adversary willing to behave.  DRanking's response is not to prevent
farming but to price it (continuous cost, Section~\ref{sec:receipts}), decay
it (Law~\ref{law:revocation}), and make a single detected equivocation
forfeit it entirely (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}).

\section{Verifiable Service Receipts}\label{sec:receipts}

The draft aggregated locally maintained counters (connect/disconnect,
send/receive successes and failures).  Such counters are first-person
testimony: sound as \emph{local} link-selection evidence, but unverifiable
the moment they are reported to a third party.  A global ranking built on
them aggregates hearsay.  DRanking makes the unit of evidence a signed
receipt.

\begin{definition}[Service receipt]\label{def:receipt}
A receipt is a tuple
\[
\rho = \langle \mathsf{srv},\, a,\, b,\, k,\, n,\, \sigma_b \rangle
\]
in which $\mathsf{srv}$ names a service kind (relay, storage challenge,
liveness probe), $a$ is the serving DID, $b$ the served DID, $k$ the epoch,
$n$ a nonce unique per $(a,b,k)$ pair scope, and $\sigma_b$ the signature of
$b$ over the preceding fields.  A receipt is \emph{valid} if $\sigma_b$
verifies and $(b,k,n)$ has not been seen before.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Forgery surface]\label{prop:forgery}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:adversary}, an adversary cannot fabricate a valid
receipt naming an honest $b$ as signer.  The only manufacturable receipts
are those signed within the adversary's own identity set (self-dealing).
\end{proposition}

Self-dealing is not prevented at this layer; it is neutralized at the trust
layer, where a receipt's contribution is weighted by the \emph{signer's}
trust (Section~\ref{sec:trust}).  A cluster of zero-trust identities signing
receipts to one another injects approximately zero weight.

\begin{definition}[Receipt graph]
The evidence of epoch $k$ is a weighted directed multigraph aggregated into
the matrix $W_k \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{V\times V}$, where $W_k[b,a]$ is the
service value receipted by signer $b$ to server $a$ during epoch $k$, under a
per-signer, per-epoch issuance cap $W_k[b,\cdot]\mathbf{1} \le w_{\max}$.
\end{definition}

The issuance cap makes receipt-signing itself a budgeted act: a signer
cannot certify more service than the protocol deems one identity able to
consume in one epoch.  This is the first of several conservation-shaped
constraints; the trust layer adds the second.

Receipts price honest weight continuously: to hold weight, an identity must
keep producing service that real counterparties keep countersigning, which
costs bandwidth and uptime for as long as the weight is to be held.  The
protocol deliberately certifies \emph{performed service} rather than proving
capacity with synthetic load; manufactured traffic between colluding
identities would burn network capacity and still be discounted to zero by
signer-trust weighting.

\section{The Trust Algebra}\label{sec:trust}

Global standing is a vector $t_k \in [0,1]^{V}$ computed from receipt
graphs and a seed vector.  We state the algebra as laws, because each law
closes a specific attack.

\begin{definition}[Seed vector]\label{def:seed}
A seed vector $s \in [0,1]^{V}$, $\sum_v s_v = 1$, assigns initial trust to
a small authority set $S$ (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}).  Seeds are the
protocol's explicit trust root: DRanking does not derive trust from nothing,
it declares where trust originates and then constrains how it flows.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Conservation]\label{law:conservation}
Trust is redistributed, never created.  The propagation matrix
$C_k$ is the row-substochastic normalization of $W_k$:
$C_k[b,a] = W_k[b,a] / \max(w_{\max}, W_k[b,\cdot]\mathbf{1})$.
A signer's total outflow of endorsement can never exceed its own unit row,
and endorsement given is budget spent.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Attenuation]\label{law:attenuation}
Trust decays along every hop by a damping factor $\alpha \in (0,1)$: a chain
of endorsements of length $\ell$ carries at most $\alpha^{\ell}$ of its
source weight.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Revocation and decay]\label{law:revocation}
Trust is a flow, not a stock: it must be re-earned every epoch because the
update law re-derives $t_{k+1}$ from current receipts; a slashed identity
(Section~\ref{sec:ledger}) is removed from the carrier of $t$ and its held
mass returns to the seed term; seeds may withdraw endorsement.
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Trust update]\label{def:update}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
t_{k+1} \;=\; \alpha\, C_k^{\mathsf{T}} t_k \;+\; (1-\alpha)\, s .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:update} is one step of power iteration toward the
personalized-PageRank/EigenTrust fixpoint~\cite{PageRank,EigenTrust}
$t^{*} = (1-\alpha)(I - \alpha C^{\mathsf{T}})^{-1} s$.  DRanking
deliberately does \emph{not} compute the fixpoint within an epoch: one step
per epoch is executed, so the fixpoint is approached along chain time.  This
choice is semantic as well as computational --- standing accrues over
history, not instantaneously --- and it is what makes the transition function
circuit-friendly in Section~\ref{sec:ivc}.

\begin{invariant}[Mass]\label{inv:mass}
If $\sum_v t_0(v) = 1$ then for all $k$,
$\sum_v t_k(v) \le 1$, with equality when every row of $C_k$ is fully
spent.  Trust mass never inflates.
\end{invariant}

\begin{proposition}[Sybil influence bound]\label{prop:sybil-bound}
Partition $V$ into an honest region $H$ (including all seeds) and an
adversarial region $A$.  Let the \emph{attack inflow} at epoch $k$ be
$g_k = \sum_{b \in H, a \in A} C_k[b,a]\, t_k(b)$, the trust-weighted
endorsement flowing from honest signers to adversarial servers, and let
$g = \sup_k g_k$.  Then for all $k$,
\[
T_k(A) \;=\; \sum_{v\in A} t_k(v) \;\le\; \frac{\alpha\, g}{1-\alpha} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since no seed lies in $A$, the seed term contributes nothing to $T_{k+1}(A)$.
Splitting the propagation term by origin region,
\[
T_{k+1}(A) \;\le\; \alpha\big( g_k + T_k(A) \big),
\]
because mass arriving in $A$ from $A$ itself is at most $\alpha\,T_k(A)$ by
Law~\ref{law:conservation} (rows are substochastic, so internal circulation
cannot amplify) and mass arriving from $H$ is exactly $\alpha\,g_k$.
Iterating from $T_0(A)=0$ gives a geometric series bounded by
$\alpha g \sum_{i\ge0} \alpha^{i} = \alpha g/(1-\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

The bound has the right shape for engineering: adversarial standing is
linear in \emph{honest endorsement actually granted to the adversary} ($g$),
and independent of how many identities the adversary creates.  Defense
concentrates on making $g$ small and expensive: endorsement costs budget
(Law~\ref{law:conservation}), honest signers only countersign service they
received, and detected misbehavior claws standing back
(Law~\ref{law:revocation}).

\begin{definition}[Soulbound rank]\label{def:soulbound}
The rank of $v$ at epoch $k$ is $t_k(v)$.  Rank is bound to the DID that
earned it: it cannot be transferred, delegated, or sold; it decays under
Law~\ref{law:revocation}; and it is forfeited entirely upon an accepted
misbehavior proof.  Section~\ref{sec:economics} shows that relaxing
non-transferability re-opens the Sybil market the rest of the protocol
closes.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Cold Start and Dilution}

At genesis, $t_0 = s$: all standing is seed standing.  As the network serves
and receipts accumulate, the propagation term grows relative to the seed
term.  The protocol commits, in advance, to a \emph{dilution schedule}: the
effective seed weight $(1-\alpha)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:update} is a
non-increasing function of cumulative receipted service, so authority
influence declines on a published curve rather than by promise.  The same
schedule governs the consensus transition in Section~\ref{sec:economics}.

\section{Sampling by Verifiable Sortition}\label{sec:sampling}

Global aggregation requires selecting, per epoch, which nodes' local receipt
sets are collected and which nodes audit the epoch commitment.  The draft
selected by lookup toward targets derived from a ``decentralized random
oracle,'' then validated the sample with entropy and Kolmogorov--Smirnov
tests.  Both halves fail, for instructive reasons.

\subsection{Why Statistical Validation Cannot Work}

The draft's tests check that sampled positions are \emph{uniformly placed}.
Uniformity of placement is orthogonal to honesty of identity: an adversary
who mints free identifiers uniformly over $R$ occupies any target fraction
of the identifier space while passing every placement test exactly as an
honest population would.  Statistical tests detect \emph{biased sampling};
the threat is \emph{cheap identity}.  No test on positions can see the
difference, so validation must instead be cryptographic (receipts,
signatures, proofs) and economic (trust weighting).  Separately, a global
random seed is a bootstrap circularity: a decentralized randomness beacon
already requires the committee that sampling is meant to select.

\subsection{Sortition}

DRanking removes the global oracle.  Each node holds a verifiable random
function (VRF)~\cite{VRF} key bound to its DID.  For epoch $k$ with
finalized ledger beacon $B_k$ (the hash of the finalized epoch-$k$ header,
unpredictable before finality under Assumption~\ref{asm:adversary}):

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Trust-weighted sortition (per node $v$, epoch $k$)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $(y, \pi) \gets \mathsf{VRF}_{\mathsf{sk}_v}(B_k \,\|\, \mathsf{role})$
\State \textbf{if} $y / 2^{|y|} < \tau \cdot t_k(v)$ \textbf{then}
       $v$ holds $\mathsf{role}$ for epoch $k$, with proof $\pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Selection probability is proportional to rank, so
Assumption~\ref{asm:honest-weight} translates directly into an
honest-majority statement about every sampled set: sortition is the functor
from trust mass to committee seats.  Grinding is closed at both inputs: the
VRF key is fixed before $B_k$ exists, and $B_k$ is fixed by finality before
the adversary can steer it.  Self-selection with public proofs also removes
the draft's median game entirely --- there is no reported quantity for a
coalition to steer, hence no game whose equilibrium must be argued.

\section{The Admission Ledger}\label{sec:ledger}

Aggregated evidence needs a single ordered record: without one, a node can
show different evidence to different observers, and no deterministic global
rank exists.  DRanking uses a deliberately small ledger.

\subsection{Minimal Trust Design}

\begin{definition}[Authority ledger]
A BFT proof-of-authority chain operated by the seed set $S$ with instant
finality (two-thirds quorum signatures on each block).  Per epoch, the chain
records: the epoch commitment (a Merkle root of $W_k$), the resulting trust
vector commitment (root of $t_{k+1}$), validator-set changes, slash records,
and reward issuance.  Raw receipt data remains in the overlay's storage
layer; the chain holds commitments.
\end{definition}

\begin{obligation}[Validators order, content certifies]\label{obl:minimal-trust}
Every recorded object must be independently verifiable from signatures and
commitments alone: receipts carry their signers' signatures, trust vectors
are recomputable from committed inputs by Equation~\eqref{eq:update}, and
slashes carry proofs.  A colluding validator quorum can censor or reorder;
it must never be able to fabricate.  Any proposed ledger content that a
validator quorum could forge is a design defect at this layer.
\end{obligation}

Censorship, the residual validator power, is bounded by a force-inclusion
rule: a node holding a validator-acknowledged submission that does not
appear on chain within a timeout publishes the acknowledgment as a
misbehavior proof, slashing the withholding quorum's members.

\subsection{Equivocation as the Objective Signal}

All positive evidence (receipts) is verifiable but collusive; the only
fully objective reputation events are negative:

\begin{definition}[Equivocation proof]
Two messages, both validly signed by the same DID, asserting contradictory
protocol statements for the same slot (two epoch commitments for the same
epoch, two votes for one height, two receipts with one nonce).  The pair is
self-certifying: verifying it requires no honesty assumption at all.
\end{definition}

Equivocation proofs are first-class ledger objects: anyone may submit one;
inclusion slashes the offender's rank to zero (Law~\ref{law:revocation})
and, in the staked phase, its bond.  This is what defeats the patient
adversary of Assumption~\ref{asm:adversary}: months of farmed standing are
forfeited by one detected contradiction, so the expected value of farming
falls with the certainty of eventual detection.

\section{Succinct State by Folding}\label{sec:ivc}

The substrate's defining deployment target is the browser: a wasm node with
neither the storage to replay history nor a reason to trust a validator
signature it cannot audit.  DRanking therefore requires the ledger to be
\emph{succinctly verifiable}: a constant-size proof attests the validity of
the entire chain state.

\subsection{The Step Function}

\begin{definition}[Epoch transition]\label{def:step}
The ledger state is
$\Sigma_k = \langle \mathsf{root}(t_k), \mathsf{root}(V_k),
\mathsf{root}(\mathsf{slash}_k), \mathsf{root}(\mathsf{bal}_k) \rangle$.
The transition
$F(\Sigma_k, \mathsf{root}(W_k)) = \Sigma_{k+1}$
is a pure function: one application of Equation~\eqref{eq:update}, slash
application, validator-set update, and reward issuance, over
Merkle-committed inputs.
\end{definition}

Because Equation~\eqref{eq:update} is a single sparse matrix--vector product
with scalar damping --- additions and multiplications over committed data ---
$F$ is circuit-friendly \emph{by construction}.  This is the payoff of
unrolling the fixpoint: the draft's alternative (an eigenvector solve per
epoch) is hostile to arithmetization; one power-iteration step is trivial
under it.  The design rule generalizes:

\begin{obligation}[Circuit discipline]
Every extension of the ledger state machine must keep $F$ pure,
deterministic, and expressible in cheap arithmetic over committed inputs.
The ledger is an admission ledger, not a general contract platform; each
added semantic multiplies proving cost and freezes harder under
Obligation~\ref{obl:upgrade}.
\end{obligation}

\subsection{Folding}

$F$ is folded by an incrementally verifiable computation scheme of the Nova
family~\cite{Nova}: each epoch folds its step instance into a running
accumulator; a compressed proof $\pi_k$ attests
``$\Sigma_k$ is reachable from $\Sigma_0$ by $k$ valid applications of
$F$.''  A fresh browser node verifies $\pi_k$ plus the finality quorum
signature on $\Sigma_k$ --- constant work, after which it trusts mathematics
for validity and the quorum only for canonicity.

Two structural facts make the combination clean.  First, IVC folds a
\emph{linear} chain of steps; BFT instant finality guarantees the chain
never reorganizes, so no re-folding branch logic exists ---
probabilistic-finality chains cannot say this.  Second, proving is
asynchronous: the chain advances by replay verification among validators
(cheap, since $F$ is pure), while the prover compresses history behind the
head; succinctness is a property of verification, and lags nothing but
itself.

\begin{obligation}[Upgrade versioning]\label{obl:upgrade}
Under IVC the circuit is the constitution: any change to $F$ changes the
verifier key.  The protocol carries an explicit version register in
$\Sigma$; upgrades bind old and new verifier keys at a committed epoch
boundary.  This mechanism ships at genesis, not as a retrofit.
\end{obligation}

What folding does \emph{not} provide: canonicity (consensus chooses the
chain), data availability (the overlay stores receipt data), or privacy of
the receipt graph.  The granularity of what $\mathsf{root}(W_k)$ exposes ---
full graph, per-node aggregates, or blinded commitments --- is an open
design decision with surveillance consequences, and zero-knowledge openings
of individual standing are its natural extension.

\section{Incentives and Economics}\label{sec:economics}

\subsection{Two Assets, Not One}

The draft made rankings ``payable.''  This is the single most consequential
error in the 2023 design:

\begin{proposition}[Payable rank defeats cost anchoring]\label{prop:soulbound}
If rank is transferable at market price $p$, the adversary's cost of
acquiring trust mass $m$ is $\min(\mathsf{farm}(m),\, p\cdot m)$, and honest
exiting nodes are rational sellers; the market clears at the price of the
cheapest accumulated history, decoupling standing from behavior.
Non-transferability (Definition~\ref{def:soulbound}) removes the second
argument of the $\min$, restoring $\mathsf{farm}(m)$ --- service, time, and
slash risk --- as the only acquisition path.
\end{proposition}

DRanking therefore carries two assets: \emph{rank}, soulbound, earned by
receipted service, governing admission and sortition weight; and
\emph{reward}, a transferable token issued per epoch to nodes in proportion
to receipted, trust-weighted service.  Reward is the distribution mechanism:
by the time the network transitions to stake, token holders are
approximately its service providers, because service was the only faucet.

\subsection{Value and Sinks}

A reward token distributed against service is worthless without a sink.
The protocol's fee loop makes the token the payment instrument for the
network's scarce services: storage persistence beyond baseline replication,
relay quality-of-service, and namespace registration.  Fees are burned or
recycled into the epoch reward pool.  The security consequence is direct:
the stake anchor of the next subsection is only as strong as the demand
routed through these sinks, so the fee loop is a security parameter, not a
business afterthought.

\subsection{The Authority-to-Stake Transition}

The ledger launches as proof-of-authority (Section~\ref{sec:ledger})
because quorum arithmetic requires a closed set and genesis has no other
Sybil anchor.  The transition to proof-of-stake replaces the fiat validator
set with a sortition over bonded token weight.  Two rules govern it:

\begin{obligation}[Security-budget crossover]
The switch is conditioned, not scheduled: stake-based validation activates
only when slashable bonded value exceeds a published multiple of the
estimated attack payoff.  Before the crossover, PoA finality signatures
remain a hybrid overlay on PoS block production, weighted down along the
same dilution schedule as the seed vector (Section~\ref{sec:trust}).
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[One abstraction boundary]
Consensus is parameterized over an abstract validator-set function
$\mathsf{vset}: \Sigma_k \to 2^{V}$; PoA supplies a constant, PoS supplies
a bonded-sortition function.  The switch changes one pure function, not the
engine.
\end{obligation}

Proof-of-work alternatives were evaluated and rejected: hash-rental markets
price a majority attack on a young chain at hours of rented compute; and
``proof of traffic'' fails twice --- synthetic load between colluding
identities is indistinguishable from service (so it buys no Sybil
resistance) while consuming the very bandwidth the network exists to
provide.  Receipted real service with trust weighting is the version of
proof-of-traffic that keeps the evidence and discards the waste.

Two residuals deserve emphasis.  Out-of-band key sales (selling the DID's
private key, rank attached) cannot be prevented cryptographically; the
mitigation is economic --- a sold identity carries its slash exposure, and
a buyer inherits a history it did not control.  The pre-dilution window is
the honest name for the PoA phase: until the dilution schedule and the
security-budget crossover retire them, the seeds are a threshold trust
root, and the paper claims exactly that, no more.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

DRanking's local layer descends from behavior scoring in file-sharing
networks --- eDonkey's ranking queues~\cite{eMule} and BitTorrent's
choking~\cite{Cohen03} --- which the 2023 draft adopted directly.
EigenTrust~\cite{EigenTrust} and personalized PageRank~\cite{PageRank}
supply the propagation fixpoint; DRanking's contribution is unrolling it
along chain epochs and binding it to signed evidence.  The social-graph
Sybil defenses SybilGuard, SybilLimit, and
SybilRank~\cite{SybilGuard,SybilLimit,SybilRank} established the
attack-edge bound pattern that Proposition~\ref{prop:sybil-bound} instantiates
over a service graph.  Byzantine-resilient peer sampling
(Brahms~\cite{Brahms}) addresses the sampling-under-adversary problem that
the draft's statistical tests could not; DRanking sidesteps it with
Algorand-style VRF sortition~\cite{Algorand,VRF} weighted by trust instead
of stake.  S/Kademlia~\cite{SKademlia} prices identifier creation with
puzzles, a complementary one-time cost.  The ledger design follows
PBFT-lineage instant finality~\cite{PBFT}; the succinct chain follows
Mina~\cite{Mina}, realized with Nova-family folding~\cite{Nova}.  The
impossibility frame is Douceur's~\cite{Douceur2002}; the Byzantine quorum
frame is Lamport, Shostak, and Pease's~\cite{LSP82}; the economic
transition draws on Nakamoto's observation that external cost, not internal
testimony, anchors open membership~\cite{Nakamoto}.

\section{Conclusion}

The 2023 draft decomposed the reputation problem correctly and secured each
component against the wrong adversary --- one who lies, rather than one who
is legion.  This rewrite keeps the decomposition and re-anchors every
component in cost: receipts make testimony expensive to forge, the trust
algebra makes influence expensive to accumulate and instant to lose,
sortition makes selection impossible to steer, the ledger makes history
impossible to equivocate, folding makes verification cheap enough for a
browser, and the two-asset economy keeps standing off the market.  The
protocol's honest claim is a priced assumption: the system is safe while
one third of trust mass costs more than attacking it pays.  Every mechanism
above is a term in that price.
