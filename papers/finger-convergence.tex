\normalfont
Chord maintains $m$ finger entries per node by refreshing one entry per
stabilization round, so a $160$-bit table takes of the order of $160$
rounds to converge and any entry learned from a join is indistinguishable
from a verified one.  This paper specifies the finger maintenance of Rings, a
Chord ring whose members are mostly browsers reachable only through a
WebRTC handshake that may take minutes or fail.  The design separates
\emph{hints} (entries inferred from admission and churn) from
\emph{verified ranges} (entries proved by an authenticated lookup), and
applies Chord's own batching lemma: one lookup for slot $i$ that returns a
successor at clockwise distance $d$ proves every slot $j$ with $2^j\le d$.
Around that lemma it builds a pure state machine with five obligations:
per-slot evidence epochs that make a late result unable to overwrite a
newer hint; a single algebraic attempt owner
$\Idle\mid\AwR\mid\AwA$ correlated by a fresh UUID; a
cyclic selection cursor that serves unverified slots round-robin, so one
slot whose successor never admits costs one attempt per rotation instead of
starving the table; a deterministic retry floor with full jitter; and
single-use, RAII-released claims on stabilization and successor-list
reports with a candidate budget of $K{+}1$ and $K$ connections per report.
We state the state relation, prove the range and starvation-freedom
propositions, bound the per-node message cost, and list the obligations
discharged by the shipped model checker.}}

\section{Introduction}

Chord~\cite{Stoica2001} routes in $O(\log N)$ hops because every node keeps
a finger table: entry $i$ is the successor of $n+2^i$.  The 2003 journal
revision~\cite{Stoica2003} maintains that table with a randomized
\texttt{fix\_fingers} that refreshes one entry per period; convergence of an
$m$-bit table therefore needs of the order of $m$ periods, and the paper
treats entries as either present or absent, never as \emph{unproved}.

Rings runs Chord over a membership that is unusual in two ways.  Most nodes
are browsers: a routing entry is usable only after a WebRTC handshake that
takes seconds to minutes and frequently fails outright, and a browser tab
suspends and resumes its maintenance loop without notice.  And the ring is
sparse in identifier space: $N\ll 2^{160}$, so a $160$-slot table has only
$O(\log N)$ \emph{distinct} successors, most of them the immediate successor.
Refreshing one slot per period spends $160$ rounds to learn $\log N$ facts.

The maintenance shipped before 0.26.0 had three defects that this design
removes.  It advanced one slot per round.  It wrote the peer learned from a
join into every slot that peer could own and could never tell such a
\emph{hint} from a proved entry, so a hint written from a stale view survived
until its slot happened to be refreshed.  And it matched lookup results to
slots by slot index alone, so a result from before a topology change, or
from before a process restart, could overwrite newer state.

The design here is a pure state machine over the finger table with an
explicit effect boundary.  Its content is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item the \emph{range lemma} (Section~\ref{sec:range}), which is Chord's own
  \texttt{init\_finger\_table} check~\cite[\S4.4]{Stoica2001} restated as a
  proof rule: one lookup proves a consecutive range of slots, and the
  current successor's stabilization report proves the local range without
  any lookup;
\item \emph{evidence epochs} (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}) that version every
  hint change and make a result issued under an older epoch unable to
  overwrite it;
\item a single \emph{attempt owner} (Section~\ref{sec:attempt}) with
  algebraic phases, a fresh UUID per lookup, a report deadline, and an
  admission lease that outlives the transport handshake it backs;
\item \emph{cyclic selection} and a deterministic \emph{retry floor}
  (Section~\ref{sec:selection}) that are starvation-free by construction;
\item single-use, RAII-released \emph{report claims} for stabilization and
  successor-list synchronization with a bounded connection budget
  (Section~\ref{sec:claims}); and
\item a \emph{scheduler} (Section~\ref{sec:scheduler}) that adds jitter and
  survives browser suspension without catch-up bursts.
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:spec} collects the state relation as invariants,
Section~\ref{sec:cost} bounds the cost, Section~\ref{sec:security} treats
the adversary, and Section~\ref{sec:verification} lists what the shipped
tests and model checker discharge.

We claim no new routing theory.  The lemma is Chord's; Zave's
work~\cite{Zave2015,Zave2017} settles that ring correctness rests on the
successor structure alone and fingers are a replaceable optimization, which
is exactly the licence this design takes to treat every finger entry as
revocable evidence.  What is new is the evidence, ownership, selection, and
claim discipline around the lemma, and the fact that each of them is stated
as a law over a pure transition that the implementation executes verbatim.

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Ring]\label{def:ring}
Identifiers are $\mathbb{Z}/2^{m}$ with $m=160$.  For $a,b$ the clockwise
distance is $\dist(a,b)=(b-a)\bmod 2^{m}$.  A membership view $V$ is a
finite set of identifiers; $\succf_V(x)$ is the member $s\in V$ minimizing
$\dist(x,s)$, and $\pred_V(x)$ the member maximizing it.  Node $n$'s
successor list is the $K$ members nearest clockwise ($K=3$ by default);
its \emph{head} $\head(n)$ is the first entry other than $n$, or $\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finger slot]\label{def:slot}
Slot $i\in[0,L)$ of node $n$, with $L\le m$ the configured width, has
target $t_i=n+2^{i}$ and true value $\succf_V(t_i)$.  The table is
\emph{sparse and no-wrap}: it stores $\hint_i\in V\cup\{\bot\}$ where $\bot$
means ``unknown or $n$ itself''.  A value equal to $n$ is stored as $\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hint join]\label{def:join}
Learning a peer $p\ne n$ with $d=\dist(n,p)$ refines the table
slot-wise: for every $i$ with $2^{i}\le d$, $\hint_i\gets p$ if
$\hint_i=\bot$ or $\dist(n,\hint_i)>d$; other slots are unchanged.  Removing
$p$ clears every slot holding it and fills the gap from the nearest
surviving hint above.  Neither operation produces proof.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Authenticated reports]\label{as:auth}
Every lookup report and topology report is signed by its origin; the
transport rejects a report whose origin does not match the peer the query
was addressed to.  A report is therefore attributable but not necessarily
\emph{solicited}: solicitation is what the tokens of
Sections~\ref{sec:attempt} and~\ref{sec:claims} establish.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Clocks]\label{as:clock}
Each node has a process-monotonic clock; all deadlines in the finger state
machine are milliseconds elapsed from the node's construction.  The
transport's handshake generations are timed on wall-clock epoch
milliseconds.  The two are never compared directly
(Section~\ref{sec:attempt}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Effect boundary]\label{as:effects}
The state machine is a function
$\mathrm{step}:(S,\mathrm{Event})\to(S,\mathrm{Actions})$.  Clock readings
and fresh UUIDs~\cite{RFC9562} enter as event fields; network sends,
locks, and randomness happen only in the interpreter of the returned
actions.  Every law below is a property of $\mathrm{step}$ alone.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Range Lemma}\label{sec:range}

\begin{lemma}[Chord range lemma~\cite{Stoica2001}]\label{lem:range}
Let $s=\succf_V(t_i)$ and $d=\dist(n,s)$.  Then for every $j\ge i$ with
$2^{j}\le d$, $\succf_V(t_j)=s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By definition of $\succf_V$, the half-open arc $[t_i,s)$ contains no member
of $V$.  For $j\ge i$ with $2^{j}\le d$ we have
$\dist(n,t_i)=2^{i}\le 2^{j}=\dist(n,t_j)\le d=\dist(n,s)$, so
$t_j\in[t_i,s]$; the first member at or after $t_j$ is therefore $s$.
\end{proof}

This is the check Chord's \texttt{init\_finger\_table} performs
(``$\mathit{finger}[i].\mathit{node}\ge\mathit{finger}[i{+}1].\mathit{start}$'',
\cite[Fig.~6]{Stoica2001}) and the source of its $O(\log N)$
expected-lookup bound for table construction; the journal
revision~\cite{Stoica2003} dropped it in favour of random refresh.  We use
it as a proof rule.

\begin{definition}[Range proof]\label{def:proof}
A lookup for slot $i$ answered by $s$ yields the proof
$\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e,s,\mathrm{end})$ where $\mathrm{id}$ is the lookup's
UUID, $e$ the evidence epoch at issue time (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}), and
\[
\mathrm{end}=
\begin{cases}
L-1 & s=n,\\
\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,s)\rfloor) & 2^{i}\le\dist(n,s),\\
\text{rejected as }\mathsf{Invalid} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
The proof covers slots $i..\mathrm{end}$.  The case $s=n$ means the arc
from $t_i$ wrapped back to $n$ without meeting a member, so $n$ owns every
remaining target.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Lookups per pass]\label{cor:lookups}
A full pass over a table whose true values partition the slots into $R$
maximal runs of equal successor needs at most $R$ routed lookups, and
$R\le\min(L,|V|)$; under Chord's uniform-identifier analysis
$\mathbb{E}[R]=O(\log N)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Local range]\label{lem:local}
Let $h=\head(n)$ and let $n$ hold a stabilization report from $h$, sent in
answer to $n$'s query and stating $\pred(h)=n$.  Then $h=\succf_V(n+1)$
and every slot $j$ with $2^{j}\le\dist(n,h)$ has value $h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\pred(h)=n$ says no member lies in $(n,h)$, so $h$ is the successor of
every target in $(n,h]$, and $t_j\in(n,h]$ iff $2^{j}\le\dist(n,h)$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:local} is the reason the local range never sends a lookup.
A routed lookup for a target in $(n,h]$ would be forwarded by $h$ around
the whole ring back to $n$ (Chord's \texttt{find\_successor} has no
``you are responsible'' shortcut for the querier), costing $2\,O(\log N)$
hops per period for a fact the stabilization exchange already carries.
The proof requires the report to echo the current stabilization token and
$h$ to be both the queried head and the head after the report is applied;
a report from a superseded head proves nothing.

\section{Evidence Epochs}\label{sec:evidence}

\begin{definition}[Evidence]\label{def:evidence}
The evidence state is $(e,(\chg_j,\ver_j)_{j<L})$: a global epoch
$e\in\mathbb{N}$ and per slot the epoch $\chg_j$ at which $\hint_j$ last
changed and a bit $\ver_j$.  A fresh table has $e=0$, $\chg_j=0$,
$\ver_j=\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Hint change]\label{law:change}
When a topology transition changes the hint vector from $H$ to $H'$ and
$C=\{j: H_j\ne H'_j\}\ne\emptyset$, then $e\gets e+1$ and for $j\in C$,
$(\chg_j,\ver_j)\gets(e,\bot)$.  If $e+1$ overflows, every $\ver_j\gets\bot$
and the active attempt is retired instead.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Admission]\label{law:admit}
A proof $\pi$ issued at epoch $e_\pi$ may write slot $j\in[i,\mathrm{end}]$
iff $\chg_j\le e_\pi$.  It is \emph{accepted} iff at least one slot of its
range admits it; an accepted proof writes $\hint_j\gets s$ (or $\bot$ if
$s=n$) and $\ver_j\gets\top$ for exactly the admitting slots, and skips the
rest without rolling them back.
\end{law}

The acceptance predicate and the commit loop are the same per-slot
comparison, so acceptance implies at least one committed slot; there is no
``accepted but nothing written'' outcome.

\begin{law}[Discontinuity]\label{law:discontinuity}
Losing the last successor invalidates all evidence: $e\gets e+1$, every
slot unverified, the attempt retired.  Isolation is not proof that a range
is empty; the ranges stay unverified but \emph{dormant} until a successor
is admitted again.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Local confirmation]\label{law:confirm}
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:local} sets $\ver_j\gets\top$ for
$j\le\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h)\rfloor$ without a lookup; if the active
attempt's slot lies in that range it is superseded as progress, not
failure.
\end{law}

\section{Attempt Ownership}\label{sec:attempt}

\begin{definition}[Attempt]\label{def:attempt}
At most one attempt exists, in one of three phases:
\[
\Idle\mid\AwR(\rho,e_\rho,\tau_r)\mid\AwA(\pi,\tau_a),
\]
where $\rho=(i,\mathrm{id})$ is the request (slot and fresh UUID),
$e_\rho$ the epoch at issue, $\tau_r$ the report deadline, $\pi$ a validated
proof, and $\tau_a$ the admission lease deadline.  Two owners are not
representable.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Consumption]\label{law:consume}
A report $(\rho',s')$ may consume the attempt iff the attempt is
$\AwR(\rho,\cdot,\cdot)$ with $\rho'=\rho$, or $\AwA(\pi,\cdot)$ with
$\rho'=\pi.\rho$ and $s'=\pi.s$.  Any other report is \emph{stale}: it
changes no state and records no failure.
\end{law}

The second clause is what keeps a duplicate report that carries the current
UUID but a different successor from evicting a proof already retained for
admission.

\begin{law}[Deadlines]\label{law:deadlines}
$\tau_r=\mathrm{issued}+T_r$ with $T_r=10\,\mathrm{s}$.
$\tau_a=\mathrm{deferred}+T_a$ with $T_a=210\,\mathrm{s}$.  The transport's
handshake generation expires after $T_h=180\,\mathrm{s}$ of wall-clock time
from reservation, and $T_a>T_h$.
\end{law}

The lease is a backstop, not the handshake's clock.  A proof is
\emph{deferred} (moved to $\AwA$) the moment a timely report names a peer
that is not yet connected, before the asynchronous handshake is awaited, so
a handler that is cancelled between the two cannot strand it.  Once the
handshake generation exists, the proof is attached to that generation and
is released by the generation's own admission or expiry.  The lease
therefore only has to \emph{outlast} the generation; because the lease
starts at report arrival on the monotonic clock and the generation at
reservation on the wall clock, $T_a=T_h+30\,\mathrm{s}$ leaves the
reservation gap as margin.  A lease that could expire first would charge a
failure and discard a proof whose handshake later succeeds; the converse
hazard, committing a proof the state machine has released, is excluded by
Law~\ref{law:consume} since a released proof owns nothing.

\begin{law}[Outcomes]\label{law:outcomes}
A consuming report is validated in the order token, deadline, geometry
(Definition~\ref{def:proof}), epoch (Law~\ref{law:admit}).  The outcomes and
their effect on the attempt and on the failure streak $k$
(Section~\ref{sec:selection}) are:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
outcome & attempt & $k$ \\
\midrule
applied (connected peer) & $\Idle$ & $0$ \\
deferred (peer not connected) & $\AwA$ & same \\
retired (active, unroutable) & $\Idle$ & $k{+}1$ \\
cancelled (send/handshake fail) & $\Idle$ & $k{+}1$ \\
expired ($\tau_r$ or $\tau_a$) & $\Idle$ & $k{+}1$ \\
$\mathsf{Invalid}$/$\mathsf{Expired}$ report & $\Idle$ & $k{+}1$ \\
$\mathsf{Stale}$ report & same & same \\
hint change at slot $i$ & $\Idle$ & same \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{law}

Topology churn is not a network failure: a hint change at the attempt's
lower slot destroys the premise of its range proof and retires the owner,
but does not raise the retry floor, following the principle in
Bamboo~\cite{Rhea2004} that backing off on new evidence feeds churn back
into maintenance latency.

\section{Selection and Retry}\label{sec:selection}

\begin{definition}[Routable region]\label{def:routable}
With $h=\head(n)$ the first routable slot is
$r=\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h)\rfloor+1$ (or $0$ if $h=\bot$).  Slots below
$r$ are the local range of Lemma~\ref{lem:local} and are never selected
for a lookup.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cursor]\label{def:cursor}
The state carries a cursor $c\in\mathbb{N}$, initially $0$.  Selection
picks
\[
\mathrm{sel}(c)=
\begin{cases}
\min A & A\ne\emptyset,\\
\min B & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
with $A=\{j\ge\max(c,r):\neg\ver_j\}$ and $B=\{j\in[r,\max(c,r)):\neg\ver_j\}$,
i.e. the first unverified routable slot cyclically from $c$.  After an
applied proof with range end $\mathrm{end}$, or a retirement of a validated
proof, $c\gets\mathrm{end}+1$.  After a failure at slot $i$ without a
proof (cancel, report timeout, invalid or expired report),
$c\gets\mathrm{run}(i)+1$ where $\mathrm{run}(i)$ is the last index of the
maximal run of hints equal to $\hint_i$ starting at $i$.
\end{definition}

The run skip encodes that slots sharing an inferred hint expect the same
successor: retrying the next slot of the run would repeat the failure it
just recorded.

\begin{proposition}[Starvation freedom]\label{prop:starve}
Let $U$ be the set of routable slots that remain unverified throughout a
sequence of attempts.  Between two selections of the same slot $u\in U$,
every other slot of $U$ is selected at least once.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each attempt at slot $j$ moves $c$ strictly past $j$ (to $\mathrm{end}+1$
or $\mathrm{run}(j)+1$, both $>j$).  $\mathrm{sel}$ scans cyclically from
$c$ over the routable region and returns the first unverified slot, so the
sequence of selected slots visits the unverified slots in cyclic index
order.  Slots of $U$ are unverified at every selection, hence each is
visited exactly once per full cycle, and $u$ is revisited only after the
cycle has passed every other member of $U$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is the correction of the design that shipped first, which
selected the lowest unverified slot: a slot whose true successor is alive
but never admits (the common NAT case) then monopolized the single attempt
forever and left every higher range unverified.

\begin{definition}[Retry floor]\label{def:retry}
The retry state is $(\ell,k,\nu)$: last issue time $\ell$, failure streak
$k\in[0,255]$, and earliest retry $\nu$.  An issue is permitted at time $t$
iff $t-\ell\ge T_{\min}=1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t\ge\nu$.  A failure sets
$k\gets k+1$ (saturating) and $\nu\gets t+f(k)$ with
$f(k)=\min(2^{\min(k,6)}\,\mathrm{s},\,60\,\mathrm{s})$, i.e.\
$2,4,8,16,32,60,60,\dots$ seconds.  Progress (an applied proof or a local
confirmation that verifies a slot or supersedes the attempt) sets
$k\gets0$, $\nu\gets\bot$; $\ell$ is kept, so progress never bypasses
$T_{\min}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Issue]\label{law:issue}
A tick at time $t$ with fresh UUID $\mathrm{id}$ first retires an expired
attempt (as a failure, returning no action on that tick so the scheduler
observes the new floor), then, if the attempt is $\Idle$, the table is not
fully verified, and Definition~\ref{def:retry} permits, selects
$i=\mathrm{sel}(c)$ and sets the attempt to $\AwR((i,\mathrm{id}),e,t+T_r)$
and $\ell\gets t$.  If $t_i$ resolves locally the proof is applied at once;
otherwise exactly one routed lookup is emitted.  At most one lookup is
emitted per tick.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Revalidation gate]\label{law:revalidate}
A periodic revalidation reopens (sets $\ver\gets\bot$ on) the run of equal
hints following the persisted cursor $x$ (the ``\texttt{fix\_finger\_index}''
of the table, moved to $\mathrm{end}$ by each applied proof), wrapping once,
iff the attempt is $\Idle$ and
\[
\neg\bigl(\exists j\ge r:\neg\ver_j\bigr)\;\vee\;k>0 .
\]
\end{law}

Pending routable work defers revalidation only while it is succeeding.
If the only pending work is failing ($k>0$), revalidation proceeds, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:starve} the reopened range is served before the
failing slot is retried; without the $k>0$ disjunct one permanently failing
slot would freeze the revalidation of every proved range.

\section{Report Claims}\label{sec:claims}

Two other report-driven flows may change topology: the stabilization report
(the head's successor list and predecessor, answering the periodic query)
and the successor-list synchronization report (a successor's list,
requested on join).  Both may cause the node to open connections to
advertised peers, and both were previously accepted from any origin at any
time.  Each is now correlated by a token with two phases.

\begin{definition}[Stabilization token]\label{def:stab-token}
The state holds at most one $(h,\mathrm{id},\phi)$ with $h$ the head
queried, $\mathrm{id}$ a fresh UUID, and $\phi\in\{\Req,\Proc\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Stabilization round]\label{law:stab}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Begin.}  With no head, the token is cleared and nothing is sent.
  While the current token is $\Proc$, nothing changes and nothing is sent.
  Otherwise the token becomes $(\head(n),\mathrm{id},\Req)$ and the query
  carrying $\mathrm{id}$ is sent to the head.
\item \emph{Claim.}  A report from origin $h'$ with token $\mathrm{id}'$
  claims iff $(h',\mathrm{id}',\Req)$ equals the stored token; the claim
  moves it to $\Proc$.  The predicate and the move are one transition, so
  two deliveries of one report cannot both claim.
\item \emph{Candidates.}  The report's predecessor and successors, in that
  order, are reduced to the first $K{+}1$ distinct peers other than $n$
  before any per-candidate work, then ordered by transport quality.  Before
  each connection the token is re-checked to be
  $(h',\mathrm{id}',\Proc)$; a mismatch ends the plan with no further
  effect.
\item \emph{Apply.}  A report is applied iff the stored token is
  $(h',\mathrm{id}',\Proc)$; application merges the successor list, emits
  the improvement query and the notify, applies Lemma~\ref{lem:local} when
  $h'$ is still the head and reported $\pred(h')=n$, and clears the token.
  There is no token-less application path.
\item \emph{Release.}  The claim is a guard released when the handler
  leaves by any path (completion, error, cancellation); release clears the
  token only if it still carries $\mathrm{id}'$.  A head change clears the
  token unconditionally.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

Clause~1's $\Proc$ case and clause~5 are a pair.  Beginning a new round
every period while a handler was mid-way through its candidate budget made
the handler observe a stale token and abandon the round without applying
the report; with one candidate whose handshake outlasts the period, the
report was never applied.  Not superseding a $\Proc$ token is safe only
because clause~5 guarantees that a token cannot remain $\Proc$ after its
handler is gone.  The guard must be constructed lazily: constructing it for
a failed claim and dropping it would release a token the caller never
owned.

\begin{definition}[Sync tokens]\label{def:sync-token}
The state holds a map from reporter $p$ to $(\mathrm{id},\phi)$, at most one
entry per current successor.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Sync round]\label{law:sync}
Begin registers $(\mathrm{id},\Req)$ for $p$ iff $p$ is a current successor
and $p$'s entry is not $\Proc$; it supersedes an unanswered entry.  Claim
and per-candidate re-check are as in Law~\ref{law:stab} with the successor
list in place of the head and budget $K$.  Every successor-list commit
\emph{prunes} entries whose reporter is no longer a successor and keeps the
rest; a plan therefore keeps its remaining budget while its reporter stays,
including when its own admissions extend the list, and loses it when the
reporter leaves.
\end{law}

The prune replaces a clear-on-any-change that revoked a plan by its own
first admission, so that realised fan-in per report was between $1$ and
$K$ depending on interleaving.

\begin{proposition}[Effect bound]\label{prop:effects}
One stabilization report causes at most $K{+}1$ connection attempts and one
topology commit; one sync report at most $K$ connection attempts.  After a
token is cleared, at most the one connection already permitted may
complete; no further candidate is issued.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The candidate list is bounded before any per-candidate effect and each
candidate is consumed once by a cursor that advances only on a successful
re-check; clearing the token makes every later re-check fail.  Apply
requires $\Proc$ and clears the token, so it happens at most once.
\end{proof}

\section{Scheduler}\label{sec:scheduler}

The scheduler is the only place that reads a wall clock or draws
randomness, and it draws from a replayable mixed state seeded by the node's
identifier and a per-lifecycle UUID.

\begin{law}[Wake time]\label{law:wake}
Let the finger status at poll time $t$ be the attempt phase and $k$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $\AwR$ or $\AwA$ with remaining lease $\Delta$: next wake $t+\Delta$;
  no jitter.
\item Entering runnable from inactive (first successor admitted) with
  $k=0$: next wake $t+1\,\mathrm{s}+J$, $J\sim[0,10\,\mathrm{s}]$.
\item Any other entry into runnable, or a change of $k$: next wake
  $t+f(k)+J'$, $J'\sim[0,f(k)]$ with $f(0)=1\,\mathrm{s}$.
\item Runnable and unchanged: keep the existing wake, so polling cannot
  postpone work.
\end{itemize}
Completing a finger turn at time $t'$ schedules $t'+f(k)+J'$ from the
completion, never from the missed deadline.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Suspension]\label{law:suspend}
If both the gap since the last poll and the lateness of a runnable finger
deadline are at least $10\,\mathrm{s}$, the deadline is redrawn as
$t+1\,\mathrm{s}+J$ and priority deferrals are reset.  A retained admission
proof stays dormant until its lease expires.  The lifecycle UUID is owned
by the ring, not the listener, so stopping and restarting a browser
listener replays the same phase; constructing a new ring draws a new one.
\end{law}

A finger turn yields to a simultaneously due topology or storage phase at
most twice before it is reserved.

\section{State Relation}\label{sec:spec}

The following hold in every reachable state of $\mathrm{step}$.

\begin{invariant}[Proof freshness]\label{inv:fresh}
$\ver_j=\top$ implies that a proof $\pi$ with $j\in[\pi.i,\pi.\mathrm{end}]$
and $\pi.e\ge\chg_j$ was applied, or Law~\ref{law:confirm} confirmed $j$,
after the last change of $\hint_j$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Single owner]\label{inv:owner}
The attempt is exactly one of $\Idle$, $\AwR$, $\AwA$; the request
identifiers of all attempts ever issued by one process are distinct; two
attempts issued by one process are at least $T_{\min}$ apart.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Exact consumption]\label{inv:consume}
A state change caused by a report is caused by a report satisfying
Law~\ref{law:consume}; a stale report changes neither state nor $k$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Failure accounting]\label{inv:failure}
$k$ increases only by the rows of Law~\ref{law:outcomes} marked $k{+}1$
and returns to $0$ only on progress; $\nu$ is set only together with an
increase of $k$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Cyclic service]\label{inv:cyclic}
Selected slots form a cyclic sequence over the unverified routable slots
(Proposition~\ref{prop:starve}).
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Token discipline]\label{inv:token}
A stabilization or sync report is applied, or opens a connection, only
while its exact $(\text{reporter},\mathrm{id})$ is $\Proc$; a token is
$\Proc$ only while a live handler holds its claim; a head change or the
reporter leaving the successor list clears the token.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Head law]\label{inv:head}
The action $\mathsf{SuccessorHeadChanged}(h)$ is the last action of a
transition iff the head moved to $h$; the stabilization token is cleared
in exactly those transitions.
\end{invariant}

\section{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

\begin{proposition}[Per-node cost]\label{prop:cost}
Per node and per full pass over the table, the routed finger lookups number
at most $R$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:lookups}) plus the number of ranges reopened
by revalidation during the pass, each lookup costing $O(\log N)$ forwarding
hops and one report; the local range costs zero lookups.  Emissions are at
least $T_{\min}$ apart and, after $k$ consecutive failures, at least $f(k)$
apart.  Per stabilization period a node sends one head query and at most
$K{+}1$ connection attempts; per sync report at most $K$.
\end{proposition}

The remaining cost that this design bounds but does not remove is a slot
whose successor never admits.  Each rotation spends one attempt on it: a
lookup, a deferral, and a handshake that occupies the single attempt for
up to $T_h$ before it is retired.  Proposition~\ref{prop:starve} guarantees
that the rest of the table converges once per rotation regardless; shortening
the rotation's cost requires the transport to remember unreachable peers
across attempts, which is outside the finger state machine.

Aggregate startup work over a fleet is $O(N\log N)$ lookups, spread per node
over the $[1,11]\,\mathrm{s}$ activation window.  The window smooths a
synchronized start; it is a per-node bound, not a global rate limit on a
seed.

\section{Security}\label{sec:security}

The adversary controls any number of identifiers, the network's delivery
order and timing, and any node other than $n$; it cannot forge signatures
(Assumption~\ref{as:auth}).  Identifier count is not bounded here (Sybil
resistance is the subject of a separate protocol).

\emph{Unsolicited reports.}  A finger report is applied only if it consumes
the attempt (Law~\ref{law:consume}); a topology report only under
Invariant~\ref{inv:token}.  An adversary who is not the queried peer cannot
match the origin; the queried peer can answer only the one query it was
sent, once.

\emph{Replay and reordering.}  A delayed report carries a UUID the state no
longer owns and is stale.  A report issued under epoch $e$ arriving after a
hint change at epoch $e'>e$ is rejected slot-wise (Law~\ref{law:admit}),
so it cannot restore the pre-change hint.  After a process restart the
table and the attempt are rebuilt empty; a pre-restart report can match no
post-restart request.  Numeric request counters that restart at zero were
exactly the hole this closes.

\emph{Wrong answers.}  A queried peer may answer with any $s$.  If
$2^{i}>\dist(n,s)$ the report is $\mathsf{Invalid}$ and charged as a
failure; otherwise it is admitted as a hint for its range and verified
\emph{relative to that peer's view}.  A lying successor can therefore
plant fingers, as in any Chord; by Zave's result~\cite{Zave2017} this
affects routing efficiency, never ring safety, and the range is
revalidated periodically.

\emph{Amplification.}  A report of any length yields at most $K{+}1$ (or
$K$) connection attempts, bounded before any per-candidate work; one finger
report yields at most one handshake, and one attempt is outstanding per
node.  A peer that answers and then refuses the handshake costs its victim
one attempt per rotation, bounded by Proposition~\ref{prop:starve}.

\emph{Clock manipulation.}  Deadlines are on the process-monotonic clock
(Assumption~\ref{as:clock}); a wall-clock change can neither extend nor
prematurely expire an attempt.

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verification}

The implementation executes the transition of Assumption~\ref{as:effects}
directly; the following obligations are discharged against that
transition, not against a shadow model.

\begin{obligation}[Oracle convergence]
For sparse, dense, wrapped, and mutation-matrix rings, driving
$\mathrm{step}$ with oracle answers reaches the finger table of the
membership view with at most $R$ routed lookups.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Bounded resource laws]
A finite explorer over the actions \{advance before deadline, advance to
deadline, progress, invalid report, late report, cancel, defer, admit
deferred, lose, duplicate, topology change, restart\} to depth $9$ checks
Invariants~\ref{inv:owner}--\ref{inv:failure}, the exact deadline cleanup,
and backoff growth, with restart modelled as production rebuilds: an empty
table rejoining the previous head.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Claim discipline]
A finite explorer over \{begin, claim, reserve candidate, execute
candidate, attempt superseded candidate, cancel, complete, deliver
superseded proof, deliver unclaimed proof, move head\} checks
Proposition~\ref{prop:effects}, that a report against a $\Req$ token
changes nothing, and that begin never revokes a $\Proc$ claim.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Starvation and gate]
A cancelled attempt at the first remote range is followed by an attempt at
the next range, selection wraps to the failing range only after every other
range is proved, and revalidation reopens a range while the only pending
work is failing.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Transport cascade]
A three-node dummy-transport ring drains the full missing-finger admission
cascade to quiescence within $20$ control submissions; a five-node ring
whose finger targets all lie in local ranges emits zero finger submissions.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Schedule]
Over $200$ deterministic nodes, activation deadlines lie in
$[1,11]\,\mathrm{s}$, saturated-retry deadlines in $[60,120]\,\mathrm{s}$,
no one-second bucket holds more than $30$ nodes, and changing only the
lifecycle UUID changes the deadline; returning to runnable from an awaited
report draws $[1,2]\,\mathrm{s}$, not the activation window.
\end{obligation}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Chord's original paper~\cite{Stoica2001} contains both the range lemma and
the hint model (``ask an immediate neighbor for a copy of its finger
table''); its journal revision~\cite{Stoica2003} replaced both with
randomized refresh, which is the behaviour most implementations inherit.
Zave~\cite{Zave2015,Zave2017} proved that Chord's ring maintenance is
correct only under the successor-list discipline and that fingers are
irrelevant to safety, which this design relies on to treat fingers as
revocable evidence.  Bamboo~\cite{Rhea2004} established that maintenance
under churn should back off on timeouts and never on new evidence, the rule
behind Law~\ref{law:outcomes}'s last row.  Kademlia~\cite{Maymounkov2002}
avoids the problem by making every lookup refresh the buckets it touches;
its $k$-buckets are the closest analogue of a proved range, though without
an epoch or an owner.  The evidence epoch is a version vector of length one
per slot~\cite{Fidge1988}; the claim guard is the standard
resource-acquisition-is-initialization idiom applied to a protocol token.

\section{Conclusion}

Finger convergence in Rings is Chord's range lemma wrapped in four
disciplines that the original paper did not need: evidence epochs, a single
correlated attempt owner, cyclic selection with a deterministic retry
floor, and single-use released claims on the reports that may open
connections.  Each is a law over one pure transition, each law is checked
against that transition, and together they bound what one node sends, what
one report can cause, and how long one unreachable peer can delay the rest
of the table.
