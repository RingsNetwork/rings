\noindent
Chord refreshes one finger per stabilization round, so a $160$-slot table
needs of the order of $160$ rounds and a hinted entry is indistinguishable
from a proved one.  Rings runs Chord over browsers whose WebRTC handshakes
take minutes or fail.  We specify its finger maintenance as one pure
transition $\mathrm{step}:(S,\mathrm{Event})\to(S,\mathrm{Actions})$ built
on Chord's range lemma (a lookup at slot $i$ answered at distance $d$
proves every slot $j$ with $2^{j}\le d$) and four disciplines: per-slot
evidence epochs, a single attempt owner $\Idle\mid\AwR\mid\AwA$ keyed by a
fresh UUID, cyclic starvation-free selection with a deterministic retry
floor, and single-use claims that bound each topology report to $K{+}1$
connections.  We state the laws, prove range, starvation-freedom, and
effect bounds, bound the per-node cost, and list the obligations
discharged by the shipped model checker.\par\vspace{0.6\baselineskip}}}

\section{Introduction}

Chord~\cite{Stoica2001} routes in $O(\log N)$ hops because node $n$ keeps
finger $i=\succf(n+2^{i})$.  The journal revision~\cite{Stoica2003}
refreshes one random finger per period; an $m$-bit table converges in
$\Theta(m)$ periods, and an entry copied from a neighbour is never
distinguished from a proved one.  Rings differs on two axes: members are
browsers, so a routing entry is usable only after a WebRTC handshake that
takes seconds to minutes and often fails; and $N\ll 2^{160}$, so a
$160$-slot table has only $O(\log N)$ distinct values.  Table~\ref{tab:delta}
summarizes what this design changes; the rest of the paper states each
change as a law over $\mathrm{step}$ and proves the bound it buys.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Chord finger maintenance versus this design}
\label{tab:delta}
\centering\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
concern & Chord 2003~\cite{Stoica2003} & here \\
\midrule

local range & routed lookups & proved by stabilization (Lem.~\ref{lem:local}) \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Ring]\label{def:ring}
Identifiers are $\mathbb{Z}/2^{m}$, $m=160$; $\dist(a,b)=(b-a)\bmod 2^{m}$.
For a membership view $V$, $\succf_V(x)=\arg\min_{s\in V}\dist(x,s)$ and
$\pred_V(x)=\arg\max_{s\in V}\dist(x,s)$.  Node $n$ keeps the $K$ nearest
clockwise members ($K=3$); $\head(n)$ is the first of them other than $n$,
or $\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finger slot]\label{def:slot}
Slot $i\in[0,L)$, $L\le m$, has target $t_i=n+2^{i}$ and true value
$\succf_V(t_i)$.  The table stores $\hint_i\in V\cup\{\bot\}$; a value equal
to $n$ is stored as $\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hint join]\label{def:join}
For a learned peer $p\ne n$ with $d=\dist(n,p)$:
\[
\forall i:\ 2^{i}\le d\wedge(\hint_i=\bot\vee\dist(n,\hint_i)>d)
\Rightarrow\hint_i\gets p .
\]
Removing $p$ clears every slot holding it and refills from the nearest
surviving hint above.  Neither operation produces proof.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Authenticated reports]\label{as:auth}
Every report is signed by its origin and rejected unless the origin is the
peer queried.  Attribution is transport's; \emph{solicitation} is what the
tokens of \S\ref{sec:attempt} and \S\ref{sec:claims} establish.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Clocks]\label{as:clock}
Finger deadlines are process-monotonic milliseconds; transport handshake
generations are wall-clock.  The two are never compared.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Effect boundary]\label{as:effects}
$\mathrm{step}:(S,\mathrm{Event})\to(S,\mathrm{Actions})$ is pure.  Clock
readings and fresh UUIDs~\cite{RFC9562} arrive as event fields; sends,
locks, and randomness happen only in the interpreter of the returned
actions.  Every law below is a property of $\mathrm{step}$ alone.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Range Lemma}\label{sec:range}

\begin{lemma}[Chord range lemma~\cite{Stoica2001}]\label{lem:range}
Let $s=\succf_V(t_i)$ and $d=\dist(n,s)$.  Then
\[
\forall j\ge i:\quad 2^{j}\le d\ \Rightarrow\ \succf_V(t_j)=s .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$[t_i,s)\cap V=\emptyset$ by definition of $\succf_V$.  For such $j$,
$2^{i}\le 2^{j}\le d$ gives $t_j\in[t_i,s]$, so the first member at or after
$t_j$ is $s$.
\end{proof}

This is Chord's \texttt{init\_finger\_table} test
$\mathit{finger}[i].\mathit{node}\ge\mathit{finger}[i{+}1].\mathit{start}$
\cite[Fig.~6]{Stoica2001}, used here as a proof rule.

\begin{definition}[Range proof]\label{def:proof}
A lookup for slot $i$ answered by $s$ yields
$\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\pend)$, with $\mathrm{id}$ the lookup UUID,
$e_\pi$ the epoch at issue (\S\ref{sec:evidence}), and
\[
\pend(i,s)=
\begin{cases}
L-1 & s=n,\\
\min(L{-}1,\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,s)\rfloor) & 2^{i}\le\dist(n,s),\\
\Invalid & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
$\pi$ covers slots $i..\pend$; $s=n$ means the arc from $t_i$ wrapped to
$n$ without meeting a member.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Lookups per pass]\label{cor:lookups}
If the true values partition the slots into $R$ maximal runs of equal
successor, a full pass needs at most $R$ routed lookups, and
$R\le\min(L,|V|)$; under uniform identifiers $\mathbb{E}[R]=O(\log N)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Local range]\label{lem:local}
Let $h=\head(n)$ and let $n$ hold a stabilization report from $h$,
answering $n$'s own query, stating $\pred(h)=n$.  Then
$\forall j:\ 2^{j}\le\dist(n,h)\Rightarrow\succf_V(t_j)=h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\pred(h)=n$ gives $(n,h)\cap V=\emptyset$; $t_j\in(n,h]$ iff
$2^{j}\le\dist(n,h)$.
\end{proof}

A routed lookup for $t_j\in(n,h]$ would travel the whole ring
($2\,O(\log N)$ hops) for a fact the stabilization exchange already
carries, so the local range never sends one.  The report must echo the
current stabilization token and $h$ must be the head both when queried and
after the report is applied.

\section{Evidence Epochs}\label{sec:evidence}

\begin{definition}[Evidence]\label{def:evidence}
The evidence state is $(e,(\chg_j,\ver_j)_{j<L})$, $e\in\mathbb{N}$ the
epoch, $\chg_j$ the epoch at which $\hint_j$ last changed, $\ver_j$ a bit.
Initially $e=0$, $\chg_j=0$, $\ver_j=\bot$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Hint change]\label{law:change}
For a topology transition $H\to H'$ with $C=\{j:H_j\ne H'_j\}\ne\emptyset$:
$e\gets e+1$ and $\forall j\in C:\ (\chg_j,\ver_j)\gets(e,\bot)$.  On
overflow of $e$: $\forall j:\ \ver_j\gets\bot$ and the attempt is retired.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Admission]\label{law:admit}
Proof $\pi$ may write slot $j\in[\pi.i,\pi.\pend]$ iff $\chg_j\le e_\pi$.
It is accepted iff some slot admits it (Algorithm~\ref{alg:admit}); admitted
slots are written, the rest are skipped, none is rolled back.  Acceptance
and commit are the same per-slot predicate, so an accepted proof always
writes.
\end{law}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\Admit(\pi)$: commit a range proof slot-wise}
\label{alg:admit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\pend)$, evidence $(e,(\chg_j,\ver_j))$
\State $A(\pi)\gets\{\,j\in[i,\pend]:\chg_j\le e_\pi\,\}$
\If{$A(\pi)=\emptyset$} \State \Return $\Stale$ \EndIf
\ForAll{$j\in A(\pi)$}
  \State $\hint_j\gets(s=n\ ?\ \bot:s)$;\quad $\ver_j\gets\top$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathrm{Applied}(\pend)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{law}[Discontinuity]\label{law:discontinuity}
Losing the last successor sets $e\gets e+1$, $\forall j:\ \ver_j\gets\bot$,
$\att\gets\Idle$.  Ranges stay unverified but $\Dormant$ until a
successor is admitted again; isolation is not proof of emptiness.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Local confirmation]\label{law:confirm}
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:local} sets $\ver_j\gets\top$ for
$j\le\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h)\rfloor$ with no lookup.  If $\att$'s slot lies in that
range, $\Progress(\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h)\rfloor)$ (Law~\ref{law:outcomes}):
superseded, not failed.
\end{law}

\section{Attempt Ownership}\label{sec:attempt}

\begin{definition}[Attempt]\label{def:attempt}
At most one attempt $\att$ exists:
\[
\att\in\ \Idle\ \mid\ \AwR(\rho,e_\rho,\tau_r)\ \mid\ \AwA(\pi,\tau_a),
\]
$\rho=(i,\mathrm{id})$ the request, $e_\rho$ the epoch at issue, $\tau_r$
the report deadline, $\pi$ a validated proof, $\tau_a$ the admission lease.
Two owners are not representable.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Consumption]\label{law:consume}
A report $(\rho',s')$ consumes the attempt iff
\[
\bigl[\AwR(\rho,\cdot,\cdot)\wedge\rho'=\rho\bigr]\ \vee\
\bigl[\AwA(\pi,\cdot)\wedge\rho'=\pi.\rho\wedge s'=\pi.s\bigr].
\]
Any other report is $\Stale$: no state change, no failure.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Deadlines]\label{law:deadlines}
\begin{gather*}
\tau_r=\mathrm{issued}+T_r,\qquad \tau_a=\mathrm{deferred}+T_a,\\
T_r=10\,\mathrm{s},\qquad T_h=180\,\mathrm{s},\qquad T_a=T_h+30\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{gather*}
$T_h$ is the transport's handshake-generation lifetime.
\end{law}

A proof is deferred to $\AwA$ the moment a timely report names an
unconnected peer, before any handshake is awaited, so a cancelled handler
cannot strand it; the generation, once reserved, releases the proof by its
own admission or expiry.  $T_a>T_h$ makes the lease a backstop that cannot
expire first and discard a proof whose handshake later succeeds; the
converse (committing a released proof) is excluded by Law~\ref{law:consume}.

\begin{law}[Outcomes]\label{law:outcomes}
A consuming report is validated in the order token, deadline, geometry
(Definition~\ref{def:proof}), epoch (Law~\ref{law:admit});
Algorithm~\ref{alg:report} gives the transitions in terms of the failure
streak $k$ and retry floor $\nu$ of Definition~\ref{def:retry} and three
named transitions on $(\att,c,k,\nu)$:
\begin{align*}
\Fail(i)&:\ (\Idle,\ \run(i)+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1))\\
\Retire(\pend)&:\ (\Idle,\ \pend+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1))\\
\Progress(\pend)&:\ (\Idle,\ \pend+1,\ 0,\ \bot).
\end{align*}
\end{law}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Report$(\rho'=(i,\mathrm{id}'),s',t,a)$; $a$ is the transport's
verdict on $s'$}
\label{alg:report}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{$\neg\,\mathrm{consumes}(\att,\rho',s')$} \State \Return $\Stale$ \Comment{Law~\ref{law:consume}} \EndIf
\If{$t\ge\tau$} \State $\Fail(i)$;\ \Return $\Expired$ \EndIf
\If{$\att=\AwA(\pi,\cdot)$} \State $\pi'\gets\pi$ \Comment{already validated}
\Else
  \State $\pend\gets\pend(i,s')$
  \If{$\pend=\Invalid$} \State $\Fail(i)$;\ \Return $\Invalid$ \EndIf
  \State $\pi'\gets(i,\mathrm{id}',e_\rho,s',\pend)$
  \If{$A(\pi')=\emptyset$} \State $\att\gets\Idle$;\ \Return $\Stale$ \Comment{churn, not failure} \EndIf
\EndIf
\If{$a=\mathrm{Connected}$} \State $\Admit(\pi')$;\ $\Progress(\pend)$
\ElsIf{$a=\mathrm{Pending}$} \State $\att\gets\AwA(\pi',t+T_a)$ \Comment{deferred}
\Else \State $\Retire(\pend)$ \Comment{unroutable}
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

A hint change at the attempt's lower slot (Law~\ref{law:change}) destroys
the premise of its proof and retires the owner without raising $k$: churn is
new evidence, not a network failure~\cite{Rhea2004}.

\section{Selection and Retry}\label{sec:selection}

\begin{definition}[Routable region]\label{def:routable}
With $h=\head(n)$, the first routable slot is
$r=\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h)\rfloor+1$ ($r=0$ if $h=\bot$).  Slots below $r$
are the local range and are never selected.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cursor and runs]\label{def:cursor}
The state carries $c\in\mathbb{N}$, initially $0$.  With
$A=\{j\ge\max(c,r):\neg\ver_j\}$ and $B=\{j\in[r,\max(c,r)):\neg\ver_j\}$,
\begin{gather*}
\sel(c)=\begin{cases}\min A & A\ne\emptyset\\ \min B & B\ne\emptyset\\ \bot & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\\
\run(i)=\max\{\,j\ge i:\forall l\in[i,j],\ \hint_l=\hint_i\,\}.
\end{gather*}
After an applied proof or a retired validated proof, $c\gets\pend+1$; after
$\Fail(i)$, $c\gets\run(i)+1$, because slots sharing a hint expect the same
successor.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Retry floor]\label{def:retry}
Retry state $(\ell,k,\nu)$: last issue time, failure streak
$k\in[0,255]$ (saturating), earliest retry.  Issue at $t$ is permitted iff
\[
t-\ell\ge T_{\min}=1\,\mathrm{s}\ \wedge\ t\ge\nu,\quad
f(k)=\min(2^{\min(k,6)}\,\mathrm{s},\,60\,\mathrm{s}).
\]
Progress sets $k\gets0$, $\nu\gets\bot$ and keeps $\ell$, so progress never
bypasses $T_{\min}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Issue]\label{law:issue}
A tick is Algorithm~\ref{alg:tick}.  At most one lookup is emitted per
tick; the expiry tick emits none so the scheduler observes the new floor.
\end{law}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Tick$(t,\mathrm{id})$: retire an expired attempt, else issue}
\label{alg:tick}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{$\att\ne\Idle\ \wedge\ t\ge\tau$} \State $\Fail(\att.i)$;\ \Return $\emptyset$ \EndIf
\If{$\att\ne\Idle\ \vee\ \forall j\,\ver_j\ \vee\ t-\ell<T_{\min}\ \vee\ t<\nu$} \State \Return $\emptyset$ \EndIf
\State $i\gets\sel(c)$;\ \textbf{if} $i=\bot$ \textbf{then return} $\emptyset$
\State $\att\gets\AwR((i,\mathrm{id}),e,t+T_r)$;\ $\ell\gets t$
\If{$t_i\in(n,\head(n)]$} \State apply Law~\ref{law:confirm};\ \Return $\emptyset$
\Else \State \Return $\{\mathrm{Lookup}(i,\mathrm{id})\}$ \EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Starvation freedom]\label{prop:starve}
Let $U$ be the routable slots that stay unverified across a sequence of
attempts.  Between two selections of $u\in U$, every other slot of $U$ is
selected at least once.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each attempt at $j$ moves $c$ strictly past $j$ ($\pend+1$ or $\run(j)+1$,
both $>j$), and $\sel$ returns the first unverified routable slot cyclically
from $c$.  Members of $U$ are unverified at every selection, so the
selected sequence visits $U$ in cyclic index order, once per cycle.
\end{proof}

The first shipped design selected $\min\{j\ge r:\neg\ver_j\}$; one slot
whose successor never admits (the NAT case) then held the single attempt
forever.

\begin{law}[Revalidation gate]\label{law:revalidate}
A periodic revalidation sets $\ver_j\gets\bot$ for $j\in[c',\run(c')]$,
$c'=c\bmod L$, iff
\[
\att=\Idle\ \wedge\ \bigl(\neg\exists j\ge r:\neg\ver_j\ \vee\ k>0\bigr).
\]
\end{law}

Pending routable work defers revalidation only while succeeding.  Without
the $k>0$ disjunct one permanently failing slot would freeze the
revalidation of every proved range; with it, Proposition~\ref{prop:starve}
serves the reopened range before the failing slot is retried.

\section{Report Claims}\label{sec:claims}

Two other report-driven flows may open connections: the stabilization
report (the head's successor list and predecessor) and the successor-list
synchronization report.  Each is correlated by a two-phase token.

\begin{definition}[Tokens]\label{def:tokens}
The stabilization token is at most one $\tok=(h,\mathrm{id},\phi)$,
$\phi\in\{\Req,\Proc\}$.  Sync tokens form a map $p\mapsto(\mathrm{id},\phi)$
with at most one entry per current successor.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Stabilization round]\label{law:stab}
The round is Algorithm~\ref{alg:stab}.  Claim is one transition (predicate
and phase move), so two deliveries of one report cannot both claim.  Candidates
are ordered by transport quality.  The release guard is constructed only by
a successful claim; a head change clears $\tok$ unconditionally.
\end{law}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Stabilization round for reporter $h'$, token $\mathrm{id}'$}
\label{alg:stab}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Begin}{$\mathrm{id}$}
  \If{$\head(n)=\bot$} \State $\tok\gets\bot$;\ \Return $\emptyset$ \EndIf
  \If{$\tok.\phi=\Proc$} \State \Return $\emptyset$ \Comment{never revoke a live claim} \EndIf
  \State $\tok\gets(\head(n),\mathrm{id},\Req)$;\ \Return $\{\mathrm{Query}(\tok)\}$
\EndProcedure
\Procedure{Handle}{$h',\mathrm{id}',\pred',\mathrm{succs}'$}
  \If{$\tok\ne(h',\mathrm{id}',\Req)$} \State \Return \Comment{unclaimed: dropped} \EndIf
  \State $\tok.\phi\gets\Proc$;\ guard $\gets$ \Call{Release}{$\mathrm{id}'$} on every exit
  \State $C\gets$ first $K{+}1$ distinct of $(\pred',\mathrm{succs}')\setminus\{n\}$
  \ForAll{$x\in C$}
    \If{$\tok\ne(h',\mathrm{id}',\Proc)$} \State \Return \EndIf
    \State await $\mathrm{Connect}(x)$
  \EndFor
  \If{$\tok=(h',\mathrm{id}',\Proc)$}
    \State merge $\mathrm{succs}'$;\ emit improvement query, notify
    \If{$\head(n)=h'\ \wedge\ \pred'=n$} \State confirm $[0,\lfloor\log_2\dist(n,h')\rfloor]$ \Comment{Lem.~\ref{lem:local}} \EndIf
    \State $\tok\gets\bot$
  \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\Procedure{Release}{$\mathrm{id}'$}
  \If{$\tok.\mathrm{id}=\mathrm{id}'$} \State $\tok\gets\bot$ \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{law}[Sync round]\label{law:sync}
Begin registers $(\mathrm{id},\Req)$ for $p$ iff $p$ is a current successor
and $p$'s entry is not $\Proc$, superseding an unanswered entry.  Claim and
per-candidate re-check follow Algorithm~\ref{alg:stab} with budget $K$.
Every successor-list commit prunes entries whose reporter left the list and
keeps the rest, so a plan keeps its budget while its reporter stays,
including when its own admissions extend the list.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Effect bound]\label{prop:effects}
One stabilization report causes at most $K{+}1$ connection attempts and one
topology commit; one sync report at most $K$.  After a token is cleared,
at most the one connection already permitted completes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$C$ is bounded before any per-candidate effect; the cursor over $C$ advances
only on a successful re-check, and a cleared token fails every later
re-check.  Apply requires $\Proc$ and clears the token, so it happens at
most once.
\end{proof}

\section{Scheduler}\label{sec:scheduler}

The scheduler is the only reader of a wall clock and the only source of
randomness, drawn from a replayable state seeded by the node identifier
and a per-lifecycle UUID.

\begin{law}[Wake time]\label{law:wake}
The finger phase at poll time $t$ is one of $\Dormant$ (no head),
$\Converged$ (no unverified routable slot, no attempt), $\Runnable$, $\AwR$,
$\AwA$, with streak $k$ and remaining lease $\Delta$.  With
$f(0)=1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $\U[0,x]$ a uniform jitter draw,
\[
\mathrm{wake}(t)=
\begin{cases}
t+\Delta & \text{(a) } \AwR\vee\AwA,\\
t+1\,\mathrm{s}+\U[0,10\,\mathrm{s}] & \text{(b) activation},\\
t+f(k)+\U[0,f(k)] & \text{(c) re-entry},\\
\text{unchanged} & \text{(d) otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
where (b) is $\Dormant\to\Runnable$ with $k=0$ (first successor admitted),
(c) any other entry into $\Runnable$ or a change of $k$, and (d)
$\Runnable$ unchanged, so polling cannot postpone work.
A finger turn completing at $t'$ schedules $t'+f(k)+\U[0,f(k)]$ from $t'$,
never from the missed deadline.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Suspension]\label{law:suspend}
If the gap since the last poll and the lateness of a runnable finger
deadline are both $\ge10\,\mathrm{s}$, the deadline is redrawn as
$t+1\,\mathrm{s}+\U[0,10\,\mathrm{s}]$ and priority deferrals reset.  A
retained proof stays dormant until $\tau_a$.  The lifecycle UUID belongs to
the ring, not the listener: restarting a listener replays the phase; a new
ring draws a new one.  A finger turn yields to a simultaneously due
topology or storage phase at most twice.
\end{law}

\section{State Relation}\label{sec:spec}

The following hold in every reachable state of $\mathrm{step}$.

\begin{invariant}[Proof freshness]\label{inv:fresh}
$\ver_j=\top\Rightarrow$ some $\pi$ with $j\in[\pi.i,\pi.\pend]$ and
$\pi.e\ge\chg_j$ was applied, or Law~\ref{law:confirm} confirmed $j$, after
the last change of $\hint_j$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Single owner]\label{inv:owner}
$\att$ is exactly one of $\Idle,\AwR,\AwA$; request identifiers issued
by one process are distinct; consecutive issues are $\ge T_{\min}$ apart.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Exact consumption]\label{inv:consume}
Every report-caused state change is caused by a report satisfying
Law~\ref{law:consume}; a $\Stale$ report changes neither state nor $k$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Failure accounting]\label{inv:failure}
$k$ increases only by $\Fail$ or $\Retire$, returns to $0$ only by
$\Progress$ or Law~\ref{law:confirm}, and $\nu$ is set only together with
an increase of $k$.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Cyclic service]\label{inv:cyclic}
Selected slots form a cyclic sequence over the unverified routable slots
(Proposition~\ref{prop:starve}).
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Token discipline]\label{inv:token}
A stabilization or sync report is applied, or opens a connection, only
while its exact $(\text{reporter},\mathrm{id})$ is $\Proc$; a token is
$\Proc$ only while a live handler holds its claim; a head change or the
reporter leaving the successor list clears it.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Head law]\label{inv:head}
$\mathsf{SuccessorHeadChanged}(h)$ is the last action of a transition iff the
head moved to $h$; the stabilization token is cleared in exactly those
transitions.
\end{invariant}

\section{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

\begin{proposition}[Per-node cost]\label{prop:cost}
Per full pass, routed finger lookups number at most $R$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:lookups}) plus the ranges reopened by revalidation
during the pass, each $O(\log N)$ hops and one report; the local range
costs zero.  Emissions are $\ge T_{\min}$ apart and, after $k$ failures,
$\ge f(k)$ apart.  Per stabilization period: one head query and $\le K{+}1$
connection attempts; per sync report $\le K$.
\end{proposition}

A slot whose successor never admits costs one attempt per rotation (a
lookup, a deferral, and up to $T_h$ of handshake); by
Proposition~\ref{prop:starve} the rest of the table still converges once
per rotation.  Fleet startup is $O(N\log N)$ lookups, each node's spread
over its own $[1,11]\,\mathrm{s}$ activation window; this is a per-node
bound, not a global rate limit on a seed.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Chord's conference paper~\cite{Stoica2001} contains both the range lemma
and the hint model; its journal revision~\cite{Stoica2003} replaced both
with randomized refresh.  Zave~\cite{Zave2015,Zave2017} proved ring
correctness rests on the successor list alone and fingers are irrelevant
to safety, the licence under which this design treats every finger as
revocable evidence.  Bamboo~\cite{Rhea2004} set the rule that maintenance
backs off on timeouts, never on new evidence (last branch of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:report}).  Kademlia's $k$-buckets~\cite{Maymounkov2002}
are the nearest analogue of a proved range, without epoch or owner.  The
evidence epoch is a length-one version vector per slot~\cite{Fidge1988}.

\section{Conclusion}

Finger convergence in Rings is Chord's range lemma under four disciplines
the original did not need: evidence epochs, one correlated attempt owner,
cyclic selection with a deterministic retry floor, and single-use claims on
connection-opening reports.  Each is a law over one pure transition, checked
against that transition, and together they bound what one node sends, what
one report can cause, and how long one unreachable peer can delay the rest
of the table.
