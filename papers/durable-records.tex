{\centering\bfseries Abstract\par}%
\vspace{0.4\baselineskip}%
\noindent
A Rings node keeps security state (transaction replay windows), privacy state (onion entry
guards) and custody state (relay mail) in local key-value stores on two platform families:
a native file system and a browser's IndexedDB.  Five open defects of these stores reduce to
two predicates: \emph{presence}, whether a record that should exist exists, and
\emph{survival}, whether a completed write outlives a crash.  We give one model for both.
A store's authority is a point of the product of a crash chain
$\Volatile\sqsubset\Cached\sqsubset\Durable$ and a meaning chain
$\Rebuild\sqsubset\Retained\sqsubset\Auth$; each point fixes what an absent record means and
which obligations the platform must discharge.  We choose a per-record presence marker as
the witness that separates a lost authoritative record from a never-written one, prove it
sound and complete for partial loss, and prove that no witness in the same failure domain
detects total loss.  We state the crash obligation per platform, verified against the
Chromium, Gecko and WebKit sources (only \jsq{strict} durability flushes in all three),
forbid eviction for authoritative records, and give a custody law that makes relay mail
survive a restart between admission and consumption, with the impossibility result that
bounds what any receiver can do for mail that has fallen behind live traffic.  A browser
can only claim durability, since an ephemeral context is indistinguishable from a persistent
one; we admit a node's own state on the claim, with each ephemeral session end as the
documented reset, and admit custody of other nodes' mail on a claim only across $r\ge2$
holders.
}\hfill\null\par

\section{Problem}\label{sec:problem}

The defects, each named by its issue:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\issue{915}] An absent native replay record is indistinguishable from a stream never
seen, so its stream restarts from $\mathsf{First}$; on non-unix targets a completed rename
can roll back.
\item[\issue{923}] The browser replay store evicts its least recently used row at its row
cap, so a retained stream can restart from $\mathsf{First}$.
\item[\issue{912}] Browser replay writes use the default durability hint, so a completed
admission can roll back after an OS crash.
\item[\issue{911}] The entry-guard store is disposable: a crash can lose or tear the guard
set, re-selecting the first hop.
\item[\issue{936}] Relay mail admitted into the replay window and queued, but not taken
before a restart, is refused as a replay on redelivery and lost.
\end{description}
\issue{915}, \issue{923} are presence defects; \issue{912}, \issue{911} are survival defects;
\issue{936} is a presence defect of a fact that lives in no store at all (that the queued mail
was never consumed).  Section~\ref{sec:model} fixes the model, Section~\ref{sec:lattice} the
authority lattice, Sections~\ref{sec:witness}--\ref{sec:custody} the five laws, and
Section~\ref{sec:plan} the order of implementation.  One defect was found after the batch:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\issue{1036}] A browser context whose IndexedDB opens but is ephemeral (a private
window, ephemeral partitioned storage) claims $\Durable$, and nothing the page observes
tells it apart; Section~\ref{sec:claimed} rules on it.
\end{description}

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Store]\label{def:store}
A store is a finite partial map $\sigma:\Nm\rightharpoonup\Vl$ from record names to values.
Its operations are $\mathrm{put}(n,v)$, $\mathrm{rm}(n)$ and $\mathrm{get}(n)$, each
returning $\Ok$ or $\Err$; $\mathrm{scan}$ returns one classification per name
(Section~\ref{sec:witness}).  For \texttt{FileStorage} the name of key $k$ is
$\name(k)=\mathrm{hex}(\mathrm{SHA1}(k))$; for \texttt{IdbStorage} it is $k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[History, completion]\label{def:history}
A history is $h=o_1\cdots o_k\,o_{k+1}^{?}$: operations $o_1..o_k$ returned, $o_{k+1}$ may
be in flight.  Let $\sigma_i=o_i\circ\cdots\circ o_1(\sigma_0)$, with $\sigma_{k+1}=\sigma_k$
when nothing is in flight.  An operation is \emph{completed} iff it returned $\Ok$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Crash, recovery image]\label{def:crash}
A crash ends the process (\emph{process crash}) or the operating system
(\emph{system crash}: kernel panic, power loss).  The recovery image $R_P(h)$ is the set of
maps the next open of the store can read on platform $P$ after a crash at the end of $h$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Loss events]\label{def:loss}
A loss event is a change of the store's physical state that no operation of the store made:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\lambda_{\mathrm{part}}(S)$, \emph{partial loss}: the records of $S\subsetneq\dom\sigma$
vanish (media error repaired by \texttt{fsck}, an operator removing or renaming one file, a
completed rename rolled back by a platform that does not discharge
Obligation~\ref{ob:survival});
\item $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$, \emph{total loss}: the whole failure domain vanishes (the
store's directory, an IndexedDB database or origin, a process heap).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Loss events are what the platform or the operator does, not what an adversary forges: a
party that can write the store can also write any witness of it, so nothing below
authenticates a store, and nothing models a state the program and platform cannot reach.

\subsection{Obligations}

\begin{obligation}[Atomicity, A]\label{ob:atomicity}
$\forall\sigma'\in R_P(h).\ \forall n.\ \sigma'(n)\in\{\sigma_i(n): i\le k+1\}\cup\{\bot\}$:
a record is never torn.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Survival, D]\label{ob:survival}
$\forall\sigma'\in R_P(h).\ \forall n.\ \sigma'(n)\in\{\sigma_k(n),\sigma_{k+1}(n)\}$:
every completed write and removal survives a process or system crash, and the one in
flight is either whole or absent.  D implies A.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[No eviction, N]\label{ob:noevict}
$\mathrm{put}(n,v)$ on $\sigma$ yields $\sigma[n\mapsto v]$ or returns
$\Err(\Exhausted)$ with $\sigma$ unchanged; no operation removes a name it was not given.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Witnessed presence, W]\label{ob:witness}
After any history and any partial loss $\lambda_{\mathrm{part}}(S)$, $\mathrm{scan}$ reports
every $n\in S\cap\dom\sigma_k$ as $\Missing(n)$, and reports $\Missing(n)$ only for an $n$
whose record some $\mathrm{put}$ made durable.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Fail closed, F]\label{ob:failclosed}
A record that does not decode is reported and kept; only its owner or an operator removes it.
\end{obligation}

\section{The authority lattice}\label{sec:lattice}

\begin{definition}[Crash chain]\label{def:crashchain}
$C=\Volatile\sqsubset\Cached\sqsubset\Durable$ with obligations
$O(\Volatile)=\emptyset$ ($R_P(h)=\{\emptyset\}$ at every restart), $O(\Cached)=\emptyset$
(any $\sigma_i(n)$, $\bot$ or a torn record), $O(\Durable)=\{A,D\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Meaning chain]\label{def:meaningchain}
$M=\Rebuild\sqsubset\Retained\sqsubset\Auth$, read as what $\sigma'(n)=\bot$ means to the
owner:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\Rebuild$: $\bot$ is the owner's default; any record may be dropped;
\item $\Retained$: $\bot$ is ``never written, or retired by the owner''; a loss is a
liveness failure;
\item $\Auth$: $\bot$ is ``never written''; a loss is a safety failure.
\end{itemize}
$O(\Rebuild)=\emptyset$, $O(\Retained)=\{N\}$, $O(\Auth)=\{N,W,F\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Authority]\label{def:authority}
An authority is $a=(c,m)\in C\times M$, ordered componentwise, with
$O(c,m)=O(c)\cup O(m)$.  $C\times M$ is a lattice (a product of chains, meet and join
componentwise) and $O$ is monotone: $a\sqsubseteq b\Rightarrow O(a)\subseteq O(b)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Admissibility]\label{law:admissible}
Let $\req(\Rebuild)=\Volatile$ and $\req(\Retained)=\req(\Auth)=\Durable$.  A store may be
opened at $(c,m)$ only if $c\sqsupseteq\req(m)$.
\end{law}
\begin{proof}[Justification]
Under $c\sqsubset\Durable$ a crash may lose a completed write, so $\bot$ after recovery can
be a loss; a meaning above $\Rebuild$ asserts that $\bot$ is not a loss.
\end{proof}
Law~\ref{law:admissible} reads $c$ as the crash class the platform establishes.  A browser
can only claim it (Assumption~\ref{as:claimed}); Section~\ref{sec:claimed} states when a
claimed class is admissible.

The existing \texttt{RecordAuthority} is the two-point chain
$\mathsf{Disposable}=(\Cached,\Rebuild)\sqsubset\mathsf{Authoritative}=(\Durable,\Auth)$
(\#909, \#910, \#914).  The law adds two points:
$\mathsf{DurableCache}=(\Durable,\Rebuild)$ for state whose loss is a downgrade, and
$\mathsf{Retained}=(\Durable,\Retained)$ for custody.  $\mathsf{MemStorage}$ is
$(\Volatile,-)$: admissible only at $\Rebuild$.

\section{Presence witness}\label{sec:witness}

\subsection{Choice}

W needs, at restore, a set $\wit\subseteq\Nm$ of names that were durably written, so that
$\wit\setminus\dom\sigma'$ are the lost names.  Fail-closed per stream (\#910) is keyed by
record name: $\mathrm{unavailable}(s)\iff\name(\rec(s))\in\U$.  A witness that does not
name the lost record cannot feed $\U$.  The candidates:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}
\toprule
Witness & names loss & $O(1)$/new key & operator step\\
\midrule
count $|\dom\sigma|$ & no & yes & edit count\\
epoch per record & no & yes & edit epoch\\
manifest log & yes & yes & edit binary log\\
per-record marker & yes & yes & remove a file\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A count or an epoch detects that something is missing but not which stream, so it can only
fail the whole store closed: one lost file would close every stream.  A manifest names the
loss but turns the operator's per-stream recovery into editing a binary log, and its torn
tail needs its own law.  We choose the marker.

\begin{definition}[Presence marker]\label{def:marker}
For an authoritative native store, the marker of name $n$ is an empty file $n.\mathtt{p}$ in
the store's own directory.  Let $R$ be the set of record names listed, $P$ the set of names
with a marker, both from one listing.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Markers are not records]\label{law:notrecord}
A name ending in $.\mathtt{p}$ is never a record name: the index and
\texttt{entry\_file\_name} skip it, so it is never indexed, scanned as $\Undec$, counted
against the byte budget, or counted by $\mathtt{TRANSACTION\_REPLAY\_STORE\_MAX\_RECORDS}$ or
the refusal of Law~\ref{law:refuse}.  Every bound counts $R$ only.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Write order]\label{law:writeorder}
An authoritative $\mathrm{put}(n,v)$ runs
\begin{align*}
&\mathrm{write}(n.\mathtt{tmp});\ \mathrm{sync};\ \mathrm{rename}(n.\mathtt{tmp},n);\
\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}};\\
&[\,n\notin P:\ \mathrm{create}(n.\mathtt{p});\ \mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}}\,]
\end{align*}
and returns $\Ok$ only after the last step.  An authoritative $\mathrm{rm}(n)$ runs
$\mathrm{remove}(n.\mathtt{p});\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}};\mathrm{remove}(n);
\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}}$.
\end{law}

\begin{invariant}[Marker implies record]\label{inv:marker}
In every physical state the program and a crash reach, $P\subseteq R$.
\end{invariant}
\begin{proof}
A marker is created only after the rename that makes $n\in R$ is flushed, and removed
before the record.  A crash between any two steps of Law~\ref{law:writeorder} leaves either
$P$ unchanged or the record already durable; by D the flushed prefix is what recovery sees.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Restore]\label{law:restore}
Restore classifies, from one listing:
\begin{align*}
n\in R,\ \text{decodes as its key} &\ \mapsto\ \Filed(k,v),\\
n\in R,\ \text{otherwise} &\ \mapsto\ \Undec(n),\\
n\in P\setminus R &\ \mapsto\ \Missing(n),
\end{align*}
and for $n\in R\setminus P$ creates $n.\mathtt{p}$ (flushed) before the store serves its
first operation.  The owner's $\U$ is $\{n:\Undec(n)\}\cup\{n:\Missing(n)\}$.
\end{law}

$R\setminus P$ is the crash between the rename and the marker: the record is whole and
present, so it witnesses itself, and the repair restores $P=R$.

A rolled back rename is not among them: under D a completed rename does not roll back, and
on a platform without D the marker's creation is not ordered after the rename, so both may
vanish together.  It stays a loss event of Definition~\ref{def:loss}, which
Law~\ref{law:writethrough} removes on Windows.

\begin{proposition}[W holds]\label{prop:w}
On a platform that discharges D, Laws~\ref{law:writeorder} and \ref{law:restore} discharge
Obligation~\ref{ob:witness} for every partial loss that leaves markers: a removed or renamed
record file, or a record lost by \texttt{fsck}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Complete.}  Let $n\in\dom\sigma_k$; its first $\mathrm{put}$ completed, so
$n\in P$ (Law~\ref{law:writeorder}).  If $n$ is lost and its marker is not, $n\in P\setminus
R$, reported $\Missing$.  \emph{Sound.}  $\Missing(n)$ requires $n\in P$, and a marker
is created only after a put of $n$ flushed its rename (Law~\ref{law:writeorder}), so that
record was durable.  A crash before the marker's creation leaves $n\in R\setminus P$, never
$\Missing$: a put that never reached its rename is never reported lost.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Total loss is undetectable]\label{prop:total}
No witness stored in the failure domain of the records detects $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
After $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ the physical state equals that of a store never opened; any
function of the physical state takes the same value on both.
\end{proof}

Total loss is therefore the documented reset: it is what the operator does by removing the
replay store, and its consequences are those of clearing every stream (the recovery law of
\texttt{message::replay}): a receiver may admit an unexpired transaction once more, and a
sender restarts at sequence $0$, which its destinations see as $\mathsf{Stale}$ or
$\mathsf{Fork}$ until it passes their retained high watermark.  A witness outside the domain
(a sibling file in the data directory) would detect a vanished store directory, but the only
producer of that state is the operator, whose clearing step it would then have to name; we
do not add it.

\begin{law}[Read agreement]\label{law:readagree}
An authoritative $\mathrm{get}(k)$ for $\name(k)\in P$ whose record is not readable as the
record of $k$ returns $\Err$, never $\Ok(\bot)$; $\Ok(\bot)$ means $\name(k)\notin P$.
\end{law}

This closes the two cases added to \issue{915} in review: a store whose root vanishes while
the node runs answers $\Err$ for every witnessed name (as its persist already fails), and an
upper-cased record file is never absent: its name is no record name, so it is never indexed,
and its lower-case marker keeps it in the scan.  On a case-sensitive volume it is
$\Missing$ to $\mathrm{scan}$, and so in $\U$; on a case-insensitive one the read of the
marked lower-case name opens the file, so $\mathrm{scan}$ files it with its intact state, and
$\mathrm{get}$ returns its value too.  Either way the stream keeps its guarantee: failed closed
in the first case, restored whole in the second.

\begin{assumption}[Browsers lose whole origins]\label{as:idbloss}
A browser deletes stored data per origin, never part of it~\cite{MDNEviction}; this
includes storage-pressure eviction of a non-persistent origin and WebKit's deletion of
script-written data after seven days without user interaction.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[IndexedDB needs no marker]\label{prop:idbwitness}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:idbloss} and N, an IndexedDB store discharges W with $P=\emptyset$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every IndexedDB write is a transaction, atomic by the specification~\cite{IndexedDB3}; with
N the program removes no row it was not told to.  By Assumption~\ref{as:idbloss} the
platform loses no proper subset of a database's rows, so no $\lambda_{\mathrm{part}}$ is
reachable and W holds vacuously; total loss is Proposition~\ref{prop:total}.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[A browser's crash class is claimed]\label{as:claimed}
A page cannot observe whether its IndexedDB outlives the browsing session.  An ephemeral
context (a private window, ephemeral partitioned third-party storage) opens, commits and
fires \jsq{complete} as a persistent one does, and \jsq{navigator.storage.persisted()}
reports exemption from eviction under storage pressure, not
ephemerality~\cite{MDNEviction}.  We write $\Durable^{\circ}$ for a claimed $\Durable$:
$\Durable$ in a persistent context, and $\Volatile$ with a $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ at
session end in an ephemeral one.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:idbloss} holds in an ephemeral context too: its session end loses the
whole partition, a total loss, so Proposition~\ref{prop:idbwitness} is unchanged.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:total} no witness in the domain detects that loss, so no observation at
open separates $\Durable^{\circ}$ from $\Durable$.



\textbf{Cost.}  W adds one marker creation and one directory flush to the first
$\mathrm{put}$ of each name, and nothing to later puts; restore lists one directory, as
today.  The replay store creates a name once per stream, so the cost is per new stream, not
per transition.

\textbf{Operator step.}  To clear one failed stream, stop the node and remove both
\texttt{<name>} and \texttt{<name>.p} from \texttt{transaction-replay}.  A store written
before W has $P=\emptyset$ and is repaired by Law~\ref{law:restore} at its first open: no
migration is needed.

\section{Survival per platform}\label{sec:survival}

D is a property of the platform's commit, so each platform discharges it separately.

\textbf{Unix.}  Law~\ref{law:writeorder} with
$\mathrm{sync}=\mathtt{File::sync\_all}$, which the Rust standard library maps to
$\mathtt{fcntl}(\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC})$ on Apple targets and to $\mathtt{fsync}$
elsewhere~\cite{AppleFsync}, and $\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}}$ the same on the directory.
The store's own directory entry is flushed at open, with its ancestors.  The rename-and-sync
pattern is the one crash-consistency studies find portable across unix file
systems~\cite{Pillai2014}.  \texttt{test\_durability} model-checks the step plans over every
crash point.

\textbf{Windows.}  A directory cannot be opened for flushing through the standard library,
so $\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}}$ is a no-op today and a completed rename can roll back
(\issue{915}).  The windows gateway is built and run in CI, so the target is in scope.
\begin{law}[Write-through rename]\label{law:writethrough}
On Windows every directory mutation of a $\Durable$ store is a rename by
$\mathtt{MoveFileExW}$ with
$\mathtt{MOVEFILE\_REPLACE\_EXISTING}\mid\mathtt{MOVEFILE\_WRITE\_THROUGH}$, which does not
return until the move is on disk~\cite{MoveFileEx}: a put renames its flushed temporary
file; a marker is created as a flushed temporary file renamed into place; a removal renames
its file to \texttt{.tmp} and then deletes it.
\end{law}
A removal's deletion need not be durable: a \texttt{.tmp} left by a crash is removed at the
next open (the index law of \texttt{FileStorage}), and it is not a record, so it never
reappears.

\textbf{IndexedDB.}  The specification makes the durability hint advisory~\cite{IndexedDB3}.
What each engine does, read from its source:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize\raggedright
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.17\columnwidth}p{0.42\columnwidth}p{0.31\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Engine & \jsq{default} & \jsq{strict}\\
\midrule
Chromium & relaxed since 121: complete fires once the data reaches the OS
buffer~\cite{ChromeDurability} & flush before complete\\
Gecko & SQLite WAL, synchronous \texttt{NORMAL} (\texttt{FULL} under a
pref)~\cite{GeckoIDB} & synchronous \texttt{EXTRA}\\
WebKit & SQLite WAL, \texttt{synchronous} left at the library default~\cite{WebKitIDB} &
a \texttt{FULL} WAL checkpoint after commit\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In WAL mode a commit under \texttt{synchronous=NORMAL} survives a process crash but may roll
back after a system crash~\cite{SQLiteWAL}, which is Chromium's relaxed behaviour too.  So
\jsq{default} discharges D against a process crash in all three engines, and against a
system crash in none that we can rely on.

\begin{law}[Strict commit]\label{law:strict}
Every read-write transaction of an IndexedDB store at $c=\Durable$ is opened with
$\{\mathit{durability}:\text{\jsq{strict}}\}$, and the store reports $\Ok$ only on the
transaction's \texttt{complete} event.
\end{law}

Under Law~\ref{law:strict} D holds to the strength of each engine's strongest commit.  We
claim no more: whether that commit also flushes a drive's write cache is the engine's
choice, as \texttt{F\_FULLFSYNC} is ours on native Apple targets.  \texttt{rexie}~0.4 opens
no transaction with options; the binding is
\texttt{IdbDatabase::\allowbreak transaction\_with\_\allowbreak str\_sequence\_\allowbreak
  and\_mode\_\allowbreak and\_options}, which
\texttt{web-sys} gates behind \texttt{web\_sys\_unstable\_apis}, so the implementation either
enables that cfg for the core crate or passes the options object through \texttt{js-sys}.

\begin{corollary}[Authoritative reads need no write]\label{cor:idbread}
\texttt{IdbStorage} restamps a row on every $\mathrm{get}$ to order eviction.  Under N
eviction never runs, so an authoritative $\mathrm{get}$ may be a read-only transaction.
\end{corollary}

\section{No eviction}\label{sec:noevict}

\begin{law}[Refuse, never evict]\label{law:refuse}
For $m\sqsupseteq\Retained$ on any backend: a $\mathrm{put}$ of a new name into a full store
returns $\Err(\Exhausted)$ with the store unchanged; rewriting an existing name never needs
room; an open with a capacity below the stored count fails.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Eviction is a forged absence]\label{prop:evict}
If an authoritative IndexedDB store evicts, W fails.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An evicted row $n\in\dom\sigma_k$ is gone with no marker (Proposition~\ref{prop:idbwitness}
has $P=\emptyset$), so restore reports nothing for it and its stream restarts from
$\mathsf{First}$: exactly \issue{923}.
\end{proof}

With Law~\ref{law:refuse} the browser replay store matches the native one (\#914): a new
stream past the bound fails closed, and the row capacity
$\mathtt{TRANSACTION\_REPLAY\_STORE\_MAX\_RECORDS}$ becomes a refusal bound rather than a
sizing hope.  Whether a remote peer can drive a browser node to the cap stops mattering to
safety; reaching it costs only new streams.

\section{Entry guards}\label{sec:guards}

\begin{law}[Guards are a durable cache]\label{law:guards}
The entry-guard store is opened at $(\Durable,\Rebuild)$, \texttt{DurableCache}: writes
discharge D (native: Law~\ref{law:writeorder} without markers, Law~\ref{law:writethrough};
browser: Law~\ref{law:strict}); a guard record that is absent or does not decode is retired
and the set is re-selected from scratch, logged at \texttt{warn} with the record's name.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Guards change only by reconciliation]\label{prop:guards}
Across a crash, the guard set the next selection reads is the last one written, unless a
loss event (Definition~\ref{def:loss}) removed it.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
D gives $\sigma'(n)\in\{\sigma_k(n),\sigma_{k+1}(n)\}$ for the one record per network; both
are whole sets that \texttt{reconcile\_guard\_state} itself produced.
\end{proof}

The meaning stays $\Rebuild$, which answers the policy question of \issue{911}: guards are
privacy state whose loss is a downgrade (one re-selection of the first hop), not a safety
failure, so failing onion routing closed on an undecodable record (F) would trade
availability for nothing the guard set protects.  Under D an undecodable guard record is no
longer reachable by a crash, only by a loss event or a schema change, which the operator
cleans before 1.0.  The browser fallback to an in-memory guard store when IndexedDB cannot
open is $(\Volatile,\Rebuild)$, admissible, and already logged.

\section{Relay custody}\label{sec:custody}

\subsection{Model}

A relay element $e$ carries a signed transaction of stream $s$ (origin account, this node,
class) at sequence $q$ with digest $h$; write $e=(s,q,h)$.  The actors:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item the carrier $\stored(d)$: the elements held for $d$ at $d+1$, retired only by $d$'s
covering remove up to an element's dot;
\item the receiver window $\W_s$, persisted at $(\Durable,\Auth)$, with the pure transition
$\obs(\W_s,q,h)\in\{\mathsf{First},\mathsf{Advance},\mathsf{Late},\mathsf{Replay},
\mathsf{Fork},\mathsf{Stale}\}$;
\item the application inbox, volatile; \emph{taking} a message is where core's custody
ends.
\end{itemize}
Today a drain admits $h$ into $\W_s$ and queues the message; a restart before the take
loses the queue but not $\W_s$, so the redelivery observes $\mathsf{Replay}$ and is
retired (\issue{936}).  The fact that is lost is ``$h$ was admitted for $e$ and not taken''.

\begin{definition}[Custody set]\label{def:custody}
The custody set of stream $s$ is $\K_s\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathrm{Digest}\times
\mathbb{N}$: the relay deliveries $(q,h,x)$ of $s$ admitted and not yet taken, where
$x=\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl}+\iota+\epsilon$ is the instant the transaction is spent (the
load-bounds horizon: $\epsilon$ the clock tolerance, $\iota=H_I+\epsilon$ for application
mail, $H_I$ the relay hold horizon).  Every component is a
function of the transaction, so its admission and its release name the same entry.  The
receiver record of $s$ is the pair $(\W_s,\K_s)$, one record under the existing key, so one
write commits both atomically (A on both platforms).
\end{definition}

Since a hold satisfies $\mathit{hold}\le\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl}$ and is delivered only while
$t<\mathit{hold}+H_I$, no carrier offers $e$ at any $t\ge x$, by a clock up to $\epsilon$ behind:
from $x$ on, the entry protects nothing.

Placing $\K_s$ in the receiver record, and not in the application or a separate store, is
forced by two constraints: core never consults the application (the layering law), and a
second record could not be committed atomically with $\W_s$, which would reopen the window
between admission and its custody.

\begin{law}[Custody]\label{law:custody}
Every replay transition at $t$ first expires the spent entries,
$\K_s\leftarrow\{(q,h,x)\in\K_s:t<x\}$ (written only if it changed).  A drain pass offers
each element $e=(s,q,h)$ it has not handed over in this process, with spent instant $x$,
by the first arm that applies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $(q,h,x)\in\K_s$: $\Requeue$;
\item $|\bigcup_s\K_s|=\mathtt{RELAY\_INBOX\_MAX\_LEN}$: $\Defer$;
\item $\obs(\W_s,q,h)=(\W_s',v)$ with $v$ permitting dispatch: commit
$(\W_s',\K_s\cup\{(q,h,x)\})$, then queue;
\item otherwise: $\Retire$,
\end{enumerate}
where $\Requeue$ queues the message again without touching $\W_s$, and every queueing
reserves room first ($\Defer$ when there is none, as today).  When the application takes a
message of $e$, core commits $(\W_s,\K_s\setminus\{(q,h,x)\})$, the \emph{release}; the
element is retired from the carrier only after its release committed.  The live path is
unchanged: it observes $\W_s$ alone.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Removal only by evidence]\label{law:evidence}
An entry leaves $\K_s$ only by its release, which follows a take, or by its expiry at $x$,
after which no carrier offers its element: never by its absence from a carrier read.
\end{law}

A read is one member's view, and the holder a membership change just named may report no
carrier, or an old one, until the hand-off lands.  An earlier revision restricted custody to
that read, $\K_s\leftarrow\K_s\cap\stored(d)$; then an admission, a read of such a holder, a
crash before the take and the hand-off's redelivery observe $\mathsf{Replay}$ with
$(q,h)\notin\K_s$ and retire untaken mail (\issue{1059}).  Absence is no evidence of removal,
so Law~\ref{law:evidence} admits none.  The in-process handed-over set follows the same law:
an untaken element is forgotten only at $x$, since forgetting it on a lagging read would offer
it twice; a taken one absent from a read is forgotten, its redelivery being a
$\mathsf{Replay}$ that is retired.  A stream's charge is spent at the maximum of its
admissions' instants, so $x$ is never later than its stream's, and a spent stream holds no
custody once its entries expired.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded custody]\label{lem:bound}
$|\bigcup_s\K_s|\le\mathtt{RELAY\_INBOX\_MAX\_LEN}$ ($64$), so a receiver record grows by
at most $64$ entries of a sequence, a digest and an instant over the whole store; an entry
whose element left the carrier without a take is held at most until its $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\Defer$ arm refuses a new entry at the bound, judged after the transition expired the
spent entries; an entry leaves only as Law~\ref{law:evidence} states, the latest at $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Custody stall]\label{rem:stall}
Law~\ref{law:evidence} buys at-least-once with liveness.  Call an entry \emph{lost} when its
element was admitted, not taken (the application did not take it, or a crash came first),
and its carrier is gone at all $r$ members without a take, as when a chain churns out without
a hand-off.  A lost entry stays until $x\le t_{\mathrm{adm}}+\mathit{ttl}+H_I+2\epsilon$.  With
$k$ lost entries custody admits $64-k$ others, and at $k=64$ the $\Defer$ arm refuses every new
relay delivery to the node, from every origin, until the earliest of them is spent: a stall of
at most $\max x-t$, about $\mathit{ttl}+7$\,d.  Flooding an inbox makes the first condition
cheap, not the second, so no single party forces it.  The bound frees itself at $x$; the
operator's reset (removing the replay store) ends it at once, at the cost of the replay
guarantee.  Freeing a lost entry earlier needs evidence of removal stronger than one read:
unanimous absence at the members of an agreeing chain is a candidate, but a writer resends a
hold until it is acknowledged, so absence there does not yet exclude a later arrival
(\issue{1139}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[At least once across restart]\label{prop:atleastonce}
Let the carrier satisfy $(\Durable,\Retained)$ (\issue{1021}).  In every history with any
number of crashes, an element $e=(s,q,h)$ leaves the carrier only if (i) the application
took a message of $e$, (ii) the live path would drop $h$ too, or (iii) its retention bound
elapsed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The drain retires $e$ in two arms.  After a release: the release follows a take, case (i).
In the last arm of Law~\ref{law:custody}: $(q,h)\notin\K_s$ and $\obs$ refuses.  If $\obs$
is $\mathsf{Replay}$, $h$ is in $\W_s$, so it was admitted earlier either live (dispatched
to the application, case (ii)) or by a relay delivery, which entered $\K_s$ in the same
commit and left it only by a release (case (i)) or by its expiry at $x$
(Law~\ref{law:evidence}), after which no carrier offers $e$.  $\mathsf{Fork}$ and $\mathsf{Stale}$ are refusals of the live path,
case (ii).  A crash cannot separate the admission from its custody entry, nor a release
from its effect, because each is one record write under D.  Case (iii) is the retention
law of \#913.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Duplicate window]\label{prop:dup}
The application receives a message of one element twice iff a crash falls after its take
and before its release committed, and then at most once more per such crash.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Before the release, $(q,h)\in\K_s$ survives the crash and the redelivery is a
$\Requeue$.  After it, $(q,h)\notin\K_s$ and $h\in\W_s$ or below its window, so the
redelivery is refused and retired.
\end{proof}

The duplicate carries the same transaction id, so an application that needs exactly-once
delivery deduplicates by it.  Moving the release before the take returns would make the
inbox's synchronous \texttt{try\_next} perform a store write; we keep the take synchronous
and accept the window, which is one record write long when the release is issued on the
take's consumption signal.

\subsection{Mail behind live traffic}

\issue{936} also asks that a relay message behind live traffic of the same origin be
delivered.  Inside the window it is: an empty slot gives $\mathsf{Late}$.  Below it, no
receiver can.

\begin{proposition}[No bounded receiver admits stale mail safely]\label{prop:stale}
Let $q<\rmin(\W_s)$ and $(q,h)\notin\K_s$.  No decision that depends only on bounded
receiver state both admits every such $e$ whose $h$ was never dispatched and refuses every
such $e$ whose $h$ was.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take two histories that end in the same $(\W_s,\K_s)$: in the first, $h$ arrived live,
was dispatched, and at least $\mathtt{REORDER\_WINDOW}$ later sequences followed; in the
second, $h$ was held, never delivered, and the same later sequences arrived live.  A holder
may hold a payload it forwarded (any hold inside the sender's proof lifetime is
admissible), so $e$ can be stored in both.  The window retains only the last $32$ digests,
so the states agree, and any function of them decides both alike: admitting is a replay in
the first, refusing is a loss in the second.  Separating them needs every digest admitted
within the sender's proof lifetime, which no bound on the store's size covers.
\end{proof}

So below the window the drain refuses as the live path does, which is the criterion
\issue{936} itself states (a refusal retires an element only when core would drop the same
payload arriving live).  Delivering such mail needs the sender to put it on a stream that
live traffic does not advance, a protocol change outside this law.

\section{Group commit}\label{sec:group}

One lock serializes every replay transition, and under Law~\ref{law:writeorder} or
Law~\ref{law:strict} each one paid a flush: about $55$ transitions per second on the native
store (macOS) and about $65$ in a browser (\issue{916}).  Group commit keeps the lock and
the flush, and moves the wait from the lock to the release.

\begin{definition}[Transition, batch]\label{def:batch}
Transitions $t_1<t_2<\cdots$ run in lock order on the \emph{speculative} tables $\hat T$:
$t_i(\hat T_{i-1})=(\hat T_i,\,w_i,\,r_i)$ with an optional record write
$w_i=(n_i,v_i)$, a result $r_i$, and an undo entry $u_i$ with $u_i(\hat T_i)=\hat T_{i-1}$.
Batches $B_1,B_2,\ldots$ partition the transitions into intervals of lock order.  Batch
$B_j$ is written by one $\mathrm{put}^{*}(W_j)$, where $W_j(n)$ is the last $v_i$ of $B_j$
written under $n$; the batches are written one at a time, in order, and $B_{j+1}$ gathers
every transition that runs while $B_j$ is written.  $T$ denotes $\hat T$ at the end of the
last batch whose $\mathrm{put}^{*}$ returned $\Ok$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Group commit]\label{law:group}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Release.} $r_i$ is returned only after $\mathrm{put}^{*}(W_{j(i)})=\Ok$,
whatever $r_i$ is (a refusal, or a transition that writes nothing): a result never rests on
a state that is not durable.
\item \emph{Atomicity per record.} $\mathrm{put}^{*}$ discharges A per name:
$\forall\sigma'\in R_P.\ \forall n\in\dom W_j.\ \sigma'(n)\in\{\sigma(n),W_j(n)\}$, with no
order promised across names.
\item \emph{Order.} For every name, the values $\mathrm{put}^{*}$ makes durable follow lock
order: batches are written in order, one at a time, and $W_j(n)$ is the last write to $n$
in $B_j$.
\item \emph{Fail closed.} $\mathrm{put}^{*}(W_j)=\Err$ $\Rightarrow$ every pending
transition (those of $B_j$ and of every later batch, all computed on $\hat T$ past $T$)
returns $\Err$, and $\hat T\leftarrow u_{\mathrm{first}}\circ\cdots\circ
u_{\mathrm{last}}(\hat T)=T$: no member is adopted.  A failed $\mathrm{put}^{*}$ may have
landed some of $W_j$ (atomicity is per record), so memory $\sqsubseteq$ disk, and every
member durable but undone is a lost release (Prop.~\ref{prop:lostrelease}).
\item \emph{Latency.} With $\phi$ a bound on one $\mathrm{put}^{*}$, a transition is
released at most $2\phi$ after it ran: the batch in flight, then its own.
\item \emph{Size.} $|\dom W_j|$ is at most the store's record bound, and its bytes at most
the store's byte bound, since $W_j$ holds one record per name; $|B_j|$ is the number of
transitions in flight, each held by its caller, as each was queued on the lock before.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\textbf{Native $\mathrm{put}^{*}$.}
\begin{align*}
&\forall n\in\dom W:\ \mathrm{write}(n.\mathtt{tmp});\ \mathrm{push}(n.\mathtt{tmp});\quad
\mathrm{barrier};\\
&\forall n:\ \mathrm{rename}(n.\mathtt{tmp},n);\quad \mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}};\\
&[\,\forall n\notin P:\ \mathrm{create}(n.\mathtt{p});\quad
\mathrm{sync}_{\mathrm{dir}}\,]\quad\text{(iff some $n\notin P$)}
\end{align*}
where $\mathrm{push}$ moves a file's data to the device and $\mathrm{barrier}$ makes every
pushed byte durable.  On Apple targets $\mathrm{push}=\mathtt{fsync}$ and
$\mathrm{barrier}=\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC}$ once, which flushes the whole drive
cache~\cite{AppleFsync}; elsewhere $\mathrm{push}=\mathtt{fsync}$, already durable, and
the barrier is empty.  A batch therefore pays two full flushes (three when it creates a
marker), as one $\mathrm{put}$ did.  A rename is flushed only after the barrier, so a name
never holds a torn record, and a marker is created only after its rename is flushed, so
Invariant~\ref{inv:marker} holds: A and W per name, as Law~\ref{law:writeorder}.  A store
that refuses (Law~\ref{law:refuse}) checks the whole batch against its budget before its
first write.

\textbf{IndexedDB $\mathrm{put}^{*}$.} One read-write transaction under
Law~\ref{law:strict} holding every put of $W$, after one budget check: all or nothing,
which is stronger than A per name.

\begin{proposition}[A lost release]\label{prop:lostrelease}
A member whose record landed and that was never released (a crash after some records of
$W_j$ landed and before $B_j$ was released, or a failed $\mathrm{put}^{*}$ that landed it)
is a transition that committed and whose caller dropped the result: an admission is in
$\W_s$ and not dispatched (its retransmission is a replay, and the message is lost, as for
any drop after admission); a lease extension covers sequences nobody was issued, so
$\mathit{issued}\le\hat L\le D$ holds with $D$ the last write; a custody entry names a
delivery still in the carrier, which the next pass requeues until its release or expiry, and a
landed release retires early only what the application already took (the duplicate window
of Law~\ref{law:custody}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By atomicity per record each name holds its value before $B_j$ or $W_j(n)$, and $W_j(n)$
is $\hat T$ restricted to $n$ after the last member that wrote $n$.  No member was
released (Release), so no caller acted on its result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Leases]\label{cor:grouplease}
The lease invariant of \texttt{message::replay::lease} becomes
$\mathrm{returned}\le D$: a reservation is released only after the batch holding its own
or an earlier lease extension is durable, while the speculative lease $\hat L$ may exceed
$D$ until then.  A failed batch undoes $(\mathit{issued},\hat L)$ of members none of which
was returned.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Custody]\label{cor:groupcustody}
$\W_s$ and $\K_s$ stay one record, and a batch writes one value per record, so a crash
still cannot separate an admission from its custody entry, nor a release from its effect:
Law~\ref{law:custody} holds unchanged.
\end{corollary}

\section{Link-local transactions}\label{sec:linklocal}

Every onion cell, cover included, crosses each hop as a core transaction addressed to the
next hop, and each such transaction is admitted in the receiver's durable replay window
$\W_s$, one durable write per cell per hop (\issue{1043}).  Group commit
(Law~\ref{law:group}) amortizes the flush, not the write: on the native durable store the
onion loop of \issue{843} sustains $100$\,KB/s, against $575$\,KB/s with an in-memory
replay store.  The onion layer already admits each cell at most once against its own replay
store $R_i$~\cite{OnionPipeline}, so the durable window repeats that defence on the hot
path.  This section states which transactions are admitted without $\W_s$, what replaces it, and why
nothing is lost (\issue{1081}).

\subsection{Classification}

\begin{definition}[Link-local]\label{def:linklocal}
Let $t$ be a transaction addressed to this node and delivered on connection $c$ of
generation $g$.  Then
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{ll}(t,c)\ \Leftrightarrow\ {}&\mathrm{class}(t)=\mathsf{Application}\\
\wedge\ {}&\mathrm{edge}(c,t)=\mathsf{Neighbour}\ \wedge\ \mathrm{ns}(t)\in D_r,
\end{align*}
where $\mathrm{edge}(c,t)=\mathsf{Neighbour}$ iff $c$'s handshake authenticated
$\mathrm{origin}(t)$ and $g$ is live (\issue{888}), $\mathrm{ns}(t)$ is the namespace prefix of
$t$'s application payload, and $D_r$ is the set of namespaces registered in advance, as
data, as \emph{delegating replay}.
\end{definition}

The predicate reads the envelope (class and payload prefix) and the connection, and matches
registered data: core never calls the application, exactly as for delegated admission
(\issue{935}), of which $\mathrm{ll}$ is the replay counterpart.  The relay carrier is not
signed, so ``direct'' is not read from the payload; the authenticated connection is the only
unforgeable witness, and a relayed copy of $t$ is never link-local.

\begin{obligation}[Delegated replay, R]\label{ob:delegated}
A namespace in $D_r$ acts on each of its payloads at most once, by its own replay key,
across connections, generations, envelopes and restarts of the receiver.
\end{obligation}

The onion namespace discharges R: $R_i[x]$ admits $(x,\nu)$ at most once while $x$ is live,
$\nu$ is bound to the cell and $R_i$ is shared by every link (Prop.~Replay bounds (i)--(ii)
of~\cite{OnionPipeline}); no layer outlives its filter, since $x\le\mathit{arr}+V$; and every
restart draws a fresh process epoch, under which every layer sealed for an earlier epoch is
rejected (D2, and Prop.~Replay bounds (vi)).

\subsection{Replay authority}

\begin{law}[Window by classification]\label{law:linklocal}
\[
\mathrm{window}(t,c)=
\begin{cases}
\W_{s,g}, & \mathrm{ll}(t,c),\\
\W_s, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
where $\W_{s,g}$ is the replay window of stream $s$ on generation $g$, held in memory only,
never written, and dropped when $g$ retires; $\W_s$ is the durable window.  Both observe by
the same transition $\obs$, and the origin quota is charged as before (it is runtime-local).
The sender is unchanged: its sequences come from its leased allocator, one stream per
class, whichever window each transaction meets.
\end{law}

Classification is a function of $(t,c)$, so a given delivery meets exactly one window, and
each window takes the sequences the other admitted as gaps, which $\obs$ admits as
$\mathsf{Advance}$; a transaction delivered both directly and relayed meets both, which
Prop.~\ref{prop:llloss} bounds.  A link-local transaction is never
relayed, so it never enters relay custody, and Law~\ref{law:custody} is unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[What the durable window gave a link-local transaction]
\label{prop:llloss}
Let $\mathrm{ll}(t,c)$.  Every delivery $t'$ with $\mathrm{digest}(t')=\mathrm{digest}(t)$
that $\W_s$ would refuse and Law~\ref{law:linklocal} admits is sent by $\mathrm{origin}(t)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Such a $t'$ either meets $\W_{s,g'}$ with $g'\ne g$ (a later generation, or after a receiver
restart), or is a relayed copy meeting $\W_s$, which never saw $t$.  In the first case
$\mathrm{edge}(c',t')=\mathsf{Neighbour}$, so $c'$ authenticated $\mathrm{origin}(t)$ as its
sender.  In the second, the relay holds $t$, which crossed only $c$: each link's transport is
authenticated and encrypted per connection, so $t$ is held only by its two ends, and
$\mathrm{dest}(t)$ binds it to this node; this node delivering $t$ to itself is no replay.
\end{proof}

Hence, against the four threats of \issue{1081}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Across a restart.} Only the origin can present $t$ again, and an origin can always
sign a fresh transaction with the same payload: no window ever protected a receiver from its
origin's resends.  Payload duplicates are R's, which the onion layer discharges by the
epoch.
\item \emph{From a different neighbour.} A neighbour $d\neq\mathrm{origin}(t)$ cannot present
$t$ as $\mathsf{Neighbour}$, and $\W_s$, keyed by origin, never related two neighbours'
traffic; a cell replayed across links is refused by $R_i$, which every link shares.
\item \emph{The envelope rather than the cell.} The same envelope on the same generation is
refused by $\W_{s,g}$; on another generation, or under a new envelope around the same cell,
it is refused by R.
\item \emph{What remains.} Nothing that R does not cover, so no further key rotation is
needed: the process epoch is the rotation.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Why a volatile window, not none]\label{prop:llvolatile}
$\W_{s,g}$ is not needed for R-soundness, but it keeps three properties at negligible cost:
(i) core's dispatch law, at most once per window, holds for every transaction within a
generation, so a namespace in $D_r$ never sees an envelope duplicate on one connection;
(ii) $\mathsf{Stale}$ and $\mathsf{Fork}$ of a misbehaving neighbour stay classified and
counted; (iii) it costs one in-memory $\obs$ (about $10\,\mu$s) and at most one window per
live generation and class, $\le\mathcal R$ windows, bounded by the connection registry and
released with each generation.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Restart}

\begin{proposition}[What a peer can replay into a restarted node]\label{prop:llrestart}
After a restart of the receiver, every $\W_{s,g}$ is gone and every generation has ended;
$\W_s$ persists.  A peer can deliver, as link-local, only transactions it signed itself, over
a new generation $g'$; $\W_{s,g'}$ admits them, and the onion layer refuses every cell among
them, since each was sealed for the receiver's previous process epoch (D2).  Within the new
epoch, a cell is admitted at most once per $(x,\nu)$, and no cell older than $V$ is admitted.
The replayable set is therefore empty for onion cells, and bounded by R for any namespace in
$D_r$.  A restart of the sender ends its generation the same way, and its leased allocator
never reuses a sequence; a link that flaps without a restart opens $g'$ with an empty window,
and a frame of $g$ cannot arrive on $g'$.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{The rate this buys.}  Under Law~\ref{law:linklocal} an onion cell costs no durable write at any receiver; a
sender still writes one lease per $64$ sequences (\issue{1043}).  The durable configuration
then pays what the in-memory configuration pays for onion traffic, whose measured rate is
$575$\,KB/s, still below the $1$\,MB/s of \issue{843}.  The law removes the durable cost; the
remaining gap is not attributed here and must be found and closed for the acceptance of
\issue{1081} to hold.

\section{Claimed durability}\label{sec:claimed}

A store at $(\Durable^{\circ},m)$ satisfies Law~\ref{law:admissible} read with the claim,
and violates it read with the ephemeral case.  Which reading applies is fixed by whose state
the store holds: refusing a node's own state refuses the node, while refusing state it holds
for other nodes moves that state to another holder.

\begin{law}[Own state on a claim]\label{law:ownclaim}
A store of the node's own state (replay, entry guards) may be opened at
$(\Durable^{\circ},m)$ for every $m$.  In an ephemeral context each session end is
$\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ of the store, the documented reset of Proposition~\ref{prop:total}.
\end{law}

For replay at $(\Durable^{\circ},\Auth)$ the reset is per ephemeral session: a receiver may
admit an unexpired transaction once more, which its expiry bounds, and a sender restarts its
streams at sequence $0$, which destinations see as $\mathsf{Stale}$ or $\mathsf{Fork}$ until
it passes their retained high watermark.  For entry guards it is one re-selection of the
first hop.

Relay carriers are the other case.  Most nodes are expected to be browsers
(\issue{1036}), so carriers must rest on browser holders: moving custody past every
browser (\issue{1037}) would concentrate it on the few native nodes.  One
$\Durable^{\circ}$ holder loses its carriers at an ephemeral session end, and a persistent
but closed tab holds them unreachably until it returns.  Both are holder losses, so
redundancy across holders replaces durability at one holder.

\begin{law}[Redundant custody]\label{law:redundant}
A carrier is admissible at $(\Durable^{\circ},\Retained)$ iff it is held by $r\ge2$ holders,
the first $r$ relay-capable nodes from its key's owner under the holder law of
\issue{1037}, and every repair period $T$ restores $r$ copies from any copy that survives.
A carrier at an established $(\Durable,\Retained)$ is admissible at $r=1$.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Survival across holders]\label{prop:redundant}
Under Law~\ref{law:redundant} a held element is lost only if every holder that held it is
lost (it left, or its storage was lost) within one repair period.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A repair pass that runs while one copy of an element survives restores $r$ copies.  Between
two passes the copies decrease only by holder loss, so the element is lost only if all of
its copies, at least $r$ after the last pass, are lost before the next.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:atleastonce} then holds with its premise, a carrier that survives, read
as the condition of Proposition~\ref{prop:redundant}.  Today $r=1$ for every carrier, so a
browser carrier is an inadmissible point, as relay carriers in the data store were before
\issue{1021}; \issue{777} implements the law: placement on the first $r$ relay-capable
nodes, acknowledgements (\issue{1037}) that count holders, and its composition with the
custody set $\K_s$.

\section{Replicated custody}\label{sec:replicated}

Law~\ref{law:redundant} fixes what must hold; this section fixes how, composed with the
holder law of \issue{1037}, the owner query of \issue{1045}, the acknowledgements of
\issue{1037}, and the custody set of Law~\ref{law:custody}.  Holders are lost to churn and
to ephemeral sessions; availability against Byzantine holders is not claimed, so a member's
copy is trusted as \issue{1037} trusts a relocating holder's.

Write $\mathrm{holds}(n)$ for $n$'s declaration, $\nxt(n)$ for its successor head,
$\own(k)$ for the $n$ with $k\in(n,\nxt(n)]$, and $p_j(n)$ for the $j$-th nearest node
strictly behind $n$ with $\mathrm{holds}$.

\begin{definition}[Members]\label{def:members}
$\Mem_r(k)$ is the first $r$ nodes $n$ of $\own(k),\nxt(\own(k)),\nxt^2(\own(k)),\dots$
with $\mathrm{holds}(n)$; its first, $h_0(k)$, is the holder of \issue{1037}.  The
\emph{rank} of a member is $\mathrm{rank}_k(n)=|\{m\in\Mem_r(k): m\text{ before }n\}|$, and its
\emph{next member} $\mathrm{next}_k(n)$ is the member of rank $\mathrm{rank}_k(n)+1$.  $r$ is
configurable, default $r=8$, aimed at a held message's loss below $10^{-3}$ over its 24\,h
retention with 90\% browser holders (\issue{777}); which session models it covers is stated
with the departure classes below, and it is to be recalibrated on \issue{773}'s churn
simulator.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Member range]\label{lem:range}
For $n$ with $\mathrm{holds}(n)$: $n\in\Mem_r(k)\iff k\in(\nxt(p_r(n)),\nxt(n)]$, and
$n=h_0(k)\iff k\in(\nxt(p_1(n)),\nxt(n)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$n\in\Mem_r(k)$ iff fewer than $r$ holders lie in $[\own(k),n)$, iff
$\own(k)\in(p_r(n),n]$.  The arcs owned by the nodes from $\nxt(p_r(n))$ to $n$ are
$(\nxt(p_r(n)),\nxt(n)]$.  At $r=1$ this is the holder's range of \issue{1037}.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Previous holders]\label{law:previous}
Each repair pass every node $n$ passes to $\nxt(n)$ the list
$\pi(n)=[(n,\nxt(n))]\mathbin{+\!\!+}\rho(n)[..r-1]$ if $\mathrm{holds}(n)$, and $\rho(n)$
otherwise, where $\rho(n)$ is the list its predecessor last passed it; a node takes a list
from its predecessor alone.  A list that comes round the ring to $n$ names every holder, and
$n$'s search for members then continues round the ring.
\end{law}

By the induction of \issue{1037} along each storage-less run, every holder learns
$p_1(n),\dots,p_r(n)$ with their successor heads, so Lemma~\ref{lem:range} is decided on
local state.

A member decides its rank on local state too: its list names $p_1,\dots,p_r$, so it counts
the holders in $[\own(k),n)$.  It finds $\mathrm{next}_k(n)$ in its successor list, or, when no
node of that list holds relay mail, by the owner query of \issue{1045} for the key just past
its list's last node, whose owner answers the first holder at or after it; an owner whose view
names none answers the last node it places, and the query is asked again past it.  So a member
reaches the next member however long the run between them, and the successor list stays at its
capacity of 3: with browsers holding relay mail (\issue{1036}) storage-less runs are short, and
the query covers the rest.

\begin{law}[Age gate]\label{law:agegate}
A node declares that it holds relay mail only once it has been online for $A$, at least four
stabilization periods (default 2\,min); until then it routes, and fetches its own mail from its
holder (\issue{1037}), but is no member and takes no copy.
\end{law}

Replica maintenance costs each node about $r\,B/(N\bar L)$ bytes per unit time, $B$ the held
bytes of the overlay, $N$ its nodes and $\bar L$ the mean member session~\cite{BlakeRodrigues}: at $r=8$, $10^5$ nodes
and a fifth of them receiving a full carrier of 4\,KiB elements, about 27\,KiB/s per node at
$\bar L=15$\,s against 0.7\,KiB/s at 10\,min, and a node that leaves within its first session
pays its join copy before it is of use.  The gate excludes such nodes, and it makes members
length-biased, which is the session model the default $r$ assumes.

A member caches $\mathrm{next}_k(n)$ for the range of keys whose next member it is, until its
view changes: every key of the range has the same next member, so one owner query serves them
all, and a member asks at most one per view change.  The successor capacity is revisited only if
measured runs routinely exceed it: raising it from 3 to 8 costs five more keep-alives a node,
less than routed queries per hold.

\begin{law}[Chain replication]\label{law:fanout}
A hold goes to $h_0$, under the writer authority of \issue{1037}.  $h_0$ joins the element and
forwards the hold to $\mathrm{next}_k(h_0)$, digest first: the bytes follow only when the next
member lacks the element.  A member of rank $j$ that admits it
(Law~\ref{law:admitcopy}) forwards it to its next member while $j<q(k)-1$, and the member of
rank $q(k)-1$ acknowledges it to the member it came from; every member acknowledges the
member it came from once its own next member acknowledged.  $h_0$ acknowledges the writer once
its next member did, at once when $q(k)=1$.  Here $q(k)=r$, or the number of holders when a
member's list came round the ring (Law~\ref{law:previous}) and names fewer.  The writer keeps
and resends the hold until acknowledged, as before.
\end{law}

So an acknowledgement means that every one of the $q(k)$ members holds the element: a chain
acknowledges only from its end, and no member acknowledges for another.

\begin{law}[Replica admission]\label{law:admitcopy}
$m$ admits a copy of the carrier at $k$ from $s$ iff $m\in\Mem_r(k)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:range}), $\mathrm{holds}(s)$, and $s$ is $m$'s previous member, $p_1(m)$ inside
the range, or its next member.  Admitted elements pass the witness of the carrier as before; a
copy is a join (S3).
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Chain repair]\label{law:memberrepair}
Chain order is ring order over the holders, so every carrier a member $m$ keeps has the same
chain neighbours, its previous holder $p_1(m)$ and its next holder $n_1(m)$, but at the ends of
its range (rank 0 has no previous member, rank $r-1$ no next).  A repair pass, periodic or
event-driven, sends at most one message to each of the two: the digest set of every carrier $m$
keeps whose chain includes that neighbour, the bytes following only for the carriers it lacks.
A member whose carriers an admitted copy changed offers them on to its other neighbour at once,
in the same bundled form.  A keeper that is no member offers the carrier to the member it can
name and deletes its copy only after a delete-capable acknowledgement from a member holding
$\sqsupseteq$ its state (S1$''$, S2$'$ with member for holder).
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Event-driven repair]\label{law:eventrepair}
A member that loses a node of its view, by a closed connection or a failed liveness check, runs
Law~\ref{law:memberrepair} at once, on its new view, when that node was $p_1(m)$ or $n_1(m)$: one
bundled exchange with each replacement neighbour, whatever the number of carriers.  The
periodic pass of period $T$ remains the backstop.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Repair delay]\label{prop:delay}
After the loss of one member of $k$, every member of $k$ holds a copy again within
$D\le T_d+r\,\delta$, where $T_d$ bounds the loss's detection (a connection close at once, a
liveness failure within its check) and $\delta$ one chain hop (a send and a join, and the owner
queries when the next member lies past the successor list); within $T$ when the event is
missed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The loss is detected by the lost member's chain neighbours within $T_d$, and each runs
Law~\ref{law:memberrepair} at once.  A copy that changes a member's carrier is offered on at
once, so the hole closes along the chain in at most $r$ hops.  A missed event leaves the
periodic pass.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Departure classes.}  $T_d$ depends on how a member leaves.  A \emph{graceful} departure
closes its connections, so its neighbours detect it at once: $T_d\approx0$, and
$D_g=r\,\delta$, seconds.  A \emph{silent} one (a crash, a dropped network, a suspended tab)
is detected only when a liveness probe goes unanswered; for any peer the deadline is
\texttt{PEER\_LIVENESS\_TIMEOUT\_MS} of 45\,s, but a member's chain neighbours, the only nodes
Law~\ref{law:eventrepair} needs to detect it, probe it faster (Law~\ref{law:chainliveness}).

\begin{law}[Chain liveness]\label{law:chainliveness}
A member probes $p_1(m)$ and $n_1(m)$ every $P_c=2.5$\,s, and suspects one whose probe is
$\tau_c=7.5$\,s unanswered: a suspect is no candidate member of any chain until it gives
$k=2$ on-time answers in a row (a late one starts the count again), which runs
Law~\ref{law:eventrepair} at both changes.  Suspicion evicts nothing; eviction stays at the
45\,s deadline.
\end{law}

A neighbour whose answers hover around $\tau_c$ so changes the chains at most twice per
$\tau_c+k\,P_c=12.5$\,s: the late probe takes $\tau_c$, and the $k$ on-time probes that clear it
are issued at least a period apart, the first no earlier than that late answer.  That is at most
$0.16$ bundled exchanges a second, with the temporary member's copies, from one slow link,
against $0.4$ without the count.

So a silent loss of a member is detected within $T_d\le P_c+\tau_c=10$\,s and
$D_s\approx10\,\mathrm{s}+r\,\delta$, while a slow but live member costs a chain shift and back,
never a copy.  The probes cost each node four messages per $P_c$ (two probes and two answers it
sends), about 1.3 more a second than the ordinary 15\,s probes of the same two peers, whatever
$r$.  With $\sigma$ the fraction of
departures that are silent and $\varepsilon_r(D)$ the probability that all $r$ members of a key
are lost within one window $D$, a held message's loss over its retention is bounded, to first
order, by the mixture
\[
\varepsilon\;\le\;(1-\sigma)\,\varepsilon_r(D_g)+\sigma\,\varepsilon_r(D_s),
\]
since an element's window opens with the loss of its first member, whose class sets $D$ (a
later member's class only shortens or lengthens the same window's repair).  By
the table of \issue{777}, $r=8$ meets $10^{-3}$ for Weibull ($k=0.5$) sessions of median
15\,s at $D=10$\,s, which is now $D_s$ as well as $D_g$, so for any $\sigma$; with the ordinary
45\,s deadline alone it needed $r\approx14$ for a silent fraction at that median.  A leave on
\jsq{pagehide} (\issue{1057}) moves a closing tab into the graceful class besides.  The laws hold
for every $r$; these figures only choose its default.

\begin{proposition}[Acknowledged copies survive]\label{prop:copies}
In a window where the views agree on $\Mem_r$ (S4), an acknowledged element has $q(k)$ copies,
and is lost only if every member holding it is lost within $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Law~\ref{law:fanout} gives $q(k)$ copies at the acknowledgement.  Copies decrease only by a
member's loss or by a deletion of Law~\ref{law:memberrepair}, made by a non-member after a
member holds its state, which leaves the members' copies as they were.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:delay} a loss is repaired from any surviving copy within $D$, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:redundant} with $D$ for the repair period.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Outside S4.}  An acknowledgement taken while views disagree still means $q(k)$ nodes
hold the element, each of which admitted it as a member of $k$ by its own view; it does not
mean they are the members the views will agree on.  No copy is deleted without a member's
acknowledgement (S1$''$), so their number does not fall but by node loss, and once the views
agree Law~\ref{law:memberrepair} moves the copies to the members.  Until then the bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:copies} holds with $D$ extended by the convergence of
Proposition~\ref{prop:converge}.

\begin{proposition}[Convergence]\label{prop:converge}
After one membership change the lists of Law~\ref{law:previous} settle within $r(L+1)$
passes, $L$ the longest storage-less run.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A list moves one hop per pass, and a change reaches the $r$-th holder after it through at
most $r$ holders and the runs before each, $r(L+1)$ hops; at $r=1$ this is the bound of
\issue{1037}.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Removal, fetch, owner.}  The recipient removes at the member it fetched from, and
tombstones spread by Law~\ref{law:memberrepair}.  Tombstones stay capped at
\texttt{RELAY\_INBOX\_MAX\_LEN}, so a lagging member can resurrect an element whose tombstone
fell off the cap; its redelivery is refused by the recipient's window ($\mathsf{Replay}$, or
$\mathsf{Stale}$ below it) and retired, case (ii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:atleastonce}, except
after a replay reset (Law~\ref{law:ownclaim}), where its retention bounds it.  A recipient
without a relay store fetches from $h_0$ and then down the chain; an element is the same
holder-signed bytes at every member, so the drain's handed-over set and $\K_s$ deduplicate
copies, and Law~\ref{law:custody} is unchanged.  No report restricts custody
(Law~\ref{law:evidence}): a lagging member's copy, or a new holder's before its hand-off, may
miss an element that still exists (\issue{1059}).

\textbf{Store capacity.}  A node keeps about $r$ times the carriers it kept as a sole holder,
so the relay store's budget scales with $r$: a browser's from 1024 rows to $1024\,r$, a native
node's from 64\,MiB to $64\,r$\,MiB, a cap on a store that evicts nothing, not space it takes.
A member whose store is full still refuses a copy (N): it does not acknowledge, so the chain
acknowledges no writer, which resends until the sender proof expires and then drops the hold
with a warning, as a full store's refusal did before.  So exhaustion never lowers $q(k)$; it
refuses new mail.  Its blast radius is the keys whose chain contains the full member, those
whose owner lies in $(p_r(m),m]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:range}): about $r$ holders' arcs refuse new
mail while it is full, and every other key is unaffected.  Isolating one origin's load from
another's within a store is the per-origin share of \issue{907} and \issue{1060}, not this
law's.

\begin{proposition}[Message budget]\label{prop:budget}
Per node and per repair pass: one list of Law~\ref{law:previous} and at most two bundled repair
messages, so at most three, whatever the carriers it keeps; plus $2r$ for each hold it relays,
and one bundled exchange per change of $p_1$ or $n_1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Laws~\ref{law:previous}, \ref{law:memberrepair} and~\ref{law:eventrepair} as stated; a hold is
one send to $h_0$, $r-1$ forwards, $r-1$ chain acknowledgements and one to the writer, and the
owner queries of its hops are served by the cached next members.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Costs.}  A hold costs $r-1$ forwarded copies and $r-1$ acknowledgements, and the
writer's acknowledgement waits for the chain's round trip, about $2(q-1)\,\delta\le2r\,\delta$.
A member that leaves mid-chain stalls the acknowledgement until its loss is detected and the
chain repaired, within $D+2r\,\delta$ of its departure ($D$ by its class); the writer keeps and
resends the hold meanwhile, so the stall delays the acknowledgement and loses nothing.  A pass offers each carrier's
digest set to two chain neighbours in one message each and copies only on a difference.  A
list of
Law~\ref{law:previous} carries $r$ pairs.  A best-effort leave on departure (\issue{1057})
shortens $T_d$ and is never needed for this section's laws.
