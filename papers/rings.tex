\begin{abstract}
Rings is an implemented algebraic structured peer-to-peer network for decentralized identity, resource ownership, transport, storage, privacy, and application protocols. Application servers, DNS authorities, and relay providers in conventional Internet deployments frequently become trusted bottlenecks for naming, routing, and user state. Rings instead models identities and resources as algebraic objects over a CorrectChord circular overlay $\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}$, pairing browser WebAssembly and native runtimes over WebRTC/SCTP while keeping all cryptographic operations on carriers disjoint from the routing identifier ring. This paper unifies the core algebraic design with six production subsystems implemented in Rings~1.0: range-proved finger convergence, non-transitive WebRTC Chord routing and single-hop bridge delegation, conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs) with HLC removal stability and Merkle data provenance, crash-durable local stores and age-gated replicated relay custody, cleanest-first slot accounting across shared node tables, and Sphinx/AEZ-v5 onion Kleisli pipelines bridging counterparty-signed service receipts to DRanking~2.0. We evaluate the Rust implementation empirically on real hardware and summarize exhaustive Stateright/BFS verification across $9,496,521$ reachable finite states ($9.50\times 10^6$) and 54 refuted safety mutants. Core object: $\mathsf{Rings}=(R,\mathcal{R}_N,\mathbf{Set}/R,\Omega_N,\mathsf{T}^{\mathcal{E}}_A,\Pi,\mathbb{G},\mathcal{X})$ with $R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}$, $\Omega_N(X,H)=(X,\operatorname{owner}_N\circ H)$, and $\mathsf{CryptoCarrier}\cap R=\varnothing$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Centralized application platforms simplify deployment, but they also concentrate
identity, naming, storage, and traffic control at service operators.  Cloud and
platform centralization therefore becomes a privacy, availability, and autonomy
risk for users who need direct communication and durable control over their
data~\cite{Chord,Kademlia,TorNetwork,IPFS}.  The Rings project is motivated by
this observation: a sovereign network should let a participant own an identity,
establish a session, route to another identity or resource, and run application
protocols without turning a single application server into the root of
authority.

Rings is implemented as a network, but this paper is organized around the
algebraic structure that makes the implementation coherent.  It states the
ideal carrier, ownership, transition, durability, admission, and privacy laws
that the Rings 1.0 implementation realizes.  The older intuition was that
Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) directly form a finite ring suitable for all
routing and cryptographic operations.  The refined model is more precise: Chord
identifiers form an additive circular carrier for placement, interval order,
replica selection, and finger targets; threshold schemes, signatures,
encryption, wide-block onion encipherment, and zero-knowledge protocols use
their own fields or groups.

The resulting design has three practical consequences.  First, DIDs and virtual
DIDs (VIDs) share the same 160-bit placement surface, so identities, mailboxes,
service descriptors, chunks, and application rendezvous points can be
routed by one owner function.  Second, Rings builds on Chord and CorrectChord
rather than replacing the overlay literature: successor lists, multi-predecessor
relations, join, rectify, stabilize, range-proved finger convergence, and
passive 1-hop gap-bridge delegation are the maintenance substrate on which
Rings semantics are layered~\cite{Chord,Stoica2003,ZaveChordCorrect,Freedman2005}.
Third, state transitions return typed effects instead of directly performing
network IO, which allows the overlay, storage, durability, load-accounting, and
onion-pipeline state machines to be reasoned about purely, model-checked with
Stateright~\cite{Stateright}, and then interpreted by browser or native runtimes.

\section{Problem Statement and Design Goals}

Rings addresses a narrow but recurring gap between existing decentralized
systems.  Kademlia-derived systems provide scalable lookup, and contemporary frameworks such as Veilid~\cite{Veilid2023} add Rust/WebAssembly node runtimes and private safety routes over Kademlia, yet the XOR metric
does not by itself give Rings the circular ownership law needed for successor
lists, replicated ownership, and interval-based placement.  Relay networks and
mixnets improve metadata resistance, but they are not structured P2P ownership
systems.  Blockchains provide global ordering, but embedding consensus in every
message or storage operation would turn Rings into a ledger rather than an
algebraic structured peer-to-peer substrate.

The problem is therefore to specify a network object
\[
  \mathsf{Rings}
\]
that simultaneously supports:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item self-certifying identity and delegated sessions;
\item structured lookup, range-proved finger convergence, and non-transitive WebRTC routing;
\item browser-native and native transport and crash-durable storage surfaces;
\item CRDT storage with tombstone stability, per-writer cryptographic provenance, and replicated relay custody;
\item Sybil-resistant slot accounting, credit reclaim, and bounded load under free identities;
\item Sphinx/AEZ-v5 onion Kleisli pipelines and counterparty-signed service receipts.
\end{enumerate}

This paper makes the following contributions.  It presents Rings as an
algebraic structured peer-to-peer network, modeling the identifier space as the
additive cyclic group \(R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}\) and lifting resource
placement into \(\mathbf{Set}/R\).  It specifies range-proved finger
convergence and bounded multi-predecessor non-transitive Chord routing over
restricted WebRTC graphs (Section~\ref{sec:chord}).  It establishes the
removal-stability law, HLC data provenance, factored carrier eviction, and
signed lease heartbeats for replicated CRDT storage (Section~\ref{sec:storage}).
It defines the crash-meaning authority lattice $C\times M$, presence markers,
per-platform crash durability, group commit, link-local replay windows, and
age-gated replicated relay custody (Section~\ref{sec:durable}).  It proves
fair slot accounting across five shared node tables, credit reclaim, and
single-charge chunk reassembly under free identities (Section~\ref{sec:load}).
It replaces legacy relay framing with client-sealed Sphinx/AEZ-v5 onion Kleisli
pipelines (Section~\ref{sec:privacy}), bridges 1.0 service receipts into the
companion DRanking 2.0 protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026} (Section~\ref{sec:trust}),
and presents empirical micro- and macro-benchmarks together with exhaustive
Stateright formal-verification metrics across all Rings 1.0 subsystems
(Section~\ref{sec:verification}).

\section{Algebraic System Model, Threat Model, and Effects}\label{sec:algebraic-model}

\subsection{Carrier, System Scope, and Threat Model}

\begin{assumption}[System scope]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathsf{fault}&=&\mathsf{fail\mbox{-}stop}\ (\text{overlay}) \mid \mathsf{crash}\ (\text{local}),\\
\mathsf{auth}(m,\sigma,k)&=&V(k,\sigma,m),\\
\mathsf{net}&=&\mathsf{async},\\
\mathsf{clock}&=&|\operatorname{clock}_p-\operatorname{clock}_q|\le\sigma,\\
\mathsf{io}&=&I_X:A_X^*\to\mathsf{IO},\\
\mathsf{consensus}&\notin&\mathsf{Overlay},\\
\mathsf{order}&=&\mathsf{SidecarWitness},\\
\mathsf{crypto\_ops}&\cap&\mathsf{route\_ops}=\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
Clock tolerance $\sigma$ bounds skew across synchronized honest nodes.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph*{Unified Threat Model}
Rings 1.0 operates under four explicit adversarial and fault assumptions across its layers while deliberately scoping out global traffic analysis without cover or majority ring capture:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Link and Network Adversary:} A passive observer monitors arbitrary underlay links and WebRTC NAT paths, while an active adversary delays, duplicates, reorders, drops, or replays valid signed control/onion messages on any link it controls. Asynchronous replay and credit exhaustion are defeated at every node boundary by durable/link-local replay windows, generation-tagged credit reclaim, and sliced-Bloom filters.
\item \emph{Colluding Onion Relays:} Up to $H-1$ relays on an $H$-hop client loop may be honest-but-curious or actively tag, shrink, or relabel packets without breaking payload confidentiality on any segment whose endpoints are honest. For sender--destination unlinkability, header/payload cryptographic unlinkability on the entry segment requires at least one honest relay in $\{r_{0,1},r_{0,2}\}$ separating $\diamond$ from $h_1$, while resistance to deterministic inter-hop timing correlation across $r_{0,2}$ additionally assumes non-collusion between guard $g=r_{0,1}$ and symbol hop $h_1$ unless cover pacing masks per-hop release delays $L(f_i)$; Sphinx header MACs, uniform $205\,\mathrm{B}$ layers, and AEZ~v5 wide-block encryption (Section~\ref{sec:privacy}) prevent bitwise correlation and detect single-attempt tagging with probability $\ge 1-2^{-128}$.
\item \emph{Byzantine Writers and Sybil Rotation:} Since minting a DID has zero cost ($\mathsf{cost}(\mathsf{did})=0$), an adversary may forge unsigned messages, spawn arbitrarily many DIDs, rotate identities rapidly, or inject stale DHT deltas. Unauthenticated mutations and skewed HLC timestamps are rejected by \code{DataProvenance}; memory/table exhaustion is bounded by cleanest-first connection slot floors (Section~\ref{sec:load}); and Sybil endorsement inflation is bounded by dual-signed \code{ServiceReceipt}s in DRanking~2.0 (Section~\ref{sec:trust}).
\item \emph{Local Storage Crashes:} Native file systems and browser IndexedDB backends may crash at arbitrary points between write, rename, and barrier sync; \code{RecordAuthority} ($C\times M$) enforces platform-native barriers (\code{F\_FULLFSYNC}, \code{MOVEFILE\_WRITE\_THROUGH}, \code{fsync}, or strict IndexedDB) and witnessed presence markers (Section~\ref{sec:durable}).
\item \emph{Out of Scope:} Rings 1.0 does not prevent end-to-end timing correlation by a global passive adversary observing both a client's guard link and its world exit when real-time interactive latency prohibits heavy cover padding, does not defend against $>50\%$ Sybil capture of the global overlay without DRanking~2.0 admission filtering, and trusts local client execution hosts/kernels.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Identifier carrier]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
M&=&2^{160},\\
R&=&\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z},\\
\delta_a(x)&=&(x-a)\bmod M,\\
(a,b]_R&=&\{x\in R:0<\delta_a(x)\leq\delta_a(b)\},\\
\mathsf{RouteOps}_R&=&\{0,+,-,\delta_a,(\_,\_]_R\},\\
\mathsf{Mult}_R&\notin&\mathsf{RouteOps}_R.
\end{array}
\]
Note that $(a,a]_R=\varnothing$ since $\delta_a(a)=0$, so self-ownership is handled prior to interval tests.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Live topology]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  N&\subset&R,\quad |N|\ge 2,\\[2pt]
  \operatorname{owner}_N(k)
    &=&\arg\min_{u\in N}\delta_k(u),\\[6pt]
  \operatorname{pred}_N(n)
    &=&\arg\min_{p\in N\setminus\{n\}}\delta_p(n)\\[2pt]
    &=&\arg\max_{p\in N\setminus\{n\}}\delta_n(p),\\[6pt]
  \operatorname{Succ}^q_N(n)
    &=&
    \operatorname{take}_q
    \bigl(\operatorname{sort}_{\delta_n}(N\setminus\{n\})\bigr).
\end{array}
\]
Each live node maps to a unique identifier in $N\subset R$ (hence $\operatorname{owner}_N(k)$ and $\operatorname{pred}_N(n)$ have unique minimizers); bootstrap singletons ($|N|=1$) resolve $\operatorname{owner}_N(k)=n$ trivially.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Correct-Chord stable base]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
r&=&|\operatorname{Succ}^r_N(n)|,\\
\mathsf{Steady}(N)&\Rightarrow&|N|\geq r+1,\\
|N|<r+1&\Rightarrow&\mathsf{Init}(N),\\
\mathsf{Maintain}&=&
  \{\mathsf{join},\mathsf{rectify}\}\\
&&\cup\{\mathsf{stabilize}\}_{CorrectChord}.
\end{array}
\]
This is the CorrectChord stable base of Zave~\cite{Zave2015,ZaveChordCorrect}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Affine replica set]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\rho&\in&\mathbb{N}_{>0},\\
\operatorname{rot}^{\rho}_i(k)
  &=&k+\left\lfloor M\cdot i/\rho\right\rfloor,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Rep}^{\rho}_N(k)
  &=&
  \{\operatorname{owner}_N(\operatorname{rot}^{\rho}_i(k)):
    0\leq i<\rho\}.
\end{array}
\]
Storage redundancy uses affine rotations of the entry key ($\rho=6$ by default
for data topics).  Successor lists remain the CorrectChord safety root for
topology maintenance.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Rings overlay object]
\[
  \mathcal{R}_N=
  (R,N,\operatorname{owner}_N,\operatorname{pred}_N,
   \operatorname{Succ}^r_N,\operatorname{Rep}^{\rho}_N,F,E),
\]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
F&\subseteq&N\times\mathbb{N}\times N,\\
E&=&\prod_{v\in VID}E_v,\\
\mathsf{SafetyRoot}(\mathcal{R}_N)&=&
  (\operatorname{pred}_N,\operatorname{Succ}^r_N),\\
\mathsf{PerfWitness}(\mathcal{R}_N)&=&F.
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Rotation equivariance]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  t\in R,\quad N+t=\{n+t:n\in N\}
  &\Rightarrow\\
  \operatorname{owner}_{N+t}(k+t)=\operatorname{owner}_N(k)+t.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \forall n\in N.\quad
  \delta_{k+t}(n+t)
  &=((n+t)-(k+t))\bmod M\\
  &=\delta_k(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{constraint}[Carrier separation]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathsf{PlacementOps}&=&
  \{0,+,-,\delta_a,(\_,\_]_R\}\\
&&\cup\{\operatorname{owner}_N,\operatorname{Rep}^\rho_N\},\\
\mathsf{CryptoOps}&=&
  \{\times,^{-1},\cdot_{scalar},\mathsf{interp}\}\\
&&\cup\{\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{subgroup},\mathsf{AEZ}\},\\
\mathsf{PlacementOps}&\cap&\mathsf{CryptoOps}=\varnothing,\\
\mathsf{CryptoCarrier}&\in&\{\mathbb{F}_q,\mathbb{G},\mathbb{Z}_q,\{0,1\}^*\}.
\end{array}
\]
\end{constraint}

\begin{obligation}[Runtime confinement]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\tau&:&S\times I\to \mathcal{E}+(S\times O\times A_X^*),\\
\operatorname{set}(\pi_A(\tau(s,i)))&\subseteq&\mathsf{cap},\\

\subsection{Category and Effect Monad}

We summarize namespace placement and pure effect emission using lightweight slice-category and writer-monad notation as a uniform interface vocabulary for the pure transition reducers verified in Section~\ref{sec:verification}, rather than relying on deep categorical machinery in later protocol proofs.

\begin{definition}[Namespace object]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\nu&\in&\{D,V,M,H,U,Q,W\},\\
H_\nu&:&X_\nu\to R,\\
(X_\nu,H_\nu)&\in&\mathbf{Set}/R,
\end{array}
\]

\begin{remark}[Static-Ring vs.\ Bounded-Churn Lookup Scope]
Invariant~\ref{inv:ring-fixpoint} guarantees exact owner resolution and $O(\log |V|)$-hop lookup~\cite{Chord,Stoica2003,ZaveChordCorrect} on a steady, finger-converged ring $\mathsf{Steady}(N)$. Under transient churn or before finger convergence completes, some fingers remain sparse or stale; payload delivery then preserves loop-free correctness within $\le 2|V|$ hops via bridge-delegated delivery (Theorem~\ref{theo:bridge-safety}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Range-Proved Finger Convergence}\label{sec:finger-convergence}

Classical Chord refreshes one random finger slot per stabilization period,
requiring $\Theta(m)$ periods ($m=160$) to converge and failing to distinguish
unverified neighbor hints from proved entries~\cite{Stoica2003}.  Because
Rings nodes include browsers whose WebRTC handshakes take seconds to minutes or
fail, \code{rings-core} (\code{dht::finger::convergence}) specifies finger maintenance as
a pure transition $\mathrm{step}:(S,\mathrm{Event})\to(S,\mathrm{Actions})$
built on the Chord Range Lemma and four operational disciplines.

\begin{lemma}[Chord Range Lemma~\cite{Chord}]\label{lem:range}
With half-open interval $[x,y)_R=\{z\in R:0\le\delta_x(z)<\delta_x(y)\}$ ($[x,x)_R=\varnothing$), for any nonempty live membership view $V\subseteq R$ and target $x\in R$,
\[
  \operatorname{succ}_V(x)=y \iff y\in V \;\land\; [x,y)_R\cap V=\varnothing.
\]
Specifically, for node $n\in V$ and slot $i\in[0,L)$ ($L\le 160$) with target $t_i=n+2^i$, let $s=\operatorname{succ}_V(t_i)$ and $d=\delta_n(s)$. If $s=n$, then $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=n$ for all $j\in[i,L)$; otherwise $2^i\le d$ and
\[
  \forall j\ge i:\quad 2^j\le d \implies \operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=s.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By definition, $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_i)=s$ implies $s\in V$ and $[t_i,s)_R\cap V=\varnothing$. If $s=n$, every $t_j$ with $i\le j<L$ lies in $[t_i,n)_R$ (since $2^i\le 2^j < 2^{160}$), so $[t_j,n)_R\cap V=\varnothing$ and $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=n$. Otherwise $2^i\le d$, and for every $j\ge i$ with $2^j\le d$ we have $t_j\in[t_i,s]_R$, so $[t_j,s)_R\subseteq[t_i,s)_R$ is empty in $V$ and $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Range proof]\label{def:range-proof}
A lookup for slot $i$ answered by $s$ yields a range proof
$\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\operatorname{end})$, where $\mathrm{id}$ is the
lookup UUID~\cite{RFC9562}, $e_\pi$ is the evidence epoch at issue, and
\[
\operatorname{end}(i,s)=
\begin{cases}
L-1, & s=n,\\
\min\bigl(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(s)\rfloor\bigr), & 2^i\le\delta_n(s),\\
\mathsf{Invalid}, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Proof $\pi$ covers the contiguous slot interval $[i,\operatorname{end}]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Lookups per pass]\label{cor:lookups}
If the true successor values partition the $L$ slots into $m_{\mathrm{runs}}$ maximal runs of
equal successor, a complete convergence pass requires at most $m_{\mathrm{runs}}\le\min(L,|V|)$
routed lookups; under uniform identifier placement, $\mathbb{E}[m_{\mathrm{runs}}]=O(\log |V|)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each answered lookup for slot $i$ yields $\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\operatorname{end})$ covering the maximal run $[i,\operatorname{end}]$ of identical successor $s$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}, advancing cursor $c\gets\operatorname{end}+1$ past the entire run. Because there are at most $L$ slots and each distinct successor begins at most one maximal run before wrapping past $n$, $m_{\mathrm{runs}}\le\min(L,|V|)$. Under uniform random placement of $|V|$ DIDs on $R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}$, expected inter-node spacing is $2^{160}/|V|$, so slots with $2^j\lesssim 2^{160}/|V|$ share $\operatorname{head}(n)$ while only the top $O(\log |V|)$ slots span distinct sub-intervals, giving $\mathbb{E}[m_{\mathrm{runs}}]=O(\log |V|)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local Range Confirmation]\label{lem:local-range}
Let $h=\operatorname{head}(n)$ be the first clockwise successor of $n$.  When
$n$ receives an authenticated stabilization report from $h$ echoing $n$'s
active stabilization token and stating $\operatorname{pred}(h)=n$, then
\[
  \forall j:\quad 2^j\le\delta_n(h) \implies \operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=h.
\]
Applying local confirmation sets $\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\top$ for all
$j\le\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor)$ with zero routed network lookups; if an
active attempt's slot lies in that range, it is superseded via
$\textsc{Progress}(\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because $h$ is $n$'s live successor head and $h$ itself attests on $n$'s fresh stabilization correlation token that $\operatorname{pred}(h)=n$, the half-open arc $(n,h)_R$ contains no member of $V$ observable between $n$ and $h$. For every finger index $j\ge 0$ with $2^j\le\delta_n(h)$, the finger target $t_j=n+2^j$ lies in $(n,h]_R$, so $[t_j,h)_R\subseteq(n,h)_R$ is empty in $V$ and $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=h$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}. Setting $\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\top$ for $0\le j\le\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor)$ therefore proves the prefix interval using only the existing stabilization RPC ($\textsc{Progress}$ avoids issuing any \code{FindSuccessor} lookup for those slots).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Monotone Evidence Epochs]\label{def:evidence-epochs}
Each node $n$ maintains an evidence state $(e_n,(\mathsf{chg}_j,\mathsf{ver}_j)_{j<L})$,
where $e_n\in\mathbb{N}$ is the node's monotone topology epoch~\cite{Fidge1988},
$\mathsf{chg}_j\in\mathbb{N}$ is the epoch at which hint $\mathsf{hint}_j$ last
changed, and $\mathsf{ver}_j\in\{\bot,\top\}$ records whether slot $j$ holds a
range-proved peer.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Hint change, slot-wise admission, and discontinuity]\label{law:evidence-epochs}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Hint change:} For any unverified topology/hint transition $H\to H'$ with changed
slots $C=\{j:H_j\neq H'_j\}\neq\varnothing$, advance $e\gets e+1$ and set
$(\mathsf{chg}_j,\mathsf{ver}_j)\gets(e,\bot)$ for all $j\in C$.
\item \emph{Slot-wise admission:} Proof $\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\operatorname{end})$
admits slot set $A(\pi)=\{j\in[i,\operatorname{end}]:\mathsf{chg}_j\le e_\pi\}$.
If $A(\pi)=\varnothing$, $\pi$ is rejected as $\mathsf{Stale}$; otherwise, for
every $j\in A(\pi)$, $\mathsf{hint}_j\gets(s=n\ ?\ \bot : s)$ and
$\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\top$.
\item \emph{Discontinuity:} Losing the last successor increments $e\gets e+1$,
clears $\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\bot$ for all $j$, and returns the attempt owner to
$\mathsf{Idle}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Single-Owner Attempt State]\label{def:attempt-owner}
At most one finger lookup attempt $\alpha$ exists at node $n$:
\[
  \alpha \in \mathsf{Idle}
  \ \mid\ \mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)
  \ \mid\ \mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t),
\]
where $\mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)\equiv\mathsf{AwR}((u,p),e_\pi,\tau_r)$ awaits a
lookup report for slot $p$ and request UUID $u$ issued at evidence epoch $e_\pi$ before monotonic deadline
$t=\tau_r=\mathrm{issued}+T_r$ ($T_r=10\,\mathrm{s}$), and
$\mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t)\equiv\mathsf{AwA}(\pi,\tau_a)$ awaits WebRTC transport
admission of proved peer $y$ at epoch $e$ before lease deadline
$t=\tau_a=\mathrm{deferred}+T_a$ ($T_h=180\,\mathrm{s}$, $T_a=T_h+30\,\mathrm{s}=210\,\mathrm{s}$).
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Attempt consumption and outcomes]\label{law:attempt-outcomes}
A report $(u',p',y')$ consumes $\alpha$ iff either $\alpha=\mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)\land(u',p')=(u,p)$ or $\alpha=\mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t)\land(u',p',y')=(u,p,y)$; all other reports are dropped as $\mathsf{Stale}$ without mutating state or failure streak $k\in[0,255]$. With cursor $c\in\mathbb{N}$ and retry cooldown deadline $\nu\in\mathbb{N}_\bot$ (an attempt may start from $\mathsf{Idle}$ only when $\nu=\bot$ or $\mathrm{now}\ge\nu$), consuming reports and timeout expirations ($\mathrm{now}\ge\tau_r$ or $\mathrm{now}\ge\tau_a$) transition $(\alpha,c,k,\nu)$ via~\cite{Rhea2004}:
\begin{align*}
\textsc{Fail}(p)&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{run}(p)+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1)),\\
\textsc{Retire}(\operatorname{end})&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{end}+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1)),\\
\textsc{Progress}(\operatorname{end})&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{end}+1,\ 0,\ \bot),
\end{align*}
where $\textsc{Fail}(p)$ handles expired or empty lookup attempts ($\mathsf{AwR}$), $\textsc{Retire}(\operatorname{end})$ handles admission refusal/expiry ($\mathsf{AwA}$), $\textsc{Progress}(\operatorname{end})$ records admitted range confirmation, $\operatorname{run}(p)=\max\{j\in[p,L):\forall l\in[p,j],\,\mathsf{hint}_l=\mathsf{hint}_p\}$, and $f(k)=\min(2^{\min(k,6)}\,\mathrm{s},\,60\,\mathrm{s})$ with minimum issue spacing $T_{\min}=1\,\mathrm{s}$. (Under Law~\ref{law:evidence-epochs}(3), losing the last successor clears $\alpha\gets\mathsf{Idle}$ and preserves monotonic backoff pacing until a head is restored.)
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Cyclic Slot Selection (\code{next\_fix\_slot})]\label{def:next-fix-slot}
With $h=\operatorname{head}(n)$ and first routable slot $r=\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor+1$ ($r=0$ if $h=\bot$), let $c\in\mathbb{N}$ be the cyclic cursor, $A=\{j\ge\max(c,r):\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\}$, and $B=\{j\in[r,\max(c,r)):\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\}$.  The selector is
\[
  \operatorname{next\_fix\_slot}(c)=\operatorname{sel}(c)=
  \begin{cases}
  \min A, & A\neq\varnothing,\\
  \min B, & A=\varnothing\land B\neq\varnothing,\\
  \bot, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Starvation freedom]\label{prop:starve}
Let $\mathcal{U}$ be the set of maximal contiguous equal-hint runs (or unretired ranges) among routable slots remaining unverified across attempts.  Between any two selections of a run/slot in $\mathcal{U}$, every other run in $\mathcal{U}$ is
selected at least once, and periodic revalidation reopens proved ranges whenever
$\alpha=\mathsf{Idle}\land(\neg\exists j\ge r:\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\ \lor\ k>0)$. (Restricting $\operatorname{run}(p)$ to populated hints $\mathsf{hint}_p\neq\bot$, as opposed to skipping contiguous $\bot$ entries before initial population, tightens selection from maximal $\bot$-runs to individual uninitialized slots.)
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every completed or failed attempt at slot $j$ advances $c$ strictly past $j$
($\operatorname{end}+1>j$ or $\operatorname{run}(j)+1>j$).  Because
$\operatorname{sel}(c)$ scans cyclically from $c$, it visits $U$ in strict
cyclic index order, preventing a single unreachable NAT peer from monopolizing
the single attempt owner.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Report Claims (\code{NotifyPredecessor} / \code{ReportFloor})]\label{law:report-claims}
Stabilization and successor-list synchronization reports are correlated by
two-phase claim tokens $\mathsf{tok}=(h,\mathrm{id},\phi)$ with
$\phi\in\{\mathsf{Req},\mathsf{Proc}\}$.  A report claims its token atomically
by moving $\mathsf{Req}\to\mathsf{Proc}$, dials at most $K+1$ distinct
candidates for stabilization (and at most $K$ for successor sync) re-checking
$\phi=\mathsf{Proc}$ before each dial, and clears $\mathsf{tok}$ on completion
or on any $\mathsf{SuccessorHeadChanged}$ transition (\code{ReportFloor}).
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Extended Topology Carrier and Multi-Predecessors]\label{def:multi-preds}

\begin{aligned}
  S_n=\bigl(&n,\,\texttt{succ}_n,\,\texttt{pred}_n,\,\operatorname{Preds}_K(n),\\
  &\texttt{fingers}_n,\,\texttt{bridged}_n,\,\Phi_n,\,\Pi_n\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)=\langle p_1,\dots,p_m\rangle$ ($m\le K$, supporting capacity $K=4$) is ordered by descending clockwise distance $\delta_n(p_1)>\dots>\delta_n(p_m)$ (ascending counter-clockwise distance $\delta_{p_i}(n)$), with primary predecessor $\texttt{pred}_n=p_1$, $\Phi_n$ denoting the finger-convergence evidence/attempt state (Definitions~\ref{def:evidence-epochs}--\ref{def:next-fix-slot}), and $\Pi_n$ denoting the report-claim and dial-backoff state (Laws~\ref{law:report-claims}--\ref{law:rectify-backoff}).  All referenced peers satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)
  &=\bigl(\operatorname{set}(\texttt{succ}_n)\cup\operatorname{set}(\operatorname{Preds}_K(n))\\
  &\qquad\cup\operatorname{ran}(\texttt{fingers}_n)\bigr)\setminus\{n\}\\
  &\subseteq\operatorname{Admitted}(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Multi-Predecessor Rectification and Candidate Dial Backoff]\label{law:rectify-backoff}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Empty-confirmed commit:} When $r=\operatorname{head}(n)$ remains
routable ($\operatorname{Routable}(n,r)$), $n$ commits $\texttt{Stabilize}(r)$
and emits $\texttt{Notify}(r)$ even if confirmation filtering leaves
$\texttt{confirmed}$ empty.
\item \emph{Candidate dial backoff (\code{DialBackoff}):} Failed or expired
candidate handshakes enter a bounded negative cache $\mathcal{B}_n$ ($B_{\max}=64$)
with exponential cooldown $\beta(f)=\min(\beta_0\cdot 2^{\min(f-1,4)},\allowbreak\,\beta_{\max})$
(base $\beta_0=5\,\mathrm{s}$, cap $\beta_{\max}=60\,\mathrm{s}$, configurable to $30$--$300\,\mathrm{s}$), suppressing redundant SDP offers while clearing $\mathcal{B}_n(c)$ immediately on explicit connects or admission.
\item \emph{Supersede-on-Closer, Retain-on-Sub-Ring (\code{rectify\_preds}):}
Given geometrically admissible non-self candidate $c\in R\setminus\{n\}$ ($c$ passes peer identity verification and is reachable or actively notified) and primary predecessor $p_1$: if
$p_1=\bot$ or $\delta_n(c)>\delta_n(p_1)$ ($c$ is strictly closer to $n$),
$\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)\gets\langle c\rangle$; if $\delta_n(c)\le\delta_n(p_1)$
(notification from a farther sub-clique predecessor), insert $c$ into
$\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)$ in descending $\delta_n(\cdot)$ order, deduplicate,
and truncate to $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{lemma}[Articulation Retention and Sub-Clique Local Finger Proof]\label{lem:articulation}
On any transitive ring, once $\operatorname{pred}_V(n)$ notifies $n$ and stale prior entries expire or depart, $\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)$ collapses to the singleton
global predecessor $[\operatorname{pred}_V(n)]$.  At an articulation node $C$
joining $m\le K$ internally stabilized cliques $V_1,\dots,V_m$, $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ retains
all $m$ sub-clique predecessors $p_i=\operatorname{pred}_{V_i}(C)$ within two
stabilization rounds (round~1 installs $p_1$ at the head, and round~2 retains all farther $p_i$ without interim eviction), protecting every bridge link $(p_i,C)$ and
allowing each $p_i$ to filter $\texttt{reported.preds}$ to
$\texttt{confirmed.pred}=\operatorname{Some}(p_i)$ and prove its local sub-clique finger
range $[0,\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_{p_i}(C)\rfloor)]$ over $V_i$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:local-range}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a transitive ring, the true global predecessor $\operatorname{pred}_V(n)$ has strictly maximal clockwise distance $\delta_n(\operatorname{pred}_V(n))$ among all $p\neq n$; once $\operatorname{pred}_V(n)$ stabilizes and sends \code{Notify}, Law~\ref{law:rectify-backoff}(3) satisfies $\delta_n(\operatorname{pred}_V(n))>\delta_n(p_1)$ for any prior $p_1$ and collapses $\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)$ to $[\operatorname{pred}_V(n)]$ (with unreachable stale predecessors removed by transport eviction). At an articulation bridge $C$ linking $m\le K$ internally stabilized cliques $V_1,\dots,V_m$, let $p_1=\operatorname{pred}_{V_{\text{near}}}(C)$ denote the global primary predecessor of $C$ (closest counter-clockwise across $\bigcup_i V_i$). Each sub-clique predecessor $p_i=\operatorname{pred}_{V_i}(C)$ satisfies $\delta_C(p_i)\le\delta_C(p_1)$, so once $p_1$ occupies index $0$ in round~1, every other $p_i$'s \code{Notify} falls into the sub-ring retention branch of \code{rectify\_preds} rather than superseding $p_1$. Since there are $m\le K$ distinct sub-clique predecessors and \code{rectify\_preds} retains the $K$ largest $\delta_C(\cdot)$ values, all $\{p_1,\dots,p_m\}$ persist inside $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)\subseteq\operatorname{Referenced}(S_C)$ within two stabilization rounds, pinning their WebRTC links and enabling each $p_i$ to filter \code{reported.preds} for itself and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:local-range} over its routable sub-clique $V_i$ on $[0,\min(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_{p_i}(C)\rfloor)]$ (resolving cross-clique keys in $(p_i,p_1]_R$ via Law~\ref{law:bridge-delivery}).
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Passive 1-Hop Gap-Bridge Hints and Bridge-Delegated Delivery]\label{law:bridge-delivery}
Whenever $n$ processes an authenticated \texttt{TopoInfo} report from
$r\in\texttt{succ}_n$, the unconfirmed peers $U(n,r)=(\operatorname{set}(\texttt{reported.preds})\cup\operatorname{set}(\texttt{reported.succ}))\setminus\{p:\rho_n(p)\}$---where $\rho_n(p)\equiv (p\in\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\cup\{n\})$---update the zero-TTL partial map $\texttt{bridged}_n:\mathsf{Did}\rightharpoonup\mathsf{Did}$ ($|\texttt{bridged}_n|\le 2K^2$, with $p\mapsto r$ iff $r\in\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\land p\notin\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)$).
Payload delivery carries $\sigma\in\operatorname{RouteStage}=\operatorname{aim}(\mathsf{Did}_{\bot})\times\mathsf{bridged}(\mathbb{B})$ (fields $(\texttt{via},\texttt{handed\_off})$ with $\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T)=\texttt{via}.\operatorname{unwrap\_or}(T)$; here $\texttt{via}=\operatorname{Some}(b)$ is fixed only at origination by a predecessor-less node via \code{reply\_via} for \code{FindSuccessorReport} replies and is delivered directly once $b$ is reached without mid-route mutation):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Rule B1 (Bridged Delivery on Local Minimum in \code{delivery\_step}):} Node $n$ first delivers directly if $\operatorname{Linked}_n(T)$ (or to $a=\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T)$ if $a\neq T\land\operatorname{Linked}_n(a)$), and otherwise runs greedy clockwise forwarding (\code{route\_toward}) over linked referenced peers $\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\cap\operatorname{Linked}(n)$ along $(n,a]_R$. Only when greedy hits a local minimum ($\operatorname{Handoff}(h)$) with $\neg\sigma.\texttt{handed\_off}$, if $\texttt{bridged}_n(a)=\operatorname{Some}(b)$ satisfies $b\neq n\land\operatorname{Linked}_n(b)\land\neg\operatorname{Linked}_n(a)$, $n$ delegates to $(b,\sigma)$ when $\delta_n(b)<\delta_n(a)$ and to $(b,(\sigma.\texttt{via},\top))$ otherwise; if no bridged hint applies, it falls back to $(h,(\sigma.\texttt{via},\top))$. When $\operatorname{Handoff}(h)$ occurs with $\sigma.\texttt{handed\_off}=\top$, \code{delivery\_step} returns $\bot$ (terminating the route acyclically).
\item \emph{Rule B2 (Gap-Bridge Lookup Delegation in \code{lookup\_successor}):} When DHT key resolution \code{find\_successor}($k$) would otherwise terminate with local head $h\notin\{k,n\}$ ($k\neq n$) across an unlinked gap, if $\exists(p\mapsto b)\in\texttt{bridged}_n$ with $b\in\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)$, $p\notin\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)$, $\operatorname{Linked}_n(b)$, and $\delta_n(k)\le\delta_n(p)<\delta_n(h)$, node $n$ delegates \code{FindSuccessorSend}($k$) via the bridge $b^*$ minimizing $\delta_n(p)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded Single-Hop Bridge Delegation and Route Safety]\label{theo:bridge-safety}
Without source-routing hop lists on the wire, $\operatorname{delivery\_step}$
over $\operatorname{RouteStage}=\operatorname{aim}(\mathsf{Did}_{\bot})\times\mathsf{bridged}(\mathbb{B})$
preserves all four route-safety invariants on any underlay graph $G=(V,L)$:
(1)~\texttt{Linked} ($\{n_k,n_{k+1}\}\in L$),
(2)~\texttt{NeverOvershoots} ($\delta_{n_k}(n_{k+1})<\delta_{n_k}(\operatorname{aim})$ on every non-crossing hop),
(3)~\texttt{SingleCrossing} and \texttt{Acyclic} (the boolean crossing bit transitions $\bot\to\top$ at most once, visiting no state $(n_k,\sigma_k)$ twice and bounding every route to $\le 2|V|$ hops), and
(4)~all-pairs cross-clique delivery in $\le 3$ hops ($2$ hops from a clique local minimum or bridge-adjacent peer, $3$ hops when greedy advances through a closer clique mate first) between two cliques $V_1,V_2$ sharing bridge $C$ whenever $(V_1\cup V_2)\setminus\{C\}\subseteq\texttt{succ}_C\cup\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ (supporting up to $s+m$ total non-bridge peers under minimal $m$-clique notifications, or up to $s+K=2K=8$, i.e.\ $C_{\mathrm{sub}}\le 4$, when $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ holds $K$ entries; larger or multi-bridge cliques fall back to TURN transport).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because $0<\delta_{n_k}(n_{k+1})<\delta_{n_k}(a)\iff \delta_{n_{k+1}}(a)=\delta_{n_k}(a)-\delta_{n_k}(n_{k+1})<\delta_{n_k}(a)$ on $R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}$, Invariant~(2) implies $\delta_{n_k}(\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T))\in\mathbb{N}$ strictly decreases across every non-crossing greedy hop within each fixed stage $\sigma$, visiting at most $|V|-1$ nodes.
At any local minimum `Handoff(h)`, $\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\cap\operatorname{Linked}(n)$ contains no node in $(n,a]_R$, so any linked bridge $b$ satisfies $\delta_n(b)>\delta_n(a)$ and both the wrapping branch of Rule~B1 and fallback handoff flip $\texttt{handed\_off}:\bot\to\top$ (while $\sigma.\texttt{via}$ never mutates mid-route, changing to $\bot$ only upon reaching $a=\operatorname{via}$ where $b$ delivers directly to $T$); since `Handoff` with $\texttt{handed\_off}=\top$ returns $\bot$, at most one ring-wrap or gap-bridge crossing occurs per route, yielding $\le 2|V|$ hops total.  In a
two-clique graph $V_1\cup V_2$ sharing bridge $C$, whenever $C$'s \texttt{succ}$_C$ and $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ cover $(V_1\cup V_2)\setminus\{C\}$ (up to $s+2=6$ combined non-bridge peers under minimal two-predecessor stabilization via Lemma~\ref{lem:articulation}, or up to $s+K=8$ peers when $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ holds $K=4$ entries), every $u\in V_i\setminus\{C\}$ learns $\texttt{bridged}_u(v)=\operatorname{Some}(C)$ for $v\in V_j\setminus\{C\}$ from $C$'s \texttt{TopoInfo}. Within full-mesh clique $V_i$, greedy forwarding takes at most one intra-clique hop $u\to w$ to the local minimum relative to $v$ ($0$ hops if $u$ itself is the local minimum or $C$ is greedy-closest in $(u,v]_R$), whereupon Rule~B1 delegates $w\to C$ and $C$ delivers directly $C\to v$ in at most $3$ hops total.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Carrier State, HLC Timestamps, and Cryptographic Data Provenance}

\begin{definition}[Data Carrier and HLC Timestamps]\label{def:carrier}
A Rings data-topic carrier (\code{dht::entry}) is a 4-tuple
\[
  x=(V,D,\rho,\beta),
\]
where $V:\mathrm{Bytes}\rightharpoonup\mathrm{Dot}$ maps live elements to
addition dots, $D:\mathrm{Digest}\rightharpoonup\mathrm{Dot}$ maps Keccak-256
element digests $h(v)$ to removal dots (tombstones), $\rho\in\mathrm{Ver}_\bot$
is the overwrite floor register, and $\beta\in\mathbb{N}_\bot$ is the carrier
liveness bound (joining by $\max$ with $\bot<t$).  Each version or dot $d$ carries a Hybrid Logical Clock (HLC) timestamp~\cite{Kulkarni2014}
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{HLC}}(d)=(w,c,o),
\]
ordered lexicographically by wall-clock time $w=\tau(d)$, logical counter
$c\in\mathbb{N}$, and writer/origin DID $o\in R$.  A removal $(e,r)\in D$
\emph{covers} an addition $(v,d)\in V$ iff $h(v)=e\land d\le r$.  A carrier $x$
is \emph{normalized} iff no addition in $V$ is covered by $D$ or $\le\rho$, and
no removal in $D$ is $\le\rho$.  The delta-state join $x\sqcup y$~\cite{Almeida2018,Preguica2018} is pointwise $\max$
followed by normalization.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Per-Writer Cryptographic Provenance (\code{DataProvenance})]\label{law:provenance}
Every element addition (\code{Append} / \code{Reset}, i.e., \code{Extend}) and removal
tombstone (\code{Retract}, i.e., \code{Tombstone}) carries a per-writer cryptographic
provenance proof $\pi_{\mathrm{prov}}\in\mathsf{DataProvenance}$ signed over the
canonical domain separator $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}core:data\mbox{-}entry:mutation}$
and a Merkle batch claim $(\mathsf{did},\mathsf{kind},\mathsf{ver},\mathrm{leaf\_count},r_{\mathrm{merkle}})$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{Verify}\bigl(&k_o,\sigma_o,\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}core:data\mbox{-}entry:mutation}\\
  &\Vert\mathsf{op}\Vert k_{\mathrm{topic}}\Vert r_{\mathrm{merkle}}\Vert(w,c,o)\bigr)=\top,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\mathsf{op}\in\{\mathsf{Reset},\mathsf{Append},\mathsf{Retract}\}$, $k_{\mathrm{topic}}\in R$
is the placement key, $r_{\mathrm{merkle}}$ commits to the batch of element digests $e=h(v)$, and $o=D(k_o)$ matches the
HLC origin DID.  Unauthenticated deltas or wall-clock timestamps exceeding local clock $t+\sigma$ are
rejected at admission.
\end{law}

\subsection{Removal Stability Horizon, Factored Carrier, and Tombstone Bounds}

\begin{definition}[Stability Horizon and Retirement]\label{def:stability}
For data topics (\code{EntryKind::Data}), writes request default retention
$\mathtt{DEFAULT\_TTL\_MS}=600\,\mathrm{s}$ up to element horizon
$H=\mathtt{MAX\_TTL\_MS}=6\,000\,\mathrm{s}$ ($6\cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{ms}$) under
tight clock-skew tolerance $\sigma=\mathtt{TS\_OFFSET\_TOLERANCE\_MS}=3\,\mathrm{s}$
(whereas offline relay-inbox mail permits $\mathtt{DEFAULT\_RELAY\_INBOX\_TTL\_MS}=86\,400\,\mathrm{s}$
up to $\mathtt{MAX\_RELAY\_INBOX\_TTL\_MS}=7\,\mathrm{d}$ bounded by signed hold instant).
A removal $(e,r)$ (or floor $\rho$) is \emph{stable} at clock $t$ iff
\[
  t\ge\tau(r)+H+\sigma.
\]
The retirement projection $\operatorname{retire}_t(x)$ drops additions with
$t\ge\tau(d)+H$ and removals/registers with $t\ge\tau(r)+H+\sigma$.
Because the horizon $H$ is uniform and $d\le r\implies\tau(d)\le\tau(r)$,
$\operatorname{retire}_t$ never leaves a live addition covered by a retired
removal and is a join-semilattice homomorphism satisfying
$\operatorname{retire}_t\circ\operatorname{retire}_s=\operatorname{retire}_{\max(s,t)}$
(distinct from the carrier-level expiry predicate $\mathrm{retired\_at}(x,t)$, which
empties surviving additions once $t\ge\beta$ if $\beta\neq\bot$).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Stable removes cover only retired adds]\label{lem:stable-covers}
If $(e,r)$ is stable at the clock $t_p$ of some node $p$, then every addition
$(v,d)$ covered by $(e,r)$ is already retired at the clock $t_q$ of every node
$q$ at the same instant ($t_q\ge t_p-\sigma\ge\tau(r)+H\ge\tau(d)+H$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since removal $(e,r)$ covers addition $(v,d)$, Definition~\ref{def:carrier} gives $d\le r$ in lexicographic HLC order, hence $\tau(d)\le\tau(r)$. By clock-skew admissibility, any two synchronized honest clocks $t_p,t_q$ at the same physical instant differ by at most $\sigma$, so $t_q\ge t_p-\sigma$. Combining stability at $p$ ($t_p\ge\tau(r)+H+\sigma$) yields $t_q\ge\tau(r)+H\ge\tau(d)+H$, so $\operatorname{retire}_{t_q}$ removes $(v,d)$ on every read, join, or sync at $q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Remove survival and Factored Carrier]\label{law:factored-carrier}
An unstable remove or register leaves a holder's replica only by
(i)~$\operatorname{retire}_t$ at stability,
(ii)~an ack-gated range handoff whose receiver holds it, or
(iii)~a platform loss event---never by storage eviction, cap, or truncation.
To enforce this, every normalized carrier $x$ factors into an element side and
a remove side:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  x &= \mathsf{E}(x)\sqcup\mathsf{R}(x),\\
  \mathsf{E}(x) &= (V,\varnothing,\bot,\beta),\qquad
  \mathsf{R}(x) = (\varnothing,D,\rho,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
$\mathsf{E}(x)$ is stored in the data store at authority $(\mathsf{Cch},\mathsf{Reb})$,
whereas $\mathsf{R}(x)$ is persisted in the dedicated \emph{removal store}
(\code{dht-removals}) at $(\mathsf{Dur},\mathsf{Ret})$ (\code{RecordAuthority::Retained}).
Byte-budget eviction maps $x\mapsto\mathsf{R}(x)$ (dropping only $\mathsf{E}(x)$),
$\mathrm{put}(k,x)$ flushes $\mathsf{R}(x)$ before writing $\mathsf{E}(x)$, and
reads project $\mathrm{get}_t(k)=\operatorname{retire}_t(\mathsf{E}_k\sqcup\mathsf{R}_k)$.
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[No Resurrection]\label{theo:no-resurrection}
Under Law~\ref{law:factored-carrier}, if holder $p$ holds $(e,r)$ at $t_0$ and
experiences no loss event before $\tau(r)+H+\sigma$, then across any sequence of
joins, evictions, and crashes, no read at $p$ after $t_0$ ever serves an
addition covered by $(e,r)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Before stability ($t<\tau(r)+H+\sigma$), Law~\ref{law:factored-carrier} keeps
$D_p(e)\ge r$ in the durable removal store across evictions and crashes, so
$\mathrm{get}_t(k)=\operatorname{retire}_t(\mathsf{E}_k\sqcup\mathsf{R}_k)$ normalizes away any covered
addition.  At and after stability, Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-covers} guarantees that
every covered addition has $t\ge\tau(d)+H$ and is stripped by
$\operatorname{retire}_t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Clock-Skew Boundary for Browser Nodes]
Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-covers} and Theorem~\ref{theo:no-resurrection} rely on bounded physical clock skew $|t_p-t_q|\le\sigma=3\,\mathrm{s}$ across synchronized honest holders. When a browser device wakes from sleep with wall-clock drift exceeding $\sigma$, Law~\ref{law:provenance} temporarily rejects skewed writes until OS/NTP time resynchronizes, prioritizing strict no-resurrection safety over skewed liveness. Conversely, because Law~\ref{law:cache-linear} evaluates local reads via pure projection $t_q\mapsto\operatorname{retire}_{t_q}(c[k])$ without a sleep-drift fence, a waking browser node $q$ whose local wall clock lags a synchronized peer $p$ by more than $\sigma$ ($t_q<t_p-\sigma$) can fail to retire and transiently surface an unretired addition $(v,d)$ whose covering tombstone $(e,r)$ has already reached stability and been garbage-collected at $p$; preventing wake-clock read resurrection requires fencing reads against a monotonic or peer-observed HLC lower bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{law}[Tombstone Bound (\code{Storage Tombstone Full})]\label{law:tombstone-bound}
Each data carrier is bounded by $T_{\max}=D_{\max}=\mathtt{ENTRY\_DATA\_MAX\_LEN}=1024$
removes.  Any peer delta $\delta$ with $|D_\delta|>T_{\max}$ is inadmissible,
and any operation or join whose retired result $y=\operatorname{retire}_t(x\sqcup\delta)$
has $|D_y|>T_{\max}$ is refused with $\mathsf{Err}(\mathsf{Exhausted})$
(\code{Storage Tombstone Full}), leaving the stored carrier unchanged.  Every
admitted carrier encodes in at most $W=w_0+D_{\max}(P_{\max}+\lambda+w_D)+T_{\max}w_T+w_\rho=33\,731\,696\,\text{B}$ ($w_0=47$, $P_{\max}=32\,768$, $\lambda=3$, $w_D=69$, $w_T=101$, $w_\rho=65\,\text{B}$; $33.73\,\text{MB}\le \tfrac{3}{4}\mathtt{TRANSPORT\_MAX\_SIZE} =45\,\text{MB}\le 60\,\text{MB}$), and any admissible sync delta simulates a sequence of $\le 2|D_\delta|+|V_\delta|+1$ writer operations.
\end{law}

\subsection{Fetch Cache Linearization and Signed Lease Heartbeats}

\begin{law}[Fetch-Cache Pure-Read Linearization]\label{law:cache-linear}
The local fetch cache $c:K\rightharpoonup X$ is a $(\mathsf{Vol},\mathsf{Reb})$
mirror whose mutations ($\mathrm{put},\mathrm{obs},\mathrm{evict}$) execute
atomically under the cache lock while reads are pure projections
$t\mapsto\operatorname{retire}_t(c[k])$ that perform zero store writes or
deletions.  Consequently, every cache transition linearizes~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}
at its store put and every read at its store get, eliminating concurrent
read-retire lost-update races.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Signed Lease Heartbeats (\code{LeaseHeartbeat})]\label{law:lease-heartbeat}
To eliminate the $120$ tombstones per hour per service ($3\,600\,\mathrm{s}/I$) and $O(N_{\mathrm{reg}}\cdot\mathrm{churn})$ read-modify-write overhead generated
when registrants overwrite timestamped descriptors every $I=30\,\mathrm{s}$,
a registrant signs a fixed lease descriptor $d$ under the unified \code{SignedDescriptor}
domain family (\texttt{rings-node:\allowbreak\{online-node,\allowbreak service,\allowbreak mail\}-descriptor} and
\texttt{rings-turn:service-descriptor}) binding its
identity, process epoch, capabilities, freshness window $T=90\,\mathrm{s}$, and
lease expiration $\ell=t+\Lambda$ ($\Lambda=10\,\mathrm{min}$).  Each heartbeat at
clock $t$ emits only $\mathrm{Extend}([d])$ (writing zero tombstones and
performing zero registry reads), rotating $d\mapsto d'$ only when $t+2I\ge\ell$
or capabilities change.  Readers at clock $t$ accept the verified descriptor of
greatest $\ell$ satisfying $\ell\le t+\Lambda+\sigma$, $T\le\Lambda$, $t\le\ell$,
and $t\le\tau^*(d)+T+\sigma$.
\end{law}

\section{Durable Local Stores and Replicated Custody}\label{sec:durable}

\subsection{Authority Lattice, Presence Markers, and Platform Crash Durability}

A Rings node persists replay windows, onion entry guards, removal records, and
relay mail across native file systems and browser IndexedDB backends.  We unify
their presence and crash-survival guarantees via a product authority lattice
(\code{RecordAuthority})~\cite{Pillai2014}.

\begin{definition}[Crash-Meaning Authority Lattice $C\times M$]\label{def:authority-lattice}
The crash chain $C=\mathsf{Vol}\sqsubset\mathsf{Cch}\sqsubset\mathsf{Dur}$ and
meaning chain $M=\mathsf{Reb}\sqsubset\mathsf{Ret}\sqsubset\mathsf{Aut}$ form
the componentwise lattice $C\times M$ with monotone obligation map
$O(c,m)=O(c)\cup O(m)$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  O(\mathsf{Vol}) &= O(\mathsf{Cch})=\varnothing,\\
  O(\mathsf{Dur}) &= \{\text{Atomicity (A)},\,\text{Survival (D)}\},\\
  O(\mathsf{Reb}) &= \varnothing,\qquad O(\mathsf{Ret})=\{\text{No-Eviction (N)}\},\\

\]

Table~\ref{tab:durable-stores} details the authority levels assigned across Rings~1.0 local stores.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Presence Markers (\code{.p}) and Per-Platform Crash Durability]\label{law:platform-durability}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Presence markers (\code{.p}):} In an authoritative native store, each
record $n=\mathrm{hex}(\mathrm{SHA1}(k))$ (used strictly as a fixed-width local filesystem key over canonical internal storage keys; SHA-256 is a drop-in substitute if untrusted external keys are exposed) has an empty witness marker $n.\mathtt{p}$
created and directory-synced only after $n.\mathtt{tmp}\to n$ is flushed and directory-synced.
On deletion, removing $n.\mathtt{p}$ is followed by a parent-directory sync barrier before unlinking $n$ and syncing the directory again, preserving $P\subseteq R$ across arbitrary OS crash points.
At restore (\code{open}), unmarked data records $n\in R\setminus P$ are repaired in place by re-creating $n.\mathtt{p}$, whereas any $n\in P\setminus R$ is classified as $\mathsf{Missing}(n)$ and fails
stream $n$ closed (discharging W for every partial loss $\lambda_{\mathrm{part}}$,
whereas total directory loss $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ leaves $P=R=\varnothing$ and serves as the documented operator reset).
\item \emph{macOS / iOS:} File data and directory entries are flushed via
$\mathtt{fcntl}(\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC})$~\cite{AppleFsync}.
\item \emph{Windows:} Temporary files flush via \code{FlushFileBuffers} and
all directory mutations execute via \code{MoveFileExW} with
\code{MOVEFILE\_REPLACE\_EXISTING} $|$ \code{MOVEFILE\_WRITE\_THROUGH}~\cite{MoveFileEx}.
\item \emph{Linux / Unix:} Data files flush via $\mathtt{fsync}$ followed by
parent directory $\mathtt{fsync}$ after atomic rename~\cite{Pillai2014}.
\item \emph{Browsers (IndexedDB):} Read-write transactions at $\mathsf{Dur}$
open with explicit $\{\mathit{durability}:\text{``\texttt{strict}''}\}$ (forcing
OS buffer flush / SQLite \texttt{EXTRA} or full WAL checkpoint across Chromium,
Gecko, and WebKit~\cite{IndexedDB3,ChromeDurability,GeckoIDB,WebKitIDB,SQLiteWAL})
combined with $\mathtt{navigator.storage.persist()}$~\cite{MDNEviction}.  Because
browsers delete origin storage whole ($P=\varnothing$) and ephemeral contexts can
only claim durability ($\mathsf{Dur}^\circ$), node-local stores open at
$(\mathsf{Dur}^\circ,m)$ with session end as $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$, while
custody of other nodes' mail requires $r\ge 2$ replicated holders.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\subsection{Group Commit and Link-Local Volatile Replay Windows}

\begin{law}[Group Commit (\code{put*})]\label{law:group-commit}
Replay transitions $t_1<t_2<\dots$ execute in lock order on speculative tables
$\hat{T}$ with undo log entries $u_i$ and coalesce into batches $B_j$ whose distinct
write-set $W_j$ is committed by
a single $\mathrm{put}^*(W_j)$ (with concurrent readers isolated from uncommitted $\hat{T}$ until commit):
on native targets, all $n.\mathtt{tmp}$ files are
pushed before a single drive-cache barrier ($\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC}$), renamed,
and directory-synced once, after which \code{Authoritative} batches run \code{witness\_batch} to create missing $n.\mathtt{p}$ markers and perform a final directory sync;
on IndexedDB, all puts share one \texttt{strict}
read-write transaction.  Callers are released only after $\mathrm{put}^*(W_j)=\mathsf{Ok}$
(latency $\le 2\phi$); on $\mathsf{Err}$, $\hat{T}$ rolls back via $u_i$ to the
last durable state $T$.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Link-Local Volatile Replay Windows ($\mathcal{W}_{s,g}$)]\label{law:link-local}
Direct neighbor control traffic (\code{Join|Sync|Leave|Send}) and application
namespaces registered in $D_r$ as delegating replay (such as Sphinx onion cells,
which enforce at-most-once admission in their own Bloom filter $R_i$ and process
epoch $e_i$) are classified by $\mathrm{ll}(t,c)$ on authenticated connection
$c$ of live generation $g$ and admitted against an in-memory per-generation
window $\mathcal{W}_{s,g}$ instead of the durable store $\mathcal{W}_s$.
This eliminates durable disk flushes on the onion/control hot path while
preserving per-generation duplicate/fork detection and cross-restart replay
immunity.
\end{law}

\subsection{Receiver Custody Set and Age-Gated Replicated Relay Custody}

\begin{definition}[Receiver Custody Set ($\mathcal{K}_s$)]\label{def:custody-set}
To prevent relay mail admitted into $\mathcal{W}_s$ from being lost if the node
restarts before the application consumes it from the volatile inbox,
the receiver record of stream $s$ co-persists $(\mathcal{W}_s,\mathcal{K}_s)$
atomically in a single $(\mathsf{Dur},\mathsf{Aut})$ record, where
$\mathcal{K}_s\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathrm{Digest}\times\mathbb{N}$ holds
admitted but untaken deliveries $(q,h,x)$ with spent horizon
$x=\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl}+H_I+2\epsilon$ (for clock-skew safety margin $\epsilon>0$ and $|\bigcup_s\mathcal{K}_s|\le\mathtt{RELAY\_INBOX\_MAX\_LEN}=64$).
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Custody Lifecycle and Age-Gated Replicated Relay Chain]\label{law:replicated-custody}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Custody drain and release:} On drain after $\mathrm{put}^*$, an
element $(s,q,h)$ already in $\mathcal{K}_s$ is requeued without re-checking
$\mathcal{W}_s$; a new element commits $(\mathcal{W}'_s,\mathcal{K}_s\cup\{(q,h,x)\})$
before queueing; and the carrier tombstone/Ack is issued only after the
application takes the message and commits $(\mathcal{W}_s,\mathcal{K}_s\setminus\{(q,h,x)\})$.
By information-theoretic indistinguishability, no bounded-window receiver can
safely admit un-custodied mail that has fallen behind live traffic ($q<\operatorname{rmin}(\mathcal{W}_s)$, where $\operatorname{rmin}(\mathcal{W}_s)$ is the lowest sequence tracked by $\mathcal{W}_s$).
\item \emph{Age-gated replicated custody ($\mathcal{M}_r(k)$):} Because browser
holders operate at claimed durability $\mathsf{Dur}^\circ$ and experience high
churn~\cite{BlakeRodrigues}, a node advertises relay custody only after
surviving an online age gate $A_{\min}$ ($\mathtt{DEFAULT\_RELAY\_AGE\_GATE\_MS}=2\,\mathrm{min}$
floored at $\ge 4$ stabilization periods, and $A_{\min}=30\,\mathrm{min}$ in production
relay profiles).  Each mail key $k$ is replicated
across a chain $\mathcal{M}_r(k)$ of the first $r$ age-gated holders clockwise
from $\operatorname{owner}_N(k)$ ($r=4$ to $\mathtt{DEFAULT\_RELAY\_INBOX\_REPLICAS}=8$).
\item \emph{Chain replication and digest repair:} Holders forward head-to-tail
along $\mathcal{M}_r(k)$ (digest first, payload on miss) and acknowledge
tail-to-head only after all $q(k)\le r$ members persist the element.  Adjacent
chain members probe liveness every $P_c=2.5\,\mathrm{s}$ (suspect after
$\tau_c=7.5\,\mathrm{s}$) and repair via bundled carrier digest-set comparison on
membership change or periodic pass, restoring $r$ copies within $D\le T_d+r\delta$
(where $T_d=P_c+\tau_c=10\,\mathrm{s}$ is worst-case silent failure detection and
$\delta$ is per-hop repair transfer time).
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\section{Fair Admission, Slot Accounting, and Load Bounds}\label{sec:load}

\subsection{Cleanest-First Slot Roster and Fair Slot Accounting}

Because a Rings DID is a free public-key digest, any node-wide table keyed solely
by origin DID can be exhausted by one adversary minting thousands of identities.
Rings anchors resource accounting to the authenticated transport connection slot.

\begin{definition}[Cleanest-First Slot Roster (\code{SlotRoster})]\label{def:slot-roster}
A node's connection registry holds at most
\[
  \mathcal{R}(s)=2(F+s+1)
\]
active neighbour edges, where $F=160$ is the finger table size and $s$ is the
successor capacity ($\mathcal{R}(4)=330$ at $s=4$; $\mathcal{R}(3)=328$ at $s=3$).
The \code{SlotRoster} indexes slots $\kappa\in\{0,\dots,\mathcal{R}(s)-1\}$, tracking
per-slot metadata $(\mathit{epoch},\mathit{strikes},\mathit{last\_seen\_ms})$
and live floor utilization across shared tables $T$:
\[
  w_t(\kappa)=\max_T\frac{\min\bigl(\operatorname{use}^t_{T,\kappa},\,f_T\bigr)}{f_T}.
\]
When a new edge $e$ is admitted, it is assigned the \emph{cleanest} free slot
$\kappa(e)=\arg\min_{\kappa\in F_t}w_t(\kappa)$ (ties broken by least index),
and charges in table $T$ remain bound to $\kappa$ until they expire by $T$'s own
horizon law.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Slot Accounting]\label{law:slot-accounting}
Every shared table $T$ is parameterized by a per-slot guaranteed floor $f_T$, a
shared overflow pool $\mathcal{P}_T$, a local share $L_T$ for principal $\lambda$,
and dedicated shares $S_d$ for $d\in D_T$, yielding the hard node-wide bound
\[
  B_T(s)=\mathcal{R}(s)\,f_T+\mathcal{P}_T+L_T+\sum_{d\in D_T}S_d.
\]
With slot usage $\operatorname{use}_\kappa$, pool overflow
$o_\kappa=\max(0,\operatorname{use}_\kappa-f_T)$, and active claimant set
$\mathcal{A}(t)=\{\kappa:o_\kappa>0\text{ or }\kappa\text{ has a queued/pending overflow request}\}$
of size $n(\mathcal{A}(t))=|\mathcal{A}(t)|\ge 1$ (waiting claimants persist in $\mathcal{A}(t)$ until served or withdrawn), an item introduced by slot
$\kappa$ is admitted iff either:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\operatorname{use}_\kappa<f_T$ (within the slot's guaranteed floor), or
\item $\sum_{\kappa'}o_{\kappa'}<\mathcal{P}_T$ and
$o_\kappa<\bigl\lceil\mathcal{P}_T / n(\mathcal{A}(t))\bigr\rceil$ (within the
slot's fair pool claim).
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-Table Slot Isolation]\label{theo:slot-isolation}
Across all five shared node tables---(1)~Application Inbox / Mailbox Ledger ($S_\mu$-bounded),
(2)~Relay Store ($S_\mu$-bounded), (3)~CRDT Removal Store ($f=128$, $\mathcal{P}=16\,384$, $L=7\,168$, $S_{\mathrm{custody}}=16\,384$, yielding $B_{\mathrm{rem}}(4)=82\,176$ items at default $s=4$; $81\,920$ at $s=3$), (4)~Transaction Replay Table ($B_{\mathrm{rep}}(4)=15\,680\le 16\,384$) and per-lane Origin Quota ($f=1$, $\mathcal{P}=2\,048$, $L=1\,056$, $S_{\mathrm{inbox}}=640$, yielding $B_{\mathrm{orig}}(4)=4\,074\le 4\,096$), and
(5)~Chunk Reassembly Buffer $O$---Law~\ref{law:slot-accounting} and
Definition~\ref{def:slot-roster} guarantee:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $|T|\le B_T(s)$ in all reachable states;
\item any edge holding slot $\kappa$ with $\operatorname{use}_\kappa<f_T$ is
never refused, regardless of how many origins, edges, or slots other parties
control;
\item an over-floor slot is guaranteed at least
$f_T+\lfloor\mathcal{P}_T/\mathcal{R}(s)\rfloor$ items as prior claimants expire;
\item under cleanest-first assignment (\code{SlotRoster}), an adversary rotating
Sybil connections cannot degrade a newcomer's floor with a single departure-and-rejoin
(unlike least-free-index selection), multiplying the single-attacker floor-pollution
threshold by $\mathcal{R}(s)-c$ ($300\times$ at $\mathcal{R}(4)=330, c=30$) distinct
admissions within one horizon $h$ (while sustained high-rate Sybil flooding past $\mathcal{R}(s)-c$ relies on connection rate limiting or DRanking~2.0 admission filtering; Section~\ref{sec:trust}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Items (1)--(3) follow because floor checks read $\operatorname{use}_\kappa$
alone, departures never erase slot charges, and with $n\le\mathcal{R}(s)$ active
claimants, any slot below $\lfloor\mathcal{P}_T/\mathcal{R}(s)\rfloor$ satisfies
$o_\kappa<\lceil\mathcal{P}_T/n\rceil$ and can refill once draining items drop
overflow below $\mathcal{P}_T$.  Item (4)
holds because a free slot with $w_t(\kappa)=0$ is always selected before any
partially charged slot $\kappa'$ with $w_t(\kappa')>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Replay Stream Eviction and Credit Reclaim}

\begin{law}[Replay Stream Spent-Horizon Eviction]\label{law:replay-eviction}
A receiver replay stream $s$ with maximum admitted transaction expiry
$x_s=\max(\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl})$ is \emph{spent} at clock $t$ once
$t\ge x_s+\iota+\sigma$ (for default control/storage/E2E traffic, the spent
horizon is $H_{\text{spent}}=600\,\mathrm{s}$; for offline-capable application
streams, $\iota=H_I+\sigma$ where $H_I=7\,\mathrm{d}$).  Because transactions past
$x_s+\iota+\sigma$ are rejected by envelope TTL and relay hold-horizon checks
prior to replay lookup, spent streams holding no custody $\mathcal{K}_s$ are
lazily evicted without reopening replay.  With $f=32$, $\mathcal{P}=3\,328$,
$L=1\,024$, and inbox share $S_\mu=768$, no honest slot can be permanently
locked out.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Credit Reclaim (\code{Credit/Shrink/Returned}) and Hard Frame Bound]\label{law:credit-reclaim}
Each edge has four lanes $\ell$ (one priority control lane with floor
$g_{\mathrm{prio}}=W=16$, and three data lanes with floor $g_\ell=1$ and cap $W=16$).
To bound total in-flight and buffered frames node-wide without dropping frames,
credit frames carry a lane generation $g$ tracking receiver-consumed frames $R_{\mathrm{rx}}$
and sender-committed frames $c_{\mathrm{tx}}$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mathsf{Credit}(A,g)$: receiver grants limit $A$ in generation $g$;
\item $\mathsf{Shrink}(L',g)$: receiver reclaims unused pooled credit on an idle
($T_{\mathrm{idle}}=1\,\mathrm{s}$) or over-share lane down to $L'=\min(R_{\mathrm{rx}}+g_\ell,A)$;
\item $\mathsf{Returned}(B,g)$: sender commits at most $B=\max(c_{\mathrm{tx}},L')$ frames and
ignores older generations.
\end{itemize}
With frame pool $\mathcal{P}_F=256$ frames ($16\,\mathrm{MiB}$), where
$c(s)=\mathcal{R}(s)(W+3)$ sums per-slot guaranteed floors across the four lanes and
$W_{\text{shrink}}=\mathcal{P}_F$ reserves reclaimed pooled credits until $\mathsf{Returned}(B,g)$ clears,
total node frame liability satisfies the hard bound
\[
  \sum_{e,\ell}\operatorname{liab}(e,\ell)\;\le\;
  B_F(s)=c(s)+W_{\text{shrink}}=\mathcal{R}(s)(W+3)+\mathcal{P}_F
\]
($6\,488$ frames at $s=3$; $6\,526$ at $s=4$), with zero honest frame drops and
guaranteed per-edge pooled throughput $\ge\min(3(W-1),\lfloor\mathcal{P}_F/n\rfloor)$.
\end{law}

\subsection{Single-Charge Chunk Reassembly and Per-Category Barrier}

\begin{law}[Single-Charge Chunk Reassembly and Credit-Time Rate Floor]\label{law:chunk-reassembly}
A chunked message $[0,n)$ of chunk size $d_0$ reserves its full footprint
$m=2n d_0$ \emph{once} on its first chunk in both the mailbox ledger and the open-message
table $O$ ($f_O=256\,\mathrm{KiB}$, $\mathcal{P}_O=128\,\mathrm{MiB}$,
$B_O(s)=\mathcal{R}(s)f_O+\mathcal{P}_O$, i.e.\ $210.5\,\mathrm{MiB}\le 256\,\mathrm{MiB}$ at $s=4$), waiting in \code{ResourceOrderedQueue}
order ($O((g+k+r)\log n)$ per event, where $g$ is granted requests, $k$ displaced waiters moved to another blocker, and $r$ named resources) and moving into dispatch at completion
without re-charging.  To prevent slowloris reservation pinning without penalizing
receiver-throttled links, the receiver tracks \emph{credit-time} $\hat{\tau}$ for the active
head-of-line message on lane $\ell$,
which advances over $[t_k,t_{k+1})$ by $\min(t_{k+1}-t_k,\,c_k d_0/r_{\mathrm{floor}})$ only
while the sender holds $c_k=A-N>0$ unused credits.  Messages must maintain a
streaming rate floor ($r_{\mathrm{floor}}\in[r_{\min},10\,\mathrm{B/ms}]$ with $r_{\min}=1\,024\,\mathrm{B/s}$)
with grace window $G=15\,\mathrm{s}$ (up to $(W+1)d_0/r_{\mathrm{floor}}+\Delta$, where $\Delta$ bounds link/RTT slack), expiring
attributably if received bytes fall below $b<r_{\mathrm{floor}}\hat{\tau}-W d_0$.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Per-Category Reassembly Barrier (\code{Control | Interactive | Bulk})]\label{law:category-barrier}
Inbound dispatch maintains independent ordering barriers per traffic category
$\kappa\in\{\mathsf{Control},\mathsf{Interactive},\mathsf{Bulk}\}$ (mapped to
protocol lanes $\{\mathit{ctl},\mathit{sto},\mathit{e2e},\mathit{app}\}$): a
frame $f$ of category $\kappa$ waits only behind unfinished reassembly events
$u\in U$ with $\mathit{seq}(u)<\mathit{seq}(f)\land\kappa(u)=\kappa$.  This
guarantees exact sequence order within every category (including chunked control
frames) while preventing bulk data reassembly from blocking control or
interactive lanes.
\end{law}

\section{Privacy Layer --- Cryptographic Carriers and Sphinx Onion Kleisli Pipeline}\label{sec:privacy}

\subsection{Separate Cryptographic Carriers}

\begin{definition}[End-to-end carrier, signed frame, and secret sharing]
End-to-end encryption (ElGamal $(g^k, m\cdot h^k)$ on prime-order group $\mathbb{G}\neq R$) and Shamir threshold secret sharing over $\mathbb{F}_q[X]$~\cite{ssss} operate strictly off the routing carrier:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  f &= (sid,seq,last,cipher,pk_s,\sigma_s),\\
  \operatorname{FrameValid}(f) &\iff V(pk_s,\sigma_s,m_f)=\top\\
  &\qquad\land\operatorname{Session}(D(pk_s),sid,e),\\
  P\in\mathbb{F}_q[X],\quad sh_i &= (i,P(i))\in\mathbb{F}_q^2,\\
  \operatorname{reconstruct}(\{sh_i\}) &= P(0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $m_f$ is the canonical serialization of $(sid,seq,last,cipher)$, and payloads exceeding link $d_0$ are sliced deterministically into $total=\lceil|data|/d_0\rceil$ indexed chunks carried inside authenticated frames before reassembly.
\end{definition}

\begin{invariant}[Chunk reassembly]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{complete}(m) &\iff
  \forall i<total(m).\;\exists!c_i\\
  &\qquad\land \sum_i |c_i|\leq B_m \land now<ttl(m).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Operation Signature and Kleisli Pipeline Composition}

Rings 1.0 replaces legacy relay framing with the \textbf{Sphinx Onion Kleisli
Pipeline} (\code{rings-node::onion}), generalizing onion circuits from
simple exit tunnels to client-sealed loops over a static operation signature
$\Sigma$ composed in the Kleisli category $\mathbf{Kl}(M)$~\cite{Danezis2009,Piotrowska2017,Hoang2015,Moggi1991,Plotkin2003,Hughes2000,Lindley2011}.

\begin{definition}[Operation Signature $\Sigma$ and Partial $\Sigma$-Algebras]\label{def:onion-signature}
The static shape signature is
\[
  \Sigma=\{\mathsf{relay}\}\cup\Sigma_W\cup\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}},
\]
containing $1+3+120=124$ rows, where $\Sigma_W=\{\mathsf{tcp},\mathsf{https},\mathsf{udp}\}$ is the set of world-facing exit stream symbols, $\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}}=\{\mathsf{invoke}\langle\mathrm{In},\mathrm{Out},L\rangle:\mathrm{In}\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{W},\,\mathrm{Out}\in\mathcal{W},\,L\in\mathcal{L}\}$ has fixed output width $W(f)=\mathrm{Out}$ across discrete width classes $\mathcal{W}=\{32,64,256,1024,16384\}\,\mathrm{B}$ and latency classes $\mathcal{L}=\{10,100,500,2000\}\,\mathrm{ms}$, and open invoke backends $\omega\in\Omega$ ($|\omega|\le 32$) include:
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\code{dht.find\_successor}, \code{dht.notify}, \code{dht.sync},
\code{storage.lookup}, \code{storage.store}, \code{service.lookup},
\code{service.register}, \code{direct.message}.
\end{center}
Each node $n$ publishes signed registrations $\mathrm{Reg}_n\subseteq\{\mathsf{relay}\}\cup\Sigma_W\cup(\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}}\times\Omega)$ (via \texttt{rings-node:onion-\{exit,invoke\}-descriptor}) carrying its 128-bit process epoch $e_n$ and quota $\operatorname{quota}(n,f,\omega)$, acting as a partial $\Sigma$-algebra with generic effect~\cite{Plotkin2003,Plotkin2009}
\[
  \llbracket f\rrbracket_n:\mathrm{Args}_f\times\mathrm{In}_f\to M\,\mathrm{Out}_f.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kleisli Pipeline Composition (Laws L1, L2)]\label{def:kleisli-pipeline}
A pipeline $P=(f_1,\bar{a}_1)\mathbin{\ggg}\cdots\mathbin{\ggg}(f_n,\bar{a}_n)$
is a well-formed morphism of the free category $\mathcal{F}(G_\Sigma)$ iff
$1\le n\le n_{\max}=4$, $\mathrm{Out}_{f_k}=\mathrm{In}_{f_{k+1}}$ for all
$k<n$, intermediate symbols satisfy $\mathsf{Intermediate}\in\operatorname{pos}(f_k)$,
and world-facing stream symbols ($\mathsf{tcp},\mathsf{https},\mathsf{udp}$) appear only at
$n=1$.  Placed on nodes $h_1,\dots,h_n$, $P$ denotes the Kleisli composite in
$\mathbf{Kl}(M)$:
\[
  \llbracket P\rrbracket(v_0)=
  \bigl(\llbracket f_1\rrbracket_{h_1}(\bar{a}_1,-)\mathbin{\ggg}\cdots\mathbin{\ggg}
  \llbracket f_n\rrbracket_{h_n}(\bar{a}_n,-)\bigr)(v_0),
\]
satisfying \textbf{Law L1 (Identity)}: $\llbracket\mathsf{relay}\rrbracket=\eta$ and
$\operatorname{Loop}(\mathsf{relay}\mathbin{\ggg}P)\overset{d}{=}\operatorname{Loop}(P)$
via normal-form $\operatorname{nf}$ reduction, and \textbf{Law L2 (Associativity)}:
$(f\mathbin{\ggg}g)\mathbin{\ggg}h=f\mathbin{\ggg}(g\mathbin{\ggg}h)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Client-Sealed Loops, Uniform Sphinx Header, and AEZ v5 Carry}

\begin{definition}[Client-Sealed Loop and Hall-Filter Draw Order (Law L7)]\label{def:onion-loop}
For client $\diamond$, entry guard $g$, and $n\in[1,4]$ symbol hops $h_1,\dots,h_n$,
each segment $k\in[0,n]$ inserts $s=2$ relays $r_{k,1},r_{k,2}$ (the minimal $s$
such that no node is adjacent to both $\diamond$ and a symbol hop):
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Loop}(P):\ \diamond&\to g=r_{0,1}\to r_{0,2}\to h_1\to r_{1,1}\to r_{1,2}\\
  &\to\cdots\to h_n\to r_{n,1}\to r_{n,2}=g\to\diamond,
\end{aligned}
\]
with total hops $H(n,s)=(s+1)n+s\le\hat{H}=14$ ($H\in\{5,8,11,14\}$) and $H-1$
distinct peer DIDs (\textbf{Law L7: Guard Closure}).  Route selection draws
$h_1,\dots,h_n$ first, then guard $g$, then remaining relays $r_{k,j}$ using a
bipartite-matching \textbf{Hall filter} $\mathcal{F}(\pi,p)$ (retaining candidates at position $p$ for partial placement $\pi$ only if extending $\pi$ admits a complete system of distinct mutually routable representatives across the remaining $\{1,\dots,H\}$ role slots) so that if the first draw succeeds,
no subsequent draw can fail, and exit marginals over the Hall-admissible exit candidate set $N_{h_1}$ equal exact quality weights
$q(d)/q(N_{h_1})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[2870-Byte Sphinx Header and 205-Byte Uniform Per-Hop Layer (Laws L3, L4)]\label{def:sphinx-header}
Every hop $i\in[1,H]$ across all roles (\code{HopRole::Transit | Terminal | GuardReturn})
peels an identical 205-byte layer
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \lambda_i &= (f_i,\bar{a}_i,\mathrm{next}_i,e_i,x,\nu_i,\kappa_i,\gamma_{i+1}),\\
  \ell &= 1+64+20+16+8+16+64+16=205\,\mathrm{B},
\end{aligned}
\]
from a fixed-length Sphinx header~\cite{Danezis2009} $\chi_i=\alpha_i\Vert\beta_i\Vert\gamma_i$ with blinded SEC1 group element $|\alpha_i|=33\,\mathrm{B}$, padded routing block $|\beta_i|=\hat{H}\ell=14\times 205=2870\,\mathrm{B}$, and MAC $|\gamma_i|=16\,\mathrm{B}$ ($|\chi_i|=2919\,\mathrm{B}$), where $x=\lceil t_{\mathrm{build}}/Q\rceil Q+X_0$ ($Q=30\,\mathrm{s}$, $X_0=120\,\mathrm{s}$, $V=150\,\mathrm{s}$) and $\kappa_i=(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}},\sigma_{\mathrm{out}})\in\{0,1\}^{256}\times\{0,1\}^{256}$ partitions the 64-byte carry field into a 32-byte inbound carry seed $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}$ and a 32-byte outbound segment seed $\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ (filled with uniform dummy bytes at pure transit relays).
Each loop selects a cell bucket class $b\in[16\,\mathrm{KiB},12\,\mathrm{MiB}]$ with uniform carry slot width $C_b=b-2919\,\mathrm{B}$ ($C_{16\,\mathrm{KiB}}=13\,465\,\mathrm{B}$, $82\%$ payload efficiency, \textbf{Law L3}) and deterministic release time $t^{\mathrm{dep}}_i=t^{\mathrm{arr}}_i+L(f_i)$ (\textbf{Law L4}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[AEZ v5 Wide-Block Body Carry (\code{OnionBodyCipher})]\label{def:aez-carry}
For each segment $k$, the client draws a 32-byte seed $\sigma_k$ (delivered as $\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ to symbol producer $p_k=h_k$ for intermediate segments $k\ge 1$) and derives
48-byte AEZ v5 keys~\cite{Hoang2015,AEZv5} $k_{r_{k,j}}$ and $k_{c_k}$ via
HKDF-SHA256.  Producer $p_k$ applies one authenticated wide-block encipherment
$\mathrm{E}^{\tau}_{k_{c_k}}$ ($\tau=16$ zero-byte authenticator) over canonical serialization $\operatorname{enc}(v_k)$ followed by
$s=2$ length-preserving wide-block encipherments $\mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,j}}}$ ($\tau=0$):
\[
\begin{aligned}
  y_{k,0} &= \mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,1}}}\circ\mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,2}}}\circ\mathrm{E}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}\bigl(\operatorname{pad}(\operatorname{enc}(v_k))\bigr),\\
  y_{k,j} &= \mathrm{D}^0_{k_{r_{k,j}}}(y_{k,j-1}),\qquad
  b_k = \mathrm{D}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}(y_{k,2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{SURB Batching, Cover Loops, Replay Admission, and Security Laws}

\begin{law}[SURB Batching, Constant-Rate Cover, and Sliced-Bloom Replay (Law L9)]\label{law:surb-replay}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{SURB batching:} A Single-Use Reply Block for world-facing session
$\varsigma$ is the $2\,979$-byte ($20+2\,919+32+8=2\,979\,\mathrm{B}$) client-sealed suffix $\upsilon=(\mathrm{next},\allowbreak\chi_\upsilon,\allowbreak\sigma_\upsilon,\allowbreak x_\upsilon)$~\cite{Chaum1981,Danezis2003}.
After deducting the $16\,\mathrm{B}$ AEZ authenticator ($C_0=C_{16\,\mathrm{KiB}}-16=13\,449\,\mathrm{B}$) and $6\,\mathrm{B}$ credit-frame overhead, a $16\,\mathrm{KiB}$ credit loop leaves $13\,443\,\mathrm{B}$ for SURBs---batching $k=\lfloor 13\,443/2979\rfloor=4$ SURBs plus its own suffix ($k+1=5$ reply credits per forward loop, $Q_{\max}=256$)---whereas a $7\,\mathrm{B}$-framed stream data cell carries up to $C_0-7=13\,442\,\mathrm{B}$ of application payload.
\item \emph{Constant-rate cover loops:} Active links emit at constant rate
$r=\rho B/V\approx 98.3\,\mathrm{units/s}$ ($\rho=0.9$, substituting cover cells when no real cell is
queued) and maintain an idle floor $r_{\mathrm{idle}}=1\,\mathrm{unit/s}$, giving an analytical single-link paced emission ceiling of $98.3\times 13\,442\,\mathrm{B}\approx 1.32\,\mathrm{MB/s}$ at $b=16\,\mathrm{KiB}$ (sustaining $1.045\pm 0.003\,\mathrm{MB/s}$ end-to-end across a 5-node live WebRTC loopback, above the $1.00\,\mathrm{MB/s}$ CI throughput floor; Section~\ref{sec:verification}).
\item \emph{\code{SlotRoster}-backed sliced-Bloom replay admission (Law L9):}
Every received cell is charged $u(b)=b/16\,\mathrm{KiB}$ units against the sender
DID's ledger $\Lambda_i(d)$ ($|\operatorname{dom}\Lambda_i|\le 2\mathcal{R}$,
per-sender budget $B=16\,384$ units, global budget $G=64B$ over $V/Q=5$ quanta)
\emph{before} SEC1 decoding and ECDH.  Valid tags $\nu_i$ are recorded in
expiry-quantized sliced Bloom filters~\cite{Bloom1970,Almeida2007} $R_i[x]$,
structured as a scalable chain of $76\,960\,\mathrm{B}$ blocks ($K=26$ slices of $M=23\,680$ bits, sealing $B=16\,384$ tags per block up to $64$ global blocks) queried via process-secret-keyed $\mathrm{Keccak\text{-}256}(\kappa\Vert\nu_i\Vert j)$ probes (per-block false-positive rate $\le 2^{-26}$,
union FP $\le 2^{-20}$, max memory $\approx 4.70\,\mathrm{MiB}$ at 64 blocks), ensuring every AEZ
key is used at most once across restarts and clock rollbacks.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Onion Pipeline Security Laws (Laws L5, L5$'$, L6, L8)]\label{theo:onion-security}
Under the Gap-DDH assumption over secp256k1 in the Random Oracle Model (modeling HKDF as a random oracle against active header-peeling queries~\cite{Danezis2009}), together with ChaCha20 PRG, HMAC-SHA256 EUF-CMA, and AEZ~v5 strong PRP / robust AEAD assumptions~\cite{Hoang2015,Degabriele2018}, let $\pi_i(P)$ denote hop $i$'s local interface projection (role, adjacent links, symbol $f_i$, arguments $\bar{a}_i$, expiry $x$, and cell class $b$), $\pi^\circ_i$ its position-erased projection (identical to $\pi_i(P)$ with hop index $i$ omitted), $\sigma(P)$ the visible loop class, and $D_i$ the cumulative deterministic delay from client build time $t_{\mathrm{build}}$ to arrival $t^{\mathrm{arr}}_i=t_{\mathrm{build}}+D_i$ modulo $Q$. Every loop built by Definitions~\ref{def:onion-loop}--\ref{def:aez-carry} satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Law L5 (Noninterference):} $\pi_i(P)=\pi_i(P')\land\sigma(P)=\sigma(P')\implies\operatorname{view}_i(P)\approx_c\operatorname{view}_i(P')$: no hop learns another hop's arguments $\bar{a}_j$, invoke backend name $\omega_j$, or segment value $v_k\neq\hat{v}_i$~\cite{Goguen1982}.
\item \textbf{Law L5$'$ (Position Hiding):} For loops $P,P'$ of arbitrary lengths $H,H'\le\hat{H}=14$, a hop's distinguishing advantage between positions $i$ and $i'$ with matching local projection $\pi^\circ_i$ under uniform build-phase quantization modulo $Q$ is bounded by
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{Adv}_i\;\le{}&\hat{H}\bigl(\epsilon_{\mathrm{Gap\text{-}DDH}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{PRG}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{MAC}}\bigr)\\
  &+\min\bigl(1,\,|D_i-D'_{i'}|/Q\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
\item \textbf{Law L6 (Client Path Authority):} $\mathrm{next}_i$ is sealed in $\lambda_i$ by $\diamond$ independently of runtime values $v_k$, admitting the Arrow composition fragment ($\mathbin{\ggg}$) while excluding promiscuous monadic bind ($\gg\!\!=$)~\cite{Lindley2011}.
\item \textbf{Law L8 (Byte Unlinkability and Tagging Resistance):} Consecutive edges carry independent uniform ciphertexts against external link observers and pure transit relays ($\chi_i\neq\chi_{i+1}$, $y_{i-1}\neq y_i$; on intermediate segments $k\ge 1$, a corrupted symbol producer $p_k=h_k$ holds segment seed $\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}$ and can correlate ciphertexts on segment $k$ only if colluding with a downstream link observer), class relabeling fails $\gamma_i=\mathrm{MAC}_{K_i}(b\Vert\beta_i)$, and any modification $\tilde{y}\neq y_{k,j}$ is rejected by consumer $\mathrm{D}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}$ with probability $\ge 1-q\cdot 2^{-128}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{AEZ}}$ across $q$ active forgery queries ($\ge 1-2^{-128}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{AEZ}}$ per single attempt)~\cite{Degabriele2018}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
We outline the hybrid sequence across an $H$-hop loop ($H\le\hat{H}=14$), adapting the Sphinx and AEZ reductions of Danezis--Goldberg~\cite{Danezis2009}, Hoang et al.~\cite{Hoang2015}, and Degabriele--Stam~\cite{Degabriele2018}:
\emph{Game~$G_0\to G_1$ (Gap-DDH Blinding \& Random-Oracle Key Schedule):} Under Gap-DDH over secp256k1 and random-oracle HKDF, even an active adversary submitting chosen group elements $\alpha$ to honest header-peeling relays cannot distinguish an uncorrupted hop $j$'s ephemeral shared secret and derived keys $(k^{\mathrm{enc}}_j,k^{\mathrm{mac}}_j,k^{\mathrm{blind}}_j)$ from uniform independent keys except with advantage $\hat{H}\epsilon_{\mathrm{Gap\text{-}DDH}}$, ensuring fresh unlinkable $\alpha_j\neq\alpha_{j+1}$ across re-blinded segments.
\emph{Game~$G_1\to G_2$ (PRG Pad \& Fixed-Width Position Hiding):} Replacing ChaCha20 keystream expansions over $|\beta|=\hat{H}\ell=2\,870\,\mathrm{B}$ at each uncorrupted hop incurs advantage at most $\hat{H}\epsilon_{\mathrm{PRG}}$ over $\hat{H}$ peeling steps. Because all three \code{HopRole} variants pack identical $205$-byte slots shifted and padded to constant width $2\,870\,\mathrm{B}$, the decrypted header tail after hop $i$ is uniform noise for both $H$ and $H'$, isolating only the local arrival-to-expiry offset $(x-t^{\mathrm{arr}}_i)$ under uniform build phase $t_{\mathrm{build}}\bmod Q$ (absent external inter-hop timing observation of deterministic releases $L(f_i)$): with $t^{\mathrm{arr}}_i=t_{\mathrm{build}}+D_i$, this offset at positions $(i,D_i)$ and $(i',D'_{i'})$ has statistical distance at most $\min(1,|D_i-D'_{i'}|/Q)$, establishing Law~L5$'$.
\emph{Game~$G_2\to G_3$ (Header MAC \& Path Authority):} Under EUF-CMA security of HMAC-SHA256 ($\hat{H}\epsilon_{\mathrm{MAC}}$), no adversary without $k^{\mathrm{mac}}_j$ can forge $\gamma_j=\mathrm{MAC}_{k^{\mathrm{mac}}_j}(b\Vert\beta_j)$ or substitute another hop class $b$ or routing descriptor $\lambda_j$; thus $\mathrm{next}_j$ and $\bar{a}_j$ remain fixed by client $\diamond$ independently of intermediate payload values $v_k$ (Law~L6), while hops $j\neq i$ see only uniform XORed/encrypted slots for $\pi_i(P)=\pi_i(P')$ (Law~L5).
\emph{Game~$G_3\to G_4$ (Wide-Block Body Carry):} For any segment $k$, the producer wraps fixed-width padded plaintext $\operatorname{pad}(\operatorname{enc}(v_k))$ through $\mathrm{E}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}$ and $s=2$ passes of $\mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,j}}}$, with keys derived from fresh nonce-protected seed $\sigma_k$ and replay protection enforced once via $R_i[x]$ (Law~\ref{law:surb-replay}). By AEZ v5 strong PRP security ($\tau=0$) at relays and robust AEAD security ($\tau=16$) at the consumer~\cite{Hoang2015,Degabriele2018}, intermediate ciphertexts $y_{k,j}$ are pseudorandom permutations over $\{0,1\}^{8C_b}$ (yielding segment-$k$ byte unlinkability $y_{i-1}\neq y_i$ against external observers and transit relays that lack $\sigma_k$), and any bit-flip, truncation, or tagging $\tilde{y}\neq y_{k,j}$ anywhere on the segment causes consumer $\mathrm{D}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}$ to fail its $0^{128}$ authenticator check with advantage at most $q\cdot 2^{-128}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{AEZ}}$ across $q$ forgery queries (Law~L8).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sidecar Witness Ordering}

\begin{definition}[Sidecar witness and partial order]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  w &= (domain,root,height,time,finality),\\
  \operatorname{Witness}(msg,w) &\iff \operatorname{Include}(H(msg),w.root)=\top\\
  &\qquad\land\operatorname{Final}(w)=\top,\\
  a\prec b &\iff domain(w_a)=domain(w_b)\\
  &\qquad\land time(w_a)<time(w_b),\\
  \chi &: Domain_a\times Domain_b\to Order,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\operatorname{Include}(H(msg),w.root)$ checks a canonical inclusion proof against a finalized sidecar state root and $\chi$ provides a deterministic cross-domain ordering comparator reconciling witnesses from distinct domains.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Counterparty-Signed Service Receipts and the DRanking 2.0 Bridge}

In an open network where identity creation is free ($\mathsf{cost}(\mathsf{did})=0$),
unsigned local behavior counters or naive median/trimmed-mean votes over unverified
peer reports are vulnerable to Sybil amplification~\cite{Douceur2002}.  Rings 1.0
therefore establishes a cryptographic evidence primitive on the wire---the
counterparty-signed \textbf{Service Receipt} (\code{ServiceReceipt})---which bridges
directly into the companion \textbf{DRanking 2.0} verifiable distributed ranking and
admission protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}.

\begin{definition}[Rings 1.0 Service Receipt (\code{ServiceReceipt})]\label{def:service-receipt}
A service receipt is a dual-authenticated tuple
\[
  \rho=\bigl\langle\mathsf{ver},\,\mathsf{srv},\,a,\,b,\,k,\,n,\,d_{\mathrm{req}},\,d_{\mathrm{rsp}},\,\sigma_a,\,\sigma_b\bigr\rangle,
\]
implemented on live probe transcripts with distinct provider/beneficiary tags (\texttt{rings-core:\allowbreak service-receipt:\allowbreak provisional:\allowbreak\{provider,\allowbreak beneficiary\}}) and bridging into DRanking~2.0 under the finalized epoch separator $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}service\mbox{-}receipt\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}$, where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mathsf{srv}\in\{\mathsf{relay},\mathsf{store},\mathsf{probe}\}$ names the metered service kind;
\item $a\in R$ is the service provider DID and $b\in R$ ($b\neq a$) is the served beneficiary DID;
\item $k\in\mathbb{N}$ is the epoch index and $n$ is a fresh per-epoch nonce;
\item $d_{\mathrm{req}},d_{\mathrm{rsp}}$ are the cryptographic digests of the exact signed request and completion transactions; and
\item $\sigma_a,\sigma_b$ verify under the distinct account keys of $a$ and $b$ over $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}service\mbox{-}receipt\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}\Vert\allowbreak\mathsf{ver}\Vert\allowbreak\mathsf{srv}\Vert\allowbreak a\Vert b\Vert k\Vert n\Vert d_{\mathrm{req}}\Vert d_{\mathrm{rsp}}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[DRanking 2.0 Bridge Morphism~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}]\label{prop:dranking-bridge}
Because forging $\sigma_b$ for an honest beneficiary $b\notin\mathsf{Adv}$ is
cryptographically infeasible, manufacturable receipts are restricted to
adversarial self-dealing ($b\in\mathsf{Adv}$).  In the companion DRanking 2.0
protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}, distinct-nonce receipts $(k,n)$ for pair $(a,b)$
fold into a cap-budgeted
receipt matrix $W_k[b,a]\le w_{\max}$ with normalized stochastic flow
$C_k[b,a]=W_k[b,a]/\max(w_{\max},W_k[b,\cdot]\mathbf{1})$. Assuming honest seed
support ($\operatorname{supp}(s)\subseteq H$), uniformly bounded attenuation
($\sup_k\alpha_k\le\alpha<1$), and initial adversarial weight
$T_0(\mathsf{Adv})\le\frac{\alpha g}{1-\alpha}$, the
seed-anchored attenuated PageRank/EigenTrust~\cite{PageRank,EigenTrust} epoch
evolution preserves
\[
  t_{k+1}=\alpha_k\,C_k^{\mathsf{T}}t_k+(1-\alpha_k)\,s,
\]
\[
  T_k(\mathsf{Adv})=\sum_{v\in\mathsf{Adv}}t_k(v)\;\le\;\frac{\alpha g}{1-\alpha}
\]
for all $k\ge 0$,
where $g=\sup_k\sum_{b\in H,a\in\mathsf{Adv}}C_k[b,a]t_k(b)$ is the honest
endorsement leakage.  Soulbound rank $\hat{t}_k(v)$ then weights VRF committee
sortition and is folded across epochs via Nova-family Incrementally Verifiable
Computation (IVC)~\cite{Nova} so browser nodes verify global admission state in
$O(1)$ time.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

Four methodological boundaries qualify the empirical and verification results reported in this section:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Single-host local execution environment:} Throughput and durability benchmarks ran on one Apple M1 Max workstation using in-memory overlay simulations, local APFS \code{F\_FULLFSYNC} barriers, and a 5-node loopback WebRTC/SCTP harness; geo-distributed WAN latency, asymmetric packet loss, hairpinned NAT traversal, and multi-browser Wasm/IndexedDB variance are deferred to multi-host testbed evaluation.
\item \emph{Static-ring scaling vs.\ continuous live churn:} Figure~\ref{fig:finger-scaling} quantifies range-proved convergence on fixed $N$-node rings up to $N=1\,024$; under sustained high churn, overlapping epoch invalidations require range revalidation (Section~\ref{sec:finger-convergence}) alongside the bridge delegation fallback (Theorem~\ref{theo:bridge-safety}).
\item \emph{Non-transitive clique capacity:} Passive 1-hop \code{TopoInfo} hints guarantee $100\%$ cross-clique delivery without loops for $C_{\mathrm{sub}}\le K=4$, degrading gracefully with $0\%$ loops beyond $2K=8$ articulation peers (Table~\ref{tab:two-clique}); larger isolated cliques require multiple bridge nodes or TURN relay fallback.
\item \emph{Finite-state model-checking bounds:} Stateright/BFS verification exhaustively checks bounded configurations and finite queue depths (Table~\ref{tab:formal-verification}), whereas unbounded majority Sybil capture and economic equilibrium fall outside Rings~1.0 and rely on the companion DRanking~2.0 admission layer (Section~\ref{sec:trust}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Structured Overlays and Non-Transitive NAT Routing.}
Distributed hash tables give Rings its closest technical ancestry. IPFS and libp2p adopt Kademlia-derived XOR routing~\cite{Kademlia,IPFS,Libp2p}, while Veilid~\cite{Veilid2023} and I2P unify XOR DHTs with encrypted safety/private routes or garlic tunnels across desktop and browser/Wasm runtimes; all excel at content-addressed or tunneled lookup but lack Chord's clockwise interval owner law required for affine replica sets, age-gated chain custody, and contiguous successor-list maintenance. Rings adopts Chord's circular geometry and range lemma~\cite{Chord,Stoica2003}, builds directly on Zave's CorrectChord safety substrate~\cite{Zave2015,ZaveChordCorrect}, applies Bamboo-style exponential timeout backoff~\cite{Rhea2004} to stabilize slow WebRTC peers, and solves non-transitive NAT/firewall cliques~\cite{Freedman2005,Ford2006} without unstructured gossip or virtual fingers~\cite{Bryan2008,RFC6940} via bounded multi-predecessors and single-crossing bridge delegation.

\textbf{Relay Mixnets, Wide-Block Ciphers, and Typed Pipelines.}
Existing anonymous networks decouple routing from structured ownership: Tor provides low-latency telescoping circuits without fixed packet padding~\cite{TorNetwork,Degabriele2018}, whereas Mixminion, Loopix, and Nym introduce Sphinx packets, SURBs, and Poisson/constant cover loops over dedicated mix directories~\cite{Chaum1981,Danezis2003,Danezis2009,Piotrowska2017,NymNetwork}. Rings unifies Sphinx fixed-length headers, client-sealed Loopix loops, and AEZ~v5 wide-block encryption~\cite{Hoang2015,AEZv5,Anderson1996} into its DHT as an Arrow/Kleisli effect pipeline~\cite{Moggi1991,Plotkin2003,Plotkin2009,Hughes2000,Lindley2011,Mokhov2019} whose hops discover $\Sigma$-algebraic service descriptors via the ring itself.

\textbf{Conflict-Free Storage, Crash Consistency, and Browser Runtimes.}
On storage and durability, Rings extends state-based and delta-state CRDTs~\cite{ShapiroCRDT,Almeida2018,Preguica2018} and Hybrid Logical Clocks~\cite{Kulkarni2014,Fidge1988} with factored removal separation, per-writer Merkle provenance, linearizable pure-read caches~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}, and platform-verified crash barriers~\cite{Pillai2014,AppleFsync,MoveFileEx,IndexedDB3} tailored to asymmetric browser churn~\cite{BlakeRodrigues,webrtc-standard,webassembly}.
