\section{Introduction}

Centralized application platforms simplify deployment, but they also concentrate
identity, naming, storage, and traffic control at service operators.  Cloud and
platform centralization therefore becomes a privacy, availability, and autonomy
risk for users who need direct communication and durable control over their
data~\cite{Kshetri2013,Zissis2012,KshetriMurugesan2013}.  The Rings project is motivated by
this observation: a sovereign network should let a participant own an identity,
establish a session, route to another identity or resource, and run application
protocols without turning a single application server into the root of
authority.

Rings is implemented as a network, but this paper is organized around the
algebraic structure that makes the implementation coherent.  It states the
ideal carrier, ownership, transition, durability, admission, and privacy laws
that the Rings 1.0 implementation realizes.  The older intuition was that
Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) directly form a finite ring suitable for all
routing and cryptographic operations.  The refined model is more precise: Chord
identifiers form an additive circular carrier for placement, interval order,
replica selection, and finger targets; threshold schemes, signatures,
encryption, wide-block onion encipherment, and zero-knowledge protocols use
their own fields or groups.

The resulting design has three practical consequences.  First, DIDs and virtual
DIDs (VIDs) share the same 160-bit placement surface, so identities, mailboxes,
service descriptors, chunks, and application rendezvous points can be
routed by one owner function.  Second, Rings builds on Chord and CorrectChord
rather than replacing the overlay literature: successor lists, multi-predecessor
relations, join, rectify, stabilize, range-proved finger convergence, and
passive 1-hop gap-bridge delegation are the maintenance substrate on which
Rings semantics are layered~\cite{Chord,Stoica2003,ZaveChordCorrect,Freedman2005}.
Third, state transitions return typed effects instead of directly performing
network IO, which allows the overlay, storage, durability, load-accounting, and
onion-pipeline state machines to be reasoned about purely, model-checked with
Stateright~\cite{Stateright}, and then interpreted by browser or native runtimes.

\section{Problem Statement and Design Goals}

Rings addresses a narrow but recurring gap between existing decentralized
systems.  Kademlia-derived systems provide scalable lookup, but the XOR metric
does not by itself give Rings the circular ownership law needed for successor
lists, replicated ownership, and interval-based placement.  Relay networks and
mixnets improve metadata resistance, but they are not structured P2P ownership
systems.  Blockchains provide global ordering, but embedding consensus in every
message or storage operation would turn Rings into a ledger rather than an
algebraic structured peer-to-peer substrate.

The problem is therefore to specify a network object
\[
  \mathcal{Rings}
\]
that simultaneously supports:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item self-certifying identity and delegated sessions;
\item structured lookup, range-proved finger convergence, and non-transitive WebRTC routing;
\item browser-native and native transport and crash-durable storage surfaces;
\item CRDT storage with tombstone stability, per-writer cryptographic provenance, and replicated relay custody;
\item Sybil-resistant slot accounting, credit reclaim, and bounded load under free identities;
\item Sphinx/AEZ-v5 onion Kleisli pipelines and counterparty-signed service receipts.
\end{enumerate}

This paper makes the following contributions.  It presents Rings as an
algebraic structured peer-to-peer network, modeling the identifier space as the
additive cyclic group \(R=\mathbb{Z}/2^{160}\mathbb{Z}\) and lifting resource
placement into \(\mathbf{Set}/R\).  It specifies range-proved finger
convergence and bounded multi-predecessor non-transitive Chord routing over
restricted WebRTC graphs (Section~\ref{sec:chord}).  It establishes the
removal-stability law, HLC data provenance, factored carrier eviction, and
signed lease heartbeats for replicated CRDT storage (Section~\ref{sec:storage}).
It defines the crash-meaning authority lattice $C\times M$, presence markers,
per-platform crash durability, group commit, link-local replay windows, and
age-gated replicated relay custody (Section~\ref{sec:durable}).  It proves
fair slot accounting across five shared node tables, credit reclaim, and
single-charge chunk reassembly under free identities (Section~\ref{sec:load}).
It replaces legacy relay framing with client-sealed Sphinx/AEZ-v5 onion Kleisli
pipelines (Section~\ref{sec:privacy}), bridges 1.0 service receipts into the
companion DRanking 2.0 protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026} (Section~\ref{sec:trust}),
and summarizes the Stateright model-checking and analytical complexity bounds
across all Rings 1.0 subsystems (Section~\ref{sec:verification}).

\subsection{Carrier and Overlay Model}

\begin{assumption}[System scope]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathsf{fault}&=&\mathsf{fail\mbox{-}stop},\\
\mathsf{auth}(m,\sigma,k)&=&V(k,\sigma,m),\\
\mathsf{net}&=&\mathsf{async},\\
\mathsf{io}&=&I_X:A_X^*\to\mathsf{IO},\\
\mathsf{consensus}&\notin&\mathsf{Overlay},\\
\mathsf{order}&=&\mathsf{SidecarWitness},\\
\mathsf{crypto\_ops}&\cap&\mathsf{route\_ops}=\varnothing.
\end{array}
\]
The fail-stop clause scopes overlay maintenance; identity, storage provenance,
load accounting, onion routing, ranking, and application protocols carry
explicit adversarial predicates at their own layers.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Identifier carrier]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
M&=&2^{160},\\
R&=&\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z},\\
\delta_a(x)&=&(x-a)\bmod M,\\
(a,b]_R&=&\{x\in R:0<\delta_a(x)\leq\delta_a(b)\},\\
\mathsf{RouteOps}_R&=&\{0,+,-,\delta_a,(\_,\_]_R\},\\
\mathsf{Mult}_R&\notin&\mathsf{RouteOps}_R.
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Live topology]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  N&\subset&R,\quad N\neq\varnothing,\\[2pt]
  \operatorname{owner}_N(k)
    &=&\arg\min_{u\in N}\delta_k(u),\\[6pt]
  \operatorname{pred}_N(n)
    &=&\arg\min_{p\in N\setminus\{n\}}\delta_p(n),\\[6pt]
  \operatorname{Succ}^q_N(n)
    &=&
    \operatorname{take}_q
    \bigl(\operatorname{sort}_{\delta_n}(N\setminus\{n\})\bigr),\\[6pt]
  \delta_a|_N&=&\mathsf{injective}.
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Correct-Chord stable base]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
r&=&|\operatorname{Succ}^r_N(n)|,\\
\mathsf{Steady}(N)&\Rightarrow&|N|\geq r+1,\\
|N|<r+1&\Rightarrow&\mathsf{Init}(N),\\
\mathsf{Maintain}&=&
  \{\mathsf{join},\mathsf{rectify}\}\\
&&\cup\{\mathsf{stabilize}\}_{CorrectChord}.
\end{array}
\]
This is the CorrectChord stable base of Zave~\cite{Zave2015,ZaveChordCorrect}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Affine replica set]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\rho&\in&\mathbb{N}_{>0},\\
\operatorname{rot}^{\rho}_i(k)
  &=&k+\left\lfloor M\cdot i/\rho\right\rfloor,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Rep}^{\rho}_N(k)
  &=&
  \{\operatorname{owner}_N(\operatorname{rot}^{\rho}_i(k)):
    0\leq i<\rho\}.
\end{array}
\]
Storage redundancy uses affine rotations of the entry key ($\rho=6$ by default
for data topics).  Successor lists remain the CorrectChord safety root for
topology maintenance.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Rings overlay object]
\[
  \mathcal{R}_N=
  (R,N,\operatorname{owner}_N,\operatorname{pred}_N,
   \operatorname{Succ}^r_N,\operatorname{Rep}^{\rho}_N,F,E),
\]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
F&\subseteq&N\times\mathbb{N}\times N,\\
E&=&\prod_{v\in VID}E_v,\\
\mathsf{SafetyRoot}(\mathcal{R}_N)&=&
  (\operatorname{pred}_N,\operatorname{Succ}^r_N),\\
\mathsf{PerfWitness}(\mathcal{R}_N)&=&F.
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Rotation equivariance]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  t\in R,\quad N+t=\{n+t:n\in N\}
  &\Rightarrow\\
  \operatorname{owner}_{N+t}(k+t)=\operatorname{owner}_N(k)+t.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \forall n\in N.\quad
  \delta_{k+t}(n+t)
  &=((n+t)-(k+t))\bmod M\\
  &=\delta_k(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{constraint}[Carrier separation]
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathsf{PlacementOps}&=&
  \{0,+,-,\delta_a,(a,b]_R\}\\
&&\cup\{\operatorname{owner}_N,\operatorname{Rep}^r_N\},\\
\mathsf{CryptoOps}&=&
  \{\times,^{-1},\cdot_{scalar},\mathsf{interp}\}\\
&&\cup\{\mathsf{pair},\mathsf{subgroup},\mathsf{AEZ}\},\\
\mathsf{PlacementOps}&\cap&\mathsf{CryptoOps}=\varnothing,\\
\mathsf{CryptoCarrier}&\in&\{\mathbb{F}_q,\mathbb{G},\mathbb{Z}_q,\{0,1\}^*\}.
\end{array}
\]
\end{constraint}

  \mathsf{let}\ f(x)=(y,\beta)\ \mathsf{in}\ (y,\alpha\cdot\beta).
\end{array}
\]
\end{definition}

\[
  (h^\star\circ g^\star)\circ f^\star
  =
  h^\star\circ(g^\star\circ f^\star).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\(((\alpha\cdot\beta)\cdot\gamma) = (\alpha\cdot(\beta\cdot\gamma))\).
\end{proof}

\end{definition}

\subsection{Base Chord and CorrectChord Maintenance}

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathsf{Base}&=&\mathsf{Chord},\\
\mathsf{Maintenance}&=&\mathsf{CorrectChord},\\
\mathsf{Object}&=&\mathcal{R}_N,\\
\mathsf{Witnesses}&=&

ring-safety maintenance law~\cite{Chord,ZaveChordCorrect}, then extends the
pure overlay state machine (\code{PeerRing}) with range-proved finger convergence and
non-transitive WebRTC routing.

\subsection{Range-Proved Finger Convergence}\label{sec:finger-convergence}

Classical Chord refreshes one random finger slot per stabilization period,
requiring $\Theta(m)$ periods ($m=160$) to converge and failing to distinguish
unverified neighbor hints from proved entries~\cite{Stoica2003}.  Because
Rings nodes include browsers whose WebRTC handshakes take seconds to minutes or
fail, \code{rings-core} (\code{dht::finger::convergence}) specifies finger maintenance as
a pure transition $\mathrm{step}:(S,\mathrm{Event})\to(S,\mathrm{Actions})$
built on the Chord Range Lemma and four operational disciplines.

\begin{lemma}[Chord Range Lemma~\cite{Chord}]\label{lem:range}
For any node $n\in R$, target $x\in R$, and live membership view $V\subseteq R$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{succ}_V(x)=y
  \iff\;&
  \bigl(y\in(n,x]\ \lor\ \forall p\in(n,y):p\notin V\bigr)\\
  &\text{with}\ [x,y)\cap V=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\]
Specifically, for slot $i\in[0,L)$ ($L\le 160$) with target $t_i=n+2^i$, let
$s=\operatorname{succ}_V(t_i)$ and $d=\delta_n(s)$.  Then
\[
  \forall j\ge i:\quad 2^j\le d \implies \operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=s.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By definition of $\operatorname{succ}_V(t_i)$, $[t_i,s)\cap V=\varnothing$.  For
any $j\ge i$ with $2^j\le d$, we have $2^i\le 2^j\le d$, so $t_j\in[t_i,s]$,
and the first member of $V$ clockwise from $t_j$ is $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Range proof]\label{def:range-proof}
A lookup for slot $i$ answered by $s$ yields a range proof
$\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\operatorname{end})$, where $\mathrm{id}$ is the
lookup UUID~\cite{RFC9562}, $e_\pi$ is the evidence epoch at issue, and
\[
\operatorname{end}(i,s)=
\begin{cases}
L-1, & s=n,\\
\min\bigl(L-1,\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(s)\rfloor\bigr), & 2^i\le\delta_n(s),\\
\mathsf{Invalid}, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Proof $\pi$ covers the contiguous slot interval $[i,\operatorname{end}]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Lookups per pass]\label{cor:lookups}
If the true successor values partition the $L$ slots into $R$ maximal runs of
equal successor, a complete convergence pass requires at most $R\le\min(L,|V|)$
routed lookups; under uniform identifier placement, $\mathbb{E}[R]=O(\log |V|)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Local Range Confirmation]\label{lem:local-range}
Let $h=\operatorname{head}(n)$ be the first clockwise successor of $n$.  When
$n$ receives an authenticated stabilization report from $h$ echoing $n$'s
active stabilization token and stating $\operatorname{pred}(h)=n$, then
\[
  \forall j:\quad 2^j\le\delta_n(h) \implies \operatorname{succ}_V(t_j)=h.
\]
Applying local confirmation sets $\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\top$ for all
$j\le\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor$ with zero routed network lookups; if an
active attempt's slot lies in that range, it is superseded via
$\textsc{Progress}(\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Monotone Evidence Epochs]\label{def:evidence-epochs}
Each node $n$ maintains an evidence state $(e,(\mathsf{chg}_j,\mathsf{ver}_j)_{j<L})$,
where $e=e(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}$ is the monotone topology epoch~\cite{Fidge1988},
$\mathsf{chg}_j\in\mathbb{N}$ is the epoch at which hint $\mathsf{hint}_j$ last
changed, and $\mathsf{ver}_j\in\{\bot,\top\}$ records whether slot $j$ holds a
range-proved peer.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Hint change, slot-wise admission, and discontinuity]\label{law:evidence-epochs}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Hint change:} For any topology transition $H\to H'$ with changed
slots $C=\{j:H_j\neq H'_j\}\neq\varnothing$, advance $e\gets e+1$ and set
$(\mathsf{chg}_j,\mathsf{ver}_j)\gets(e,\bot)$ for all $j\in C$.
\item \emph{Slot-wise admission:} Proof $\pi=(i,\mathrm{id},e_\pi,s,\operatorname{end})$
admits slot set $A(\pi)=\{j\in[i,\operatorname{end}]:\mathsf{chg}_j\le e_\pi\}$.
If $A(\pi)=\varnothing$, $\pi$ is rejected as $\mathsf{Stale}$; otherwise, for
every $j\in A(\pi)$, $\mathsf{hint}_j\gets(s=n\ ?\ \bot : s)$ and
$\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\top$.
\item \emph{Discontinuity:} Losing the last successor increments $e\gets e+1$,
clears $\mathsf{ver}_j\gets\bot$ for all $j$, and returns the attempt owner to
$\mathsf{Idle}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Single-Owner Attempt State]\label{def:attempt-owner}
At most one finger lookup attempt $\alpha$ exists at node $n$:
\[
  \alpha \in \mathsf{Idle}
  \ \mid\ \mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)
  \ \mid\ \mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t),
\]
where $\mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)\equiv\mathsf{AwR}((p,u),e_\rho,\tau_r)$ awaits a
lookup report for slot $p$ and request UUID $u$ before monotonic deadline
$t=\tau_r=\mathrm{issued}+T_r$ ($T_r=10\,\text{s}$), and
$\mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t)\equiv\mathsf{AwA}(\pi,\tau_a)$ awaits WebRTC transport
admission of proved peer $y$ at epoch $e$ before lease deadline
$t=\tau_a=\mathrm{deferred}+T_a$ ($T_h=180\,\text{s}$, $T_a=T_h+30\,\text{s}=210\,\text{s}$).
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Attempt consumption and outcomes]\label{law:attempt-outcomes}
A report $(u',p',y')$ consumes $\alpha$ iff either $\alpha=\mathsf{AwR}(u,p,t)\land(u',p')=(u,p)$ or $\alpha=\mathsf{AwA}(u,p,y,e,t)\land(u',p',y')=(u,p,y)$; all other reports are dropped as $\mathsf{Stale}$ without mutating state or failure streak $k\in[0,255]$.  On a consuming report, $(\alpha,c,k,\nu)$ transitions via~\cite{Rhea2004}:
\begin{align*}
\textsc{Fail}(p)&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{run}(p)+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1)),\\
\textsc{Retire}(\operatorname{end})&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{end}+1,\ k+1,\ t+f(k+1)),\\
\textsc{Progress}(\operatorname{end})&:\ (\mathsf{Idle},\ \operatorname{end}+1,\ 0,\ \bot),
\end{align*}
where $\operatorname{run}(p)=\max\{j\ge p:\forall l\in[p,j],\,\mathsf{hint}_l=\mathsf{hint}_p\}$ and $f(k)=\min(2^{\min(k,6)}\,\text{s},\,60\,\text{s})$ with minimum issue spacing $T_{\min}=1\,\text{s}$.
\end{law}

\begin{definition}[Cyclic Slot Selection (\code{next\_fix\_slot})]\label{def:next-fix-slot}
With $h=\operatorname{head}(n)$ and first routable slot $r=\lfloor\log_2\delta_n(h)\rfloor+1$ ($r=0$ if $h=\bot$), let $c\in\mathbb{N}$ be the cyclic cursor, $A=\{j\ge\max(c,r):\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\}$, and $B=\{j\in[r,\max(c,r)):\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\}$.  The selector is
\[
  \operatorname{next\_fix\_slot}(c)=\operatorname{sel}(c)=
  \begin{cases}
  \min A, & A\neq\varnothing,\\
  \min B, & A=\varnothing\land B\neq\varnothing,\\
  \bot, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Starvation freedom]\label{prop:starve}
Let $U$ be the set of routable slots that remain unverified across a sequence
of attempts.  Between any two selections of $u\in U$, every other slot in $U$ is
selected at least once, and periodic revalidation reopens proved ranges whenever
$\alpha=\mathsf{Idle}\land(\neg\exists j\ge r:\neg\mathsf{ver}_j\ \lor\ k>0)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every completed or failed attempt at slot $j$ advances $c$ strictly past $j$
($\operatorname{end}+1>j$ or $\operatorname{run}(j)+1>j$).  Because
$\operatorname{sel}(c)$ scans cyclically from $c$, it visits $U$ in strict
cyclic index order, preventing a single unreachable NAT peer from monopolizing
the single attempt owner.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Report Claims (\code{NotifyPredecessor} / \code{ReportFloor})]\label{law:report-claims}
Stabilization and successor-list synchronization reports are correlated by
two-phase claim tokens $\mathsf{tok}=(h,\mathrm{id},\phi)$ with
$\phi\in\{\mathsf{Req},\mathsf{Proc}\}$.  A report claims its token atomically
by moving $\mathsf{Req}\to\mathsf{Proc}$, dials at most $K+1$ distinct
candidates for stabilization (and at most $K$ for successor sync) re-checking
$\phi=\mathsf{Proc}$ before each dial, and clears $\mathsf{tok}$ on completion
or on any $\mathsf{SuccessorHeadChanged}$ transition (\code{ReportFloor}).
\end{law}

Rather than injecting unstructured neighborhood gossip or virtual fingers~\cite{Bryan2008,RFC8840}
(which would violate the direct-link invariant $\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\subseteq\operatorname{Admitted}(n)$),
Rings resolves non-transitive WebRTC routing with zero extra control frames via
four mechanisms:

\begin{definition}[Extended Topology Carrier and Multi-Predecessors]\label{def:multi-preds}
Each node $n\in V$ maintains
\[
\begin{aligned}
  S_n=\bigl(&n,\,\texttt{succ}_n,\,\texttt{pred}_n,\,\operatorname{Preds}_K(n),\\
  &\texttt{fingers}_n,\,\texttt{bridged}_n,\,\Phi_n,\,\Pi_n\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)=\langle p_1,\dots,p_m\rangle$ ($m\le K$, supporting capacity $K=4$) is ordered by descending clockwise distance $\delta_n(p_1)>\dots>\delta_n(p_m)$ (ascending counter-clockwise distance $\delta_{p_i}(n)$), with primary predecessor $\texttt{pred}_n=p_1$.  All referenced peers satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)
  &=\bigl(\operatorname{set}(\texttt{succ}_n)\cup\operatorname{set}(\operatorname{Preds}_K(n))\\
  &\qquad\cup\operatorname{ran}(\texttt{fingers}_n)\bigr)\setminus\{n\}
  \subseteq\operatorname{Admitted}(n).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Multi-Predecessor Rectification and Candidate Dial Backoff]\label{law:rectify-backoff}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Empty-confirmed commit:} When $r=\operatorname{head}(n)$ remains
routable ($\operatorname{Routable}(n,r)$), $n$ commits $\texttt{Stabilize}(r)$
and emits $\texttt{Notify}(r)$ even if confirmation filtering leaves
$\texttt{confirmed}$ empty.
\item \emph{Candidate dial backoff (\code{DialBackoff}):} Failed or expired
candidate handshakes enter a bounded negative cache $\mathcal{B}_n$ ($B_{\max}=64$)
with exponential cooldown $\beta(f)=\min(\beta_0\cdot 2^{\min(f-1,4)},\,\beta_{\max})$
(configurable from base $\beta_0=5\,\text{s}$ and cap $\beta_{\max}=60\,\text{s}$ up to $30\text{--}300\,\text{s}$), suppressing redundant SDP offers while allowing explicit application connects or successful admission to clear $\mathcal{B}_n(c)$ immediately.
\item \emph{Supersede-on-Closer, Retain-on-Sub-Ring (\code{rectify\_preds}):}
Given geometrically admissible candidate $c$ and primary predecessor $p_1$: if
$p_1=\bot$ or $\delta_n(c)>\delta_n(p_1)$ ($c$ is strictly closer to $n$),
$\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)\gets\langle c\rangle$; if $\delta_n(c)\le\delta_n(p_1)$
(notification from a farther sub-clique predecessor), insert $c$ into
$\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)$ in descending $\delta_n(\cdot)$ order, deduplicate,
and truncate to $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{lemma}[Articulation Retention and Sub-Clique Local Finger Proof]\label{lem:articulation}
On any transitive ring, $\operatorname{Preds}_K(n)$ collapses to the singleton
global predecessor $[\operatorname{pred}_V(n)]$.  At an articulation node $C$
joining $m\le K$ cliques $V_1,\dots,V_m$, $\operatorname{Preds}_K(C)$ retains
all $m$ sub-clique predecessors $p_i=\operatorname{pred}_{V_i}(C)$ within two
stabilization rounds, protecting every bridge link $(p_i,C)$ from eviction and
allowing each $p_i$ to filter $\texttt{reported.preds}$ to
$\texttt{confirmed.pred}=\operatorname{Some}(p_i)$ and prove its local finger
range $[0,\lfloor\log_2\delta_{p_i}(C)\rfloor]$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:local-range}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{law}[Passive 1-Hop Gap-Bridge Hints and Bridge-Delegated Delivery]\label{law:bridge-delivery}
Whenever $n$ processes an authenticated \texttt{TopoInfo} report from
$r\in\texttt{succ}_n$, the unconfirmed peers $U(n,r)=(\operatorname{set}(\texttt{reported.preds})\cup\operatorname{set}(\texttt{reported.succ}))\setminus\{p:\rho_n(p)\}$ update the zero-TTL partial map $\texttt{bridged}_n:\mathsf{Did}\rightharpoonup\mathsf{Did}$ ($|\texttt{bridged}_n|\le 2K^2$, with $p\mapsto r$ iff $r\in\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)\land p\notin\operatorname{Referenced}(S_n)$).
Payload delivery carries $\sigma\in\operatorname{RouteStage}=\operatorname{aim}(\mathsf{Did}_{\bot})\times\mathsf{bridged}(\mathbb{B})$ (fields $(\texttt{via},\texttt{handed\_off})$ with $\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T)=\texttt{via}.\operatorname{unwrap\_or}(T)$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Rule B1 (1-Hop Bridge Target Forwarding):} If $\neg\operatorname{Linked}_n(T)$, $\sigma.\texttt{via}=\bot$, and $\texttt{bridged}_n(T)=\operatorname{Some}(b)$ with $\operatorname{Linked}_n(b)$, forward to $(b,\sigma)$ when $\delta_n(b)<\delta_n(T)$ and to $(b,(\bot,\top))$ when $\delta_n(b)>\delta_n(T)\land\neg\sigma.\texttt{handed\_off}$.
\item \emph{Rule B2 (Gap-Bridge Handoff):} When greedy clockwise routing toward $a=\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T)$ hits a local minimum ($\operatorname{Handoff}(h)$) with $\neg\sigma.\texttt{handed\_off}\land\sigma.\texttt{via}=\bot$, if $\exists(p\mapsto b)\in\texttt{bridged}_n$ with $\operatorname{Linked}_n(b)\land\delta_n(a)\le\delta_n(p)<\delta_n(h)$, delegate across the gap via $(b^*,(\bot,\top))$ minimizing $\delta_n(p)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded Single-Hop Bridge Delegation and Route Safety]\label{theo:bridge-safety}
Without source-routing hop lists on the wire, $\operatorname{delivery\_step}$
over $\operatorname{RouteStage}=\operatorname{aim}(\mathsf{Did}_{\bot})\times\mathsf{bridged}(\mathbb{B})$
preserves all four route-safety invariants on any underlay graph $G=(V,L)$:
(1)~\texttt{Linked} ($\{n_k,n_{k+1}\}\in L$),
(2)~\texttt{NeverOvershoots} ($\delta_{n_k}(n_{k+1})<\delta_{n_k}(\operatorname{aim})$ on every non-crossing hop),
(3)~\texttt{SingleCrossing} and \texttt{Acyclic} (the boolean crossing bit transitions $\bot\to\top$ at most once, visiting no state $(n_k,\sigma_k)$ twice and bounding every route to $\le 2|V|$ hops), and
(4)~all-pairs delivery in $\le 2$ hops ($\le 3$ hops pre-sync) across bridge-connected cliques of size $\le K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Within each fixed stage $\sigma$, $\delta_{n_k}(\operatorname{aim}(\sigma,T))\in\mathbb{N}$
strictly decreases on every non-crossing hop, visiting at most $|V|-1$ nodes.
Rules B1 (second branch) and B2 flip $\texttt{handed\_off}:\bot\to\top$ and
cannot fire when $\texttt{handed\_off}=\top$, so at most one ring-wrap or
gap-bridge crossing occurs per route, yielding $\le 2|V|$ hops total.  In a
two-clique graph $V_1\cup V_2$ sharing bridge $C$, Lemma~\ref{lem:articulation}
places all of $(V_1\cup V_2)\setminus\{C\}$ into $C$'s \texttt{TopoInfo}, so
every $u\in V_i\setminus\{C\}$ learns $\texttt{bridged}_u(v)=\operatorname{Some}(C)$
for $v\in V_j\setminus\{C\}$ and delivers via $u\to C\to v$ in 2 hops.
\end{proof}

\]

\begin{definition}[Biased circular order]
\[
  A\leq_C B
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \delta_C(A)\leq\delta_C(B).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Biased Circular Comparison}

\ElsIf{$a > b$} \Return $B <_C A$
\Else \ \Return $A = B$
\EndIf
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{definition}[Replicated entry algebra and placement invariant]
\[
  \mathcal{E}^{join}_v=(E_v,\sqsubseteq,\sqcup,\bot),\qquad
  \mathcal{E}^{final}_v=(E_v,\mathsf{conflict}_v,\mathsf{finalize}_v),
\]
where $(E_v,\sqsubseteq,\sqcup,\bot)$ is a join-semilattice~\cite{ShapiroCRDT}
($x\sqcup y=y\sqcup x$, $(x\sqcup y)\sqcup z=x\sqcup(y\sqcup z)$, $x\sqcup x=x$,
$x\sqcup\bot=x$), and placement satisfies
\[
  \operatorname{Inv}_{place}\equiv
  \forall \nu,x.\;
  \operatorname{replicas}(H_\nu(x))
  \subseteq
  \operatorname{Rep}^{\rho}_N(H_\nu(x)).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Mailbox and hidden service descriptor]
\[
\begin{aligned}
  mbox(d,e) &= H_M(\mathtt{rings.mailbox}\Vert d\Vert e),\\
  entry_M &= (\eta,exp,proof_s,dst,cipher),\\
  h &= (\nu,\rho,Intro,Relay,Cap,K,exp,rank),\\
  VID(h) &= H_H(h),\qquad \mathsf{Lookup}(h)=P^N_H(h).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Carrier State, HLC Timestamps, and Cryptographic Data Provenance}

\begin{definition}[Data Carrier and HLC Timestamps]\label{def:carrier}
A Rings data-topic carrier (\code{dht::entry}, \code{crdt.rs}) is a 4-tuple
\[
  x=(V,D,\rho,\beta),
\]
where $V:\mathrm{Bytes}\rightharpoonup\mathrm{Dot}$ maps live elements to
addition dots, $D:\mathrm{Digest}\rightharpoonup\mathrm{Dot}$ maps Keccak-256
element digests $h(v)$ to removal dots (tombstones), $\rho\in\mathrm{Ver}_\bot$
is the overwrite floor register, and $\beta\in\mathbb{N}_\bot$ is the carrier
liveness bound.  Each version/dot carries a Hybrid Logical Clock (HLC) timestamp
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{HLC}}=(w,c,o),
\]
ordered lexicographically by wall-clock time $w=\tau(d)$, logical counter
$c\in\mathbb{N}$, and writer/origin DID $o\in R$.  A removal $(e,r)\in D$
\emph{covers} an addition $(v,d)\in V$ iff $h(v)=e\land d\le r$.  A carrier $x$
is \emph{normalized} iff no addition in $V$ is covered by $D$ or $\le\rho$, and
no removal in $D$ is $\le\rho$.  The join $x\sqcup y$ is pointwise $\max$
followed by normalization.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Per-Writer Cryptographic Provenance (\code{DataProvenance})]\label{law:provenance}
Every element addition (\code{Append} / \code{Extend}, \issue{1179}) and removal
tombstone (\code{Tombstone}, \issue{1220}) carries a per-writer cryptographic
provenance proof $\pi_{\mathrm{prov}}\in\mathsf{DataProvenance}$ signed over the
canonical domain separator $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}data\mbox{-}carrier\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{Verify}\bigl(&k_o,\sigma_o,\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}data\mbox{-}carrier\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}\\
  &\Vert\mathsf{op}\Vert k_{\mathrm{topic}}\Vert e\Vert(w,c,o)\bigr)=\top,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\mathsf{op}\in\{\mathsf{Add},\mathsf{Remove}\}$, $k_{\mathrm{topic}}\in R$
is the placement key, $e=h(v)$ is the element digest, and $o=D(k_o)$ matches the
HLC origin DID.  Unauthenticated deltas or clock skews exceeding $c+\sigma$ are
rejected at admission.
\end{law}

\subsection{Removal Stability Horizon, Factored Carrier, and Tombstone Bounds}

\begin{definition}[Stability Horizon and Retirement]\label{def:stability}
With element retention horizon $H=86\,400\,\text{s}$ (configurable down to
$6\cdot 10^6\,\text{ms}$ on fast-expiring topics) and clock-skew tolerance
$\sigma=300\,\text{s}$ ($3\,000\,\text{ms}$ under tight NTP), a removal $(e,r)$
(or floor $\rho$) is \emph{stable} at clock $t$ iff
\[
  t\ge\tau(r)+H+\sigma.
\]
The retirement projection $\operatorname{retire}_t(x)$ drops additions with
$t\ge\tau(d)+H$ and removals/registers with $t\ge\tau(r)+H+\sigma$.
$\operatorname{retire}_t$ is a join-semilattice homomorphism satisfying
$\operatorname{retire}_t\circ\operatorname{retire}_s=\operatorname{retire}_{\max(s,t)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Stable removes cover only retired adds]\label{lem:stable-covers}
If $(e,r)$ is stable at the clock $t_p$ of some node $p$, then every addition
$(v,d)$ covered by $(e,r)$ is already retired at the clock $t_q$ of every node
$q$ at the same instant ($t_q\ge t_p-\sigma\ge\tau(r)+H\ge\tau(d)+H$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{law}[Remove survival and Factored Carrier]\label{law:factored-carrier}
An unstable remove or register leaves a holder's replica only by
(i)~$\operatorname{retire}_t$ at stability,
(ii)~an ack-gated range handoff whose receiver holds it, or
(iii)~a platform loss event---never by storage eviction, cap, or truncation.
To enforce this, every normalized carrier $x$ factors into an element side and
a remove side:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  x &= \mathsf{E}(x)\sqcup\mathsf{R}(x),\\
  \mathsf{E}(x) &= (V,\varnothing,\bot,\beta),\qquad
  \mathsf{R}(x) = (\varnothing,D,\rho,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
$\mathsf{E}(x)$ is stored in the data store at authority $(\mathsf{Cch},\mathsf{Reb})$,
whereas $\mathsf{R}(x)$ is persisted in the dedicated \emph{removal store}
(\code{dht-removals}) at $(\mathsf{Dur},\mathsf{Ret})$ (\code{RecordAuthority::Retained}).
Byte-budget eviction maps $x\mapsto\mathsf{R}(x)$ (dropping only $\mathsf{E}(x)$),
and $\mathrm{put}(k,x)$ flushes $\mathsf{R}(x)$ before writing $\mathsf{E}(x)$.
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[No Resurrection]\label{theo:no-resurrection}
Under Law~\ref{law:factored-carrier}, if holder $p$ holds $(e,r)$ at $t_0$ and
experiences no loss event before $\tau(r)+H+\sigma$, then across any sequence of
joins, evictions, and crashes, no read at $p$ after $t_0$ ever serves an
addition covered by $(e,r)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Before stability ($t<\tau(r)+H+\sigma$), Law~\ref{law:factored-carrier} keeps
$D_p(e)\ge r$ in the durable removal store across evictions and crashes, so
$\mathrm{get}(k)=\mathsf{E}_k\sqcup\mathsf{R}_k$ normalizes away any covered
addition.  At and after stability, Lemma~\ref{lem:stable-covers} guarantees that
every covered addition has $t\ge\tau(d)+H$ and is stripped by
$\operatorname{retire}_t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Tombstone Bound (\code{Storage Tombstone Full})]\label{law:tombstone-bound}
Each data carrier is bounded by $T_{\max}=D_{\max}=\mathtt{ENTRY\_DATA\_MAX\_LEN}=1024$
removes.  Any peer delta $\delta$ with $|D_\delta|>T_{\max}$ is inadmissible,
and any operation or join whose retired result $y=\operatorname{retire}_t(x\sqcup\delta)$
has $|D_y|>T_{\max}$ is refused with $\mathsf{Err}(\mathsf{Exhausted})$
(\code{Storage Tombstone Full}), leaving the stored carrier unchanged.  Every
admitted carrier encodes in at most $W=w_0+D_{\max}(P_{\max}+\lambda+w_D)+T_{\max}w_T+w_\rho\approx 33.83\,\text{MB}\le\mathtt{TRANSPORT\_MAX\_SIZE}$ ($60\,\text{MB}$), and any admissible sync delta simulates a sequence of $\le 2|D_\delta|+|V_\delta|+1$ writer operations.
\end{law}

\subsection{Fetch Cache Linearization and Signed Lease Heartbeats}

\begin{law}[Fetch Cache Pure-Read Linearization (Law 6)]\label{law:cache-linear}
The local fetch cache $c:K\rightharpoonup X$ is a $(\mathsf{Vol},\mathsf{Reb})$
mirror whose mutations ($\mathrm{put},\mathrm{obs},\mathrm{evict}$) execute
atomically under the cache lock while reads are pure projections
$t\mapsto\operatorname{retire}_t(c[k])$ that perform zero store writes or
deletions.  Consequently, every cache transition linearizes~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}
at its store put and every read at its store get, eliminating concurrent
read-retire lost-update races (\issue{922}).
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Signed Lease Heartbeats (\code{LeaseHeartbeat})]\label{law:lease-heartbeat}
To eliminate the $\approx 200$ tombstones and $O(N^2)$ read traffic generated
when service registrants overwrite timestamped descriptors every $I=30\,\text{s}$
(\issue{918}), a registrant signs a fixed lease descriptor $d$ over domain
$\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}lease\mbox{-}heartbeat\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}$ binding its
identity, process epoch, capabilities, freshness window $T=90\,\text{s}$, and
lease expiration $\ell=t+\Lambda$ ($\Lambda=10\,\text{min}$).  Each heartbeat at
clock $t$ emits only $\mathrm{Extend}([d])$ (writing zero tombstones and
performing zero registry reads), rotating $d\mapsto d'$ only when $t+2I\ge\ell$
or capabilities change.  Readers at clock $t$ accept the verified descriptor of
greatest $\ell$ satisfying $\ell\le t+\Lambda+\sigma$, $T\le\Lambda$, $t\le\ell$,
and $t\le\tau^*(d)+T+\sigma$.
\end{law}

\section{Durable Local Stores and Replicated Custody}\label{sec:durable}

\subsection{Authority Lattice, Presence Markers, and Platform Crash Durability}

A Rings node persists replay windows, onion entry guards, removal records, and
relay mail across native file systems and browser IndexedDB backends.  We unify
their presence and crash-survival guarantees via a product authority lattice
(\code{RecordAuthority})~\cite{Pillai2014}.

\begin{definition}[Crash-Meaning Authority Lattice $C\times M$]\label{def:authority-lattice}
The crash chain $C=\mathsf{Vol}\sqsubset\mathsf{Cch}\sqsubset\mathsf{Dur}$ and
meaning chain $M=\mathsf{Reb}\sqsubset\mathsf{Ret}\sqsubset\mathsf{Aut}$ form
the componentwise lattice $C\times M$ with monotone obligation map
$O(c,m)=O(c)\cup O(m)$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  O(\mathsf{Vol}) &= O(\mathsf{Cch})=\varnothing,\\
  O(\mathsf{Dur}) &= \{\text{Atomicity (A)},\,\text{Survival (D)}\},\\
  O(\mathsf{Reb}) &= \varnothing,\qquad O(\mathsf{Ret})=\{\text{No-Eviction (N)}\},\\
  O(\mathsf{Aut}) &= \{\text{N},\,\text{Witnessed-Presence (W)},\,\text{Fail-Closed (F)}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the Admissibility Law, a store may open at $(c,m)$ only if $c\sqsupseteq\operatorname{req}(m)$, where $\operatorname{req}(\mathsf{Reb})=\mathsf{Vol}$ and $\operatorname{req}(\mathsf{Ret})=\operatorname{req}(\mathsf{Aut})=\mathsf{Dur}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Presence Markers (\code{.p}) and Per-Platform Crash Durability]\label{law:platform-durability}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Presence markers (\code{.p}):} In an authoritative native store, each
record $n=\mathrm{hex}(\mathrm{SHA1}(k))$ has an empty witness marker $n.\mathtt{p}$
created and directory-synced only after $n.\mathtt{tmp}\to n$ is flushed, and
removed before $n$ on deletion, maintaining invariant $P\subseteq R$.  At
restore, any $n\in P\setminus R$ is classified as $\mathsf{Missing}(n)$ and fails
stream $n$ closed (discharging W for every partial loss $\lambda_{\mathrm{part}}$,
whereas total directory loss $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is provably undetectable by
any in-domain witness and serves as the documented operator reset).
\item \emph{macOS / iOS:} File data and directory entries are flushed via
$\mathtt{fcntl}(\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC})$~\cite{AppleFsync}.
\item \emph{Windows:} Temporary files flush via $\mathtt{FlushFileBuffers}$ and
all directory mutations execute via $\mathtt{MoveFileExW}$ with
$\mathtt{MOVEFILE\_REPLACE\_EXISTING}\mid\mathtt{MOVEFILE\_WRITE\_THROUGH}$~\cite{MoveFileEx}.
\item \emph{Linux / Unix:} Data files flush via $\mathtt{fsync}$ followed by
parent directory $\mathtt{fsync}$ after atomic rename~\cite{Pillai2014}.
\item \emph{Browsers (IndexedDB):} Read-write transactions at $\mathsf{Dur}$
open with explicit $\{\mathit{durability}:\mathtt{"strict"}\}$ (forcing
OS buffer flush / SQLite \texttt{EXTRA} or full WAL checkpoint across Chromium,
Gecko, and WebKit~\cite{IndexedDB3,ChromeDurability,GeckoIDB,WebKitIDB,SQLiteWAL})
combined with $\mathtt{navigator.storage.persist()}$~\cite{MDNEviction}.  Because
browsers delete origin storage whole ($P=\varnothing$) and ephemeral contexts can
only claim durability ($\mathsf{Dur}^\circ$), node-local stores open at
$(\mathsf{Dur}^\circ,m)$ with session end as $\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$, while
custody of other nodes' mail requires $r\ge 2$ replicated holders.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\subsection{Group Commit and Link-Local Volatile Replay Windows}

\begin{law}[Group Commit (\code{put*})]\label{law:group-commit}
Replay transitions $t_1<t_2<\dots$ execute in lock order on speculative tables
$\hat{T}$ with undo log entries $u_i$ and coalesce into batches $B_j$ written by
a single $\mathrm{put}^*(W_j)$: on native targets, all $n.\mathtt{tmp}$ files are
pushed before a single drive-cache barrier ($\mathtt{F\_FULLFSYNC}$), renamed,
and directory-synced once; on IndexedDB, all puts share one \texttt{strict}
read-write transaction.  Callers are released only after $\mathrm{put}^*(W_j)=\mathsf{Ok}$
(latency $\le 2\phi$); on $\mathsf{Err}$, $\hat{T}$ rolls back via $u_i$ to the
last durable state $T$.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Link-Local Volatile Replay Windows ($\mathcal{W}_{s,g}$)]\label{law:link-local}
Direct neighbor control traffic (\code{Join|Sync|Leave|Send}) and application
namespaces registered in $D_r$ as delegating replay (such as Sphinx onion cells,
which enforce at-most-once admission in their own Bloom filter $R_i$ and process
epoch $e_i$) are classified by $\mathrm{ll}(t,c)$ on authenticated connection
$c$ of live generation $g$ and admitted against an in-memory per-generation
window $\mathcal{W}_{s,g}$ instead of the durable store $\mathcal{W}_s$.
This eliminates durable disk flushes on the onion/control hot path while
preserving per-generation duplicate/fork detection and cross-restart replay
immunity.
\end{law}

\subsection{Receiver Custody Set and Age-Gated Replicated Relay Custody}

\begin{definition}[Receiver Custody Set ($\mathcal{K}_s$)]\label{def:custody-set}
To prevent relay mail admitted into $\mathcal{W}_s$ from being lost if the node
restarts before the application consumes it from the volatile inbox (\issue{936}),
the receiver record of stream $s$ co-persists $(\mathcal{W}_s,\mathcal{K}_s)$
atomically in a single $(\mathsf{Dur},\mathsf{Aut})$ record, where
$\mathcal{K}_s\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathrm{Digest}\times\mathbb{N}$ holds
admitted but untaken deliveries $(q,h,x)$ with spent horizon
$x=\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl}+H_I+2\epsilon$ ($|\bigcup_s\mathcal{K}_s|\le\mathtt{RELAY\_INBOX\_MAX\_LEN}=64$).
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Custody Lifecycle and Age-Gated Replicated Relay Chain]\label{law:replicated-custody}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Custody drain and release:} On drain after $\mathrm{put}^*$, an
element $(s,q,h)$ already in $\mathcal{K}_s$ is requeued without re-checking
$\mathcal{W}_s$; a new element commits $(\mathcal{W}'_s,\mathcal{K}_s\cup\{(q,h,x)\})$
before queueing; and the carrier tombstone/Ack is issued only after the
application takes the message and commits $(\mathcal{W}_s,\mathcal{K}_s\setminus\{(q,h,x)\})$.
By information-theoretic indistinguishability, no bounded-window receiver can
safely admit un-custodied mail that has fallen behind live traffic ($q<\operatorname{rmin}(\mathcal{W}_s)$).
\item \emph{Age-gated replicated custody ($\mathcal{M}_r(k)$):} Because browser
holders operate at claimed durability $\mathsf{Dur}^\circ$ and experience high
churn~\cite{BlakeRodrigues}, a node advertises relay custody only after
surviving an online age gate $A_{\min}$ ($A_{\min}=30\,\text{min}$ in production
profiles, or $\ge 4$ stabilization periods).  Each mail key $k$ is replicated
across a chain $\mathcal{M}_r(k)$ of the first $r$ age-gated holders clockwise
from $\operatorname{owner}_N(k)$ ($r=4$ to $r=8$).
\item \emph{Chain replication and digest repair:} Holds forward head-to-tail
along $\mathcal{M}_r(k)$ (digest first, payload on miss) and acknowledge
tail-to-head only after all $q(k)\le r$ members persist the element.  Adjacent
chain members probe liveness every $P_c=2.5\,\text{s}$ (suspect after
$\tau_c=7.5\,\text{s}$) and repair via bundled carrier digest-set comparison on
membership change or periodic pass, restoring $r$ copies within $D\le T_d+r\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\section{Fair Admission, Slot Accounting, and Load Bounds}\label{sec:load}

\subsection{Cleanest-First Slot Roster and Fair Slot Accounting}

Because a Rings DID is a free public-key digest, any node-wide table keyed solely
by origin DID can be exhausted by one adversary minting thousands of identities
(\issue{907}, \issue{1060}).  Rings anchors resource accounting to the
authenticated transport connection slot.

\begin{definition}[Cleanest-First Slot Roster (\code{SlotRoster})]\label{def:slot-roster}
A node's connection registry holds at most
\[
  \mathcal{R}(s)=2(F+s+1)
\]
active neighbour edges, where $F=160$ is the finger table size and $s$ is the
successor capacity ($\mathcal{R}(4)=330$ at $s=4$; $\mathcal{R}(3)=328$ at $s=3$).
The \code{SlotRoster} indexes slots $\kappa\in\{0,\dots,\mathcal{R}(s)-1\}$, tracking
per-slot metadata $(\mathit{epoch},\mathit{strikes},\mathit{last\_seen\_ms})$
and live floor utilization across shared tables $T$:
\[
  w_t(\kappa)=\max_T\frac{\min\bigl(\operatorname{use}^t_{T,\kappa},\,f_T\bigr)}{f_T}.
\]
When a new edge $e$ is admitted, it is assigned the \emph{cleanest} free slot
$\kappa(e)=\arg\min_{\kappa\in F_t}w_t(\kappa)$ (ties broken by least index),
and charges in table $T$ remain bound to $\kappa$ until they expire by $T$'s own
horizon law.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Slot Accounting]\label{law:slot-accounting}
Every shared table $T$ is parameterized by a per-slot guaranteed floor $f_T$, a
shared overflow pool $\mathcal{P}_T$, a local share $L_T$ for principal $\lambda$,
and dedicated shares $S_d$ for $d\in D_T$, yielding the hard node-wide bound
\[
  B_T(s)=\mathcal{R}(s)\,f_T+\mathcal{P}_T+L_T+\sum_{d\in D_T}S_d.
\]
With slot usage $\operatorname{use}_\kappa$, pool overflow
$o_\kappa=\max(0,\operatorname{use}_\kappa-f_T)$, and active claimant set
$\mathcal{A}(t)=\{\kappa:o_\kappa>0\text{ or }\kappa\text{ requests pool overflow}\}$
of size $n(\mathcal{A}(t))=|\mathcal{A}(t)|\ge 1$, an item introduced by slot
$\kappa$ is admitted iff either:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\operatorname{use}_\kappa<f_T$ (within the slot's guaranteed floor), or
\item $\sum_{\kappa'}o_{\kappa'}<\mathcal{P}_T$ and
$o_\kappa<\bigl\lceil\mathcal{P}_T / n(\mathcal{A}(t))\bigr\rceil$ (within the
slot's fair pool claim).
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-Table Slot Isolation]\label{theo:slot-isolation}
Across all five shared node tables---(1)~Application Inbox / Mailbox Ledger,
(2)~Relay Store, (3)~CRDT Removal Store, (4)~Transaction Replay Table, and
(5)~Chunk Reassembly Buffer $O$---Law~\ref{law:slot-accounting} and
Definition~\ref{def:slot-roster} guarantee:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $|T|\le B_T(s)$ in all reachable states;
\item any edge holding slot $\kappa$ with $\operatorname{use}_\kappa<f_T$ is
never refused, regardless of how many origins, edges, or slots other parties
control;
\item an over-floor slot is guaranteed at least
$f_T+\lceil\mathcal{P}_T/\mathcal{R}(s)\rceil$ items as prior claimants expire;
\item under cleanest-first assignment (\code{SlotRoster}), an adversary rotating
Sybil connections cannot degrade a newcomer's floor with a single departure-and-rejoin
(unlike least-free-index selection), requiring $\mathcal{R}(s)-c$ distinct slot
admissions within one horizon $h$ to touch every free slot.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Items (1)--(3) follow because floor checks read $\operatorname{use}_\kappa$
alone, departures never erase slot charges, and claimants above
$\lceil\mathcal{P}_T/n\rceil$ cannot grow while prior items drain.  Item (4)
holds because a free slot with $w_t(\kappa)=0$ is always selected before any
partially charged slot $\kappa'$ with $w_t(\kappa')>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Replay Stream Eviction and Credit Reclaim}

\begin{law}[Replay Stream Spent-Horizon Eviction]\label{law:replay-eviction}
A receiver replay stream $s$ with maximum admitted transaction expiry
$x_s=\max(\mathit{ts}+\mathit{ttl})$ is \emph{spent} at clock $t$ once
$t\ge x_s+\iota+\sigma$ (for default control/storage/E2E traffic, the spent
horizon is $H_{\text{spent}}=600\,\text{s}$; for offline-capable application
streams, $\iota=H_I+\sigma$ where $H_I=7\,\text{d}$).  Because transactions past
$x_s+\iota+\sigma$ are rejected by envelope TTL and relay hold-horizon checks
prior to replay lookup, spent streams holding no custody $\mathcal{K}_s$ are
lazily evicted without reopening replay.  With $f=32$, $\mathcal{P}=3\,328$,
$L=1\,024$, and inbox share $S_\mu=768$, no honest slot can be permanently
locked out.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Credit Reclaim (\code{Credit/Shrink/Returned}) and Hard Frame Bound]\label{law:credit-reclaim}
Each edge has four lanes $\ell$ (one priority control lane with floor
$g_{\mathrm{prio}}=W=16$, and three data lanes with floor $g_\ell=1$ and cap $W=16$).
To bound total in-flight and buffered frames node-wide without dropping frames,
credit frames carry a lane generation $g$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mathsf{Credit}(A,g)$: receiver grants limit $A$ in generation $g$;
\item $\mathsf{Shrink}(L',g)$: receiver reclaims unused pooled credit on an idle
($T_{\mathrm{idle}}=1\,\text{s}$) or over-share lane down to $L'=\min(R+g_\ell,A)$;
\item $\mathsf{Returned}(B,g)$: sender commits at most $B=\max(c,L')$ frames and
ignores older generations.
\end{itemize}
With frame pool $\mathcal{P}_F=256$ frames ($16\,\text{MiB}$), total node frame
liability satisfies the hard bound
\[
  \sum_{e,\ell}\operatorname{liab}(e,\ell)\;\le\;
  B_F(s)=c(s)+W_{\text{shrink}}=\mathcal{R}(s)(W+3)+\mathcal{P}_F
\]
($6\,488$ frames at $s=3$; $6\,526$ at $s=4$), with zero honest frame drops and
guaranteed per-edge pooled throughput $\ge\min(3(W-1),\lfloor\mathcal{P}_F/n\rfloor)$.
\end{law}

\subsection{Single-Charge Chunk Reassembly and Per-Category Barrier}

\begin{law}[Single-Charge Chunk Reassembly and Credit-Time Rate Floor]\label{law:chunk-reassembly}
A chunked message $[0,n]$ of chunk size $d_0$ reserves its full footprint
$m=2n d_0$ \emph{once} on its first chunk in both the mailbox ledger and the open-message
table $O$ ($f_O=256\,\text{KiB}$, $\mathcal{P}_O=128\,\text{MiB}$,
$B_O(s)=\mathcal{R}(s)f_O+\mathcal{P}_O$), waiting in \code{ResourceOrderedQueue}
order ($O((g+k+r)\log n)$ per event) and moving into dispatch at completion
without re-charging.  To prevent slowloris reservation pinning without penalizing
receiver-throttled links, the receiver tracks \emph{credit-time} $\hat{\tau}$,
which advances over $[t_k,t_{k+1})$ by $\min(t_{k+1}-t_k,\,c_k d_0/\rho)$ only
while the sender holds $c_k=A-N>0$ unused credits.  Messages must maintain a
streaming rate floor ($r_{\min}=1024\,\text{B/s}$ to $\rho=10\,\text{B/ms}$)
with grace window $G=15\,\text{s}$ (up to $(W+1)d_0/\rho+\Delta$), expiring
attributably if received bytes fall below $b<\rho\hat{\tau}-W d_0$.
\end{law}

\begin{law}[Per-Category Reassembly Barrier (\code{Control | Interactive | Bulk})]\label{law:category-barrier}
Inbound dispatch maintains independent ordering barriers per traffic category
$\kappa\in\{\mathsf{Control},\mathsf{Interactive},\mathsf{Bulk}\}$ (mapped to
protocol lanes $\{\mathit{ctl},\mathit{sto},\mathit{e2e},\mathit{app}\}$): a
frame $f$ of category $\kappa$ waits only behind unfinished reassembly events
$u\in U$ with $\mathit{seq}(u)<\mathit{seq}(f)\land\kappa(u)=\kappa$.  This
guarantees exact sequence order within every category (including chunked control
frames) while preventing bulk data reassembly from blocking control or
interactive lanes (\issue{927}).
\end{law}

\section{Privacy Layer --- Cryptographic Carriers and Sphinx Onion Kleisli Pipeline}\label{sec:privacy}

\subsection{Separate Cryptographic Carriers}

\begin{definition}[End-to-end carrier, signed frame, and secret sharing]
End-to-end encryption and secret sharing operate over groups $\mathbb{G}$ of
prime order $q$ ($\mathbb{G}\neq R$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:elgamal}) and finite
fields $\mathbb{F}_q[X]$~\cite{ssss}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  f &= (sid,seq,last,cipher,pk_s,\sigma_s),\\
  \operatorname{FrameValid}(f) &\iff V(pk_s,\sigma_s,m_f)=\top\land\operatorname{Session}(D(pk_s),sid,e),\\
  P\in\mathbb{F}_q[X],\quad sh_i &= (i,P(i))\in\mathbb{F}_q^2,\quad
  \operatorname{reconstruct}(\{sh_i\})=P(0).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{ElGamal Encryption and Decryption on $\mathbb{G}$}
\label{alg:elgamal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Encryption}{$m, h=g^x, g$}
\State Sample $k \in \mathbb{Z}_q$;\ \Return $(c_1 = g^k,\ c_2 = m\cdot h^k)$
\EndProcedure
\Procedure{Decryption}{$c_1, c_2, x$}
\State \Return $m = c_2\cdot(c_1)^{-x}$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{End-to-End Chunking Algorithm}
\label{alg:e2e_chunking}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{E2E Chunking}{$data, chunk\_size$}
\State $chunks \gets []$;\ $total \gets \lceil\operatorname{len}(data)/chunk\_size\rceil$
\For{$i \in [0, total)$}
\State append $data[i\cdot chunk\_size : (i+1)\cdot chunk\_size]$ to $chunks$
\EndFor
\State \Return $chunks$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

  \iff

  \land \sum_i |c_i|\leq B_m
  \land now<ttl(m).
\]
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Operation Signature and Kleisli Pipeline Composition}

Rings 1.0 replaces legacy relay framing with the \textbf{Sphinx Onion Kleisli
Pipeline} (\code{rings-node::onion}, \issue{834}), generalizing onion circuits from
simple exit tunnels to client-sealed loops over a static operation signature
$\Sigma$ composed in the Kleisli category $\mathbf{Kl}(M)$~\cite{Danezis2009,Piotrowska2017,Hoang2015,Moggi1991,Plotkin2003,Hughes2000,Lindley2011}.

\begin{definition}[Operation Signature $\Sigma$ and Partial $\Sigma$-Algebras (D1$'$, D2)]\label{def:onion-signature}
The static shape signature is
\[
  \Sigma=\{\mathsf{relay},\mathsf{tcp},\mathsf{https}\}\cup\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}},
\]
where $\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}}=\{\mathsf{invoke}\langle\mathrm{In},\mathrm{Out},W,L\rangle:W\in\mathcal{W},\,L\in\mathcal{L}\}$ spans discrete output-width classes $W(f)$ and latency classes $L(f)$, and invoke backends $\omega\in\Omega$ include the core distributed operations:
\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\code{dht.find\_successor}, \code{dht.notify}, \code{dht.sync},
\code{storage.lookup}, \code{storage.store}, \code{service.lookup},
\code{service.register}, \code{direct.message}.
\end{center}
Each node $n$ publishes signed registrations $\mathrm{Reg}_n\subseteq\{\mathsf{relay},\mathsf{tcp},\mathsf{https}\}\cup(\Sigma_{\mathsf{invoke}}\times\Omega)$ carrying its 128-bit process epoch $e_n$ and quota $\operatorname{quota}(n,f,\omega)$, acting as a partial $\Sigma$-algebra with generic effect~\cite{Plotkin2003,Plotkin2009}
\[
  \llbracket f\rrbracket_n:\mathrm{Args}_f\times\mathrm{In}_f\to M\,\mathrm{Out}_f.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kleisli Pipeline Composition (D4a, D4b, Laws L1, L2)]\label{def:kleisli-pipeline}
A pipeline $P=(f_1,\bar{a}_1)\mathbin{\ggg}\cdots\mathbin{\ggg}(f_n,\bar{a}_n)$
is a well-formed morphism of the free category $\mathcal{F}(G_\Sigma)$ iff
$1\le n\le n_{\max}=4$, $\mathrm{Out}_{f_k}=\mathrm{In}_{f_{k+1}}$ for all
$k<n$, intermediate symbols satisfy $\mathsf{Intermediate}\in\operatorname{pos}(f_k)$,
and world-facing stream symbols ($\mathsf{tcp},\mathsf{https}$) appear only at
$n=1$.  Placed on nodes $h_1,\dots,h_n$, $P$ denotes the Kleisli composite in
$\mathbf{Kl}(M)$:
\[
  \llbracket P\rrbracket(v_0)=
  \bigl(\llbracket f_1\rrbracket_{h_1}(\bar{a}_1,-)\mathbin{\ggg}\cdots\mathbin{\ggg}
  \llbracket f_n\rrbracket_{h_n}(\bar{a}_n,-)\bigr)(v_0),
\]
satisfying \textbf{Law L1 (Identity)}: $\llbracket\mathsf{relay}\rrbracket=\eta$ and
$\operatorname{Loop}(\mathsf{relay}\mathbin{\ggg}P)\overset{d}{=}\operatorname{Loop}(P)$
via normal-form $\operatorname{nf}$ reduction, and \textbf{Law L2 (Associativity)}:
$(f\mathbin{\ggg}g)\mathbin{\ggg}h=f\mathbin{\ggg}(g\mathbin{\ggg}h)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Client-Sealed Loops, Uniform Sphinx Header, and AEZ v5 Carry}

\begin{definition}[Client-Sealed Loop and Hall-Filter Draw Order (D5, Law L7)]\label{def:onion-loop}
For client $\diamond$, entry guard $g$, and $n\in[1,4]$ symbol hops $h_1,\dots,h_n$,
each segment $k\in[0,n]$ inserts $s=2$ relays $r_{k,1},r_{k,2}$ (the minimal $s$
such that no node is adjacent to both $\diamond$ and a symbol hop):
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Loop}(P):\ \diamond&\to g=r_{0,1}\to r_{0,2}\to h_1\to r_{1,1}\to r_{1,2}\\
  &\to\cdots\to h_n\to r_{n,1}\to r_{n,2}=g\to\diamond,
\end{aligned}
\]
with total hops $H(n,s)=(s+1)n+s\le\hat{H}=14$ ($H\in\{5,8,11,14\}$) and $H-1$
distinct peer DIDs (\textbf{Law L7: Guard Closure}).  Route selection draws
$h_1,\dots,h_n$ first, then guard $g$, then remaining relays $r_{k,j}$ using a
bipartite-matching \textbf{Hall filter} $F(\pi,p)$ so that if the first draw succeeds,
no subsequent draw can fail, and exit marginals equal exact quality weights
$q(d)/q(N_{h_1})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[2870-Byte Sphinx Header and 205-Byte Uniform Per-Hop Layer (D6, D6$''$, Laws L3, L4)]\label{def:sphinx-header}
Every hop $i\in[1,H]$ across all roles (\code{HopRole::Transit | Terminal | GuardReturn})
peels an identical 205-byte layer
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \lambda_i &= (f_i,\bar{a}_i,\mathrm{next}_i,e_i,x,\nu_i,\kappa_i,\gamma_{i+1}),\\
  \ell &= 1+64+20+16+8+16+64+16=205\,\text{B},
\end{aligned}
\]
from a fixed-length Sphinx header~\cite{Danezis2009} $\chi_i=\alpha_i\Vert\beta_i\Vert\gamma_i$ with blinded SEC1 group element $|\alpha_i|=33\,\text{B}$, padded routing block $|\beta_i|=\hat{H}\ell=14\times 205=2870\,\text{B}$, and MAC $|\gamma_i|=16\,\text{B}$ ($|\chi_i|=2919\,\text{B}$), where $x=\lceil t_{\mathrm{build}}/Q\rceil Q+X_0$ ($Q=30\,\text{s}$, $X_0=120\,\text{s}$, $V=150\,\text{s}$).
Each loop selects a cell bucket class $b\in[16\,\text{KiB},12\,\text{MiB}]$ with uniform carry slot width $C_b=b-2919\,\text{B}$ ($C_{16\,\text{KiB}}=13\,465\,\text{B}$, $82\%$ payload efficiency, \textbf{Law L3}) and deterministic release time $t^{\mathrm{dep}}_i=t^{\mathrm{arr}}_i+L(f_i)$ (\textbf{Law L4}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[AEZ v5 Wide-Block Body Carry (\code{OnionBodyCipher}, D7)]\label{def:aez-carry}
For each segment $k$, the client draws a 32-byte seed $\sigma_k$ and derives
48-byte AEZ v5 keys~\cite{Hoang2015,AEZv5} $k_{r_{k,j}}$ and $k_{c_k}$ via
HKDF-SHA256.  Producer $p_k$ applies one authenticated wide-block encipherment
$\mathrm{E}^{\tau}_{k_{c_k}}$ ($\tau=16$ zero-byte authenticator) followed by
$s=2$ length-preserving wide-block encipherments $\mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,j}}}$ ($\tau=0$):
\[
\begin{aligned}
  y_{k,0} &= \mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,1}}}\circ\mathrm{E}^0_{k_{r_{k,2}}}\circ\mathrm{E}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}\bigl(\operatorname{pad}(\operatorname{enc}(v_k))\bigr),\\
  y_{k,j} &= \mathrm{D}^0_{k_{r_{k,j}}}(y_{k,j-1}),\qquad
  b_k = \mathrm{D}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}(y_{k,2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{SURB Batching, Cover Loops, Replay Admission, and Security Laws}

\begin{law}[SURB Batching, Constant-Rate Cover, and Sliced-Bloom Replay (D8, Law L9)]\label{law:surb-replay}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{SURB batching:} A Single-Use Reply Block for world-facing session
$\varsigma$ is the 2979-byte client-sealed suffix $\upsilon=(\mathrm{next},\chi_\upsilon,\sigma_\upsilon,x_\upsilon)$~\cite{Chaum1981,Danezis2003}.
A 16\,KiB credit loop batches $k=\lfloor 13\,443/2979\rfloor=4$ SURBs in its
carry plus its own suffix ($k+1=5$ reply credits per forward loop, $Q_{\max}=256$).
\item \emph{Constant-rate cover loops:} Active links emit at constant rate
$r=\rho B/V$ units/s ($\rho=0.9$, substituting cover cells when no real cell is
queued) and maintain an idle floor $r_{\mathrm{idle}}=1\,\text{unit/s}$, sustaining
$\ge 1.32\,\text{MB/s}$ single-client download throughput at $b=16\,\text{KiB}$.
\item \emph{\code{SlotRoster}-backed sliced-Bloom replay admission (Law L9):}
Every received cell is charged $u(b)=b/16\,\text{KiB}$ units against the sender
DID's ledger $\Lambda_i(d)$ ($|\operatorname{dom}\Lambda_i|\le 2\mathcal{R}$,
per-sender budget $B=16\,384$ units, global budget $G=64B$ over $V/Q=5$ quanta)
\emph{before} SEC1 decoding and ECDH.  Valid tags $\nu_i$ are recorded in
expiry-quantized sliced Bloom filters~\cite{Bloom1970,Almeida2007} $R_i[x]$
($K=26$ slices of $M=23\,680$ bits, block false-positive rate $\le 2^{-26}$,
union FP $\le 2^{-20}$, max memory $\approx 5.3\,\text{MB}$), ensuring every AEZ
key is used at most once across restarts and clock rollbacks.
\end{enumerate}
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Onion Pipeline Security Laws (Laws L5, L5$'$, L6, L8)]\label{theo:onion-security}
Under the ideal cipher/AEZ, PRG, MAC, HKDF, and DDH assumptions, every loop built
by Definitions~\ref{def:onion-loop}--\ref{def:aez-carry} satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Law L5 (Noninterference):} $\pi_i(P)=\pi_i(P')\land\sigma(P)=\sigma(P')\implies\operatorname{view}_i(P)\approx_c\operatorname{view}_i(P')$: no hop learns another hop's arguments $\bar{a}_j$, invoke backend name $\omega_j$, or segment value $v_k\neq\hat{v}_i$~\cite{Goguen1982}.
\item \textbf{Law L5$'$ (Position Hiding):} For loops $P,P'$ of arbitrary lengths $H,H'\le\hat{H}=14$, a hop's distinguishing advantage between positions $i$ and $i'$ with matching local projection $\pi^\circ_i$ is bounded tightly by
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}_i\;\le\;\hat{H}\epsilon_{\mathrm{PRG}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{MAC}}+\epsilon_\alpha+\frac{|D_i-D'_{i'}|}{Q}.
\]
\item \textbf{Law L6 (Client Path Authority):} $\mathrm{next}_i$ is sealed in $\lambda_i$ by $\diamond$ independently of runtime values $v_k$, admitting the Arrow composition fragment ($\mathbin{\ggg}$) while excluding promiscuous monadic bind ($\gg\!\!=$)~\cite{Lindley2011}.
\item \textbf{Law L8 (Byte Unlinkability and Tagging Resistance):} Consecutive edges carry independent uniform ciphertexts ($\chi_i\neq\chi_{i+1}$, $y_{i-1}\neq y_i$), class relabeling fails $\gamma_i=\mathrm{MAC}_{K_i}(b\Vert\beta_i)$, and any modification $\tilde{y}\neq y_{k,j}$ is rejected by consumer $\mathrm{D}^{16}_{k_{c_k}}$ with probability $\ge 1-2^{-128}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{AEZ}}$~\cite{Degabriele2018}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Counterparty-Signed Service Receipts and the DRanking 2.0 Bridge}

In an open network where identity creation is free ($\mathsf{cost}(\mathsf{did})=0$),
unsigned local behavior counters or naive median/trimmed-mean votes over unverified
peer reports are vulnerable to Sybil amplification~\cite{Douceur2002}.  Rings 1.0
therefore establishes a cryptographic evidence primitive on the wire---the
counterparty-signed \textbf{Service Receipt} (\code{ServiceReceipt})---which bridges
directly into the companion \textbf{DRanking 2.0} verifiable distributed ranking and
admission protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}.

\begin{definition}[Rings 1.0 Service Receipt (\code{ServiceReceipt})]\label{def:service-receipt}
A service receipt is a dual-authenticated tuple
\[
  \rho=\bigl\langle\mathsf{ver},\,\mathsf{srv},\,a,\,b,\,k,\,n,\,d_{\mathrm{req}},\,d_{\mathrm{rsp}},\,\sigma_a,\,\sigma_b\bigr\rangle,
\]
signed over the canonical domain separator $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}service\mbox{-}receipt\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}$, where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mathsf{srv}\in\{\mathsf{relay},\mathsf{store},\mathsf{probe}\}$ names the metered service kind;
\item $a\in R$ is the service provider DID and $b\in R$ ($b\neq a$) is the served beneficiary DID;
\item $k\in\mathbb{N}$ is the epoch index and $n$ is a fresh per-epoch nonce;
\item $d_{\mathrm{req}},d_{\mathrm{rsp}}$ are the cryptographic digests of the exact signed request and completion transactions; and
\item $\sigma_a,\sigma_b$ verify under the distinct account keys of $a$ and $b$ over $\mathtt{rings\mbox{-}service\mbox{-}receipt\mbox{-}v1\backslash 0}\Vert\mathsf{ver}\Vert\mathsf{srv}\Vert a\Vert b\Vert k\Vert n\Vert d_{\mathrm{req}}\Vert d_{\mathrm{rsp}}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[DRanking 2.0 Bridge Morphism~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}]\label{prop:dranking-bridge}
Because forging $\sigma_b$ for an honest beneficiary $b\notin\mathsf{Adv}$ is
cryptographically infeasible, manufacturable receipts are restricted to
adversarial self-dealing ($b\in\mathsf{Adv}$).  In the companion DRanking 2.0
protocol~\cite{RingsDRanking2026}, verified 1.0 receipts fold into a cap-budgeted
receipt matrix $W_k[b,a]\le w_{\max}$ with normalized stochastic flow
$C_k[b,a]=W_k[b,a]/\max(w_{\max},W_k[b,\cdot]\mathbf{1})$, driving the
seed-anchored attenuated PageRank/EigenTrust~\cite{PageRank,EigenTrust} epoch
evolution
\[
  t_{k+1}=\alpha_k\,C_k^{\mathsf{T}}t_k+(1-\alpha_k)\,s,
\]
\[
  T_k(\mathsf{Adv})=\sum_{v\in\mathsf{Adv}}t_k(v)\;\le\;\frac{\alpha g}{1-\alpha},
\]
where $g=\sup_k\sum_{b\in H,a\in\mathsf{Adv}}C_k[b,a]t_k(b)$ is the honest
endorsement leakage.  Soulbound rank $\hat{t}_k(v)$ then weights VRF committee
sortition and is folded across epochs via Nova-family Incrementally Verifiable
Computation (IVC)~\cite{Nova} so browser nodes verify global admission state in
$O(1)$ time.
\end{proposition}

\section{Related Work}

Distributed hash tables give Rings its closest technical ancestry.  IPFS and
libp2p use Kademlia-derived routing, where distance is measured by XOR and
routing state is organized into buckets~\cite{Kademlia,IPFS,Libp2p}.  This is a
strong fit for content-addressed lookup, but Rings needs a different law: the
owner of a DID or VID is the first live node clockwise from that point, topology
repair follows successor-list and multi-predecessor maintenance, and storage
redundancy places affine rotations of an entry key around the ring.  The paper
therefore uses Chord's circular order and range lemma as the base model~\cite{Chord,Stoica2003},
applies Zave's CorrectChord results for ring safety~\cite{Zave2015,ZaveChordCorrect},
incorporates Bamboo's timeout-only backoff principle~\cite{Rhea2004}, and solves
non-transitive WebRTC clique routing~\cite{Freedman2005,Ford2006} without
unstructured gossip or virtual fingers~\cite{Bryan2008,RFC8840}.

Relay anonymity systems take a different route.  Tor, Mixminion, Loopix, and Nym
route traffic over selected relay paths to hide communication metadata~\cite{TorNetwork,Danezis2003,Piotrowska2017,NymNetwork}.
Rings integrates Sphinx fixed-length headers~\cite{Danezis2009}, Chaum/Mixminion
SURBs~\cite{Chaum1981,Danezis2003}, Loopix-style client loops~\cite{Piotrowska2017},
and AEZ v5 wide-block enciphering~\cite{Hoang2015,AEZv5,Anderson1996,Degabriele2018}
directly into its structured overlay as a typed Kleisli pipeline~\cite{Moggi1991,Plotkin2003,Plotkin2009,Hughes2000,Lindley2011,Mokhov2019}
whose hops are partial $\Sigma$-algebras discovered through the DHT.

On the storage and durability side, Rings combines Shapiro et al.'s state-based
CRDTs~\cite{ShapiroCRDT} and Herlihy--Wing linearizability~\cite{HerlihyWing1990}
with crash-consistent file system and browser IndexedDB commit laws~\cite{Pillai2014,IndexedDB3,BlakeRodrigues}.
Finally, WebRTC and WebAssembly provide portable browser transport and execution
targets~\cite{webrtc-standard,webassembly} that interpret the exact same pure
state transitions as native nodes.

\section{Conclusion}

Rings is an algebraic structured peer-to-peer network: structured because
ownership, range-proved finger convergence, non-transitive WebRTC bridge
routing, affine CRDT storage replication, and age-gated relay custody are
defined over CorrectChord's circular overlay, and algebraic because each layer
preserves an explicit carrier, authority lattice, slot-accounting law, or
Kleisli morphism rather than hiding semantics inside ad hoc network procedures.
