{\centering\bfseries Abstract\par}%
\vspace{0.4\baselineskip}%
\noindent
A Rings identity is a key digest, so anyone can mint as many as they like.  Every table a
remote party can grow must therefore be accounted to something that is not free.  We take the
authenticated neighbour edge: a connection the node itself admitted, of which it holds at most
$\Rg$ at once.  One accounting law covers every such table: each current edge has a reserved
floor, overflow and the charges of departed edges share a pool, and the node-wide bound is the
sum.  An honest edge is then refused only past its own floor with the pool full, and a free
identity consumes at most the share of the edge that carries it.  We apply the law to the
receiver's replay streams (with an eviction horizon that cannot reopen replay), to the removal
store, to received frames through a credit-reclaim protocol that makes the frame bound hard and
independent of the connection count, and to reassembled messages, which are charged once at
their first chunk so that no honest message is refused.  We state the complexity obligations of
the admission hot paths, and show that the barrier's control exemption is the identity
``a lane waits for reassembly iff it carries chunks''.
}\hfill\null\par

\section{Problem}\label{sec:problem}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline]
\item[\issue{907}] The receiver's replay table never evicts, and a new stream fails closed once
the table is full.  Streams are keyed by the origin account, which costs nothing, so 16\,384 key
pairs and one message each lock a node out of every new origin, permanently.
\item[\issue{1060}, residual] The removal store charges each sender a share, and senders are
free keys, so 64 identities fill it.
\item[\issue{934}] Credit bounds the frames held per connection, not per node: about 1.3\,GiB
at the connection cap.  Advertised credit cannot be taken back, so no hard node-wide bound
exists today.
\item[\issue{932}] A reassembled message is re-charged at its full size on the inbound actor,
where it cannot wait, and is dropped when that charge does not fit.  A chunk that finds the
reassembly budget full is dropped too.
\item[\issue{938}] Admission costs $O(n)$ per event and takes node-global locks per frame,
exactly when the node is saturated.
\item[\issue{927}] It is unclear whether the barrier's control exemption is still needed under
per-lane credit.
\end{description}

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\begin{definition}[Principals]\label{def:principals}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item An \emph{origin} $o$ is an account DID, recovered from a signature.  Anyone can create
one, at no cost.
\item An \emph{edge} $e$ is a handshake-authenticated connection the node admitted
(\texttt{EdgeRelation::Neighbour}).  The registry holds at most
$\Rg=2(F+s+1)$ connection records in any phase, where $F=160$ is the number of finger slots and
$s$ the number of successors ($328$ at $s=3$).  So at most $\Rg$ edges are \emph{current} at
any instant.  An edge identity is also a free key; what is bounded is the number of edges
current at once.
\item A \emph{lane} $(e,\ell)$ is one of the four credit-controlled channels of an edge.
\item The \emph{local} principal $\lambda$ is the node itself: its own operations, and
deliveries out of its own stores.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Table]\label{def:table}
A table $T$ is a node-wide set of items that remote input can grow: replay streams, removal
records, frames held, reassembly bytes, mailbox bytes.  Every item $x\in T$ carries a principal
$\pi(x)\in\E\cup\{\lambda\}$, the edge or local principal that \emph{introduced} it, and it
leaves $T$ only by a transition of $T$'s own law (consumption, retirement at a horizon, or an
acknowledged move).
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[What a remote peer can send]\label{as:remote}
A remote party can:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item mint origins without bound;
\item hold up to $\Rg$ edges at once, and replace an edge by a fresh identity whenever
connection admission accepts one;
\item send, on each edge, anything the edge's credit, quotas and admission laws let through.
\end{itemize}
It cannot authenticate as an edge it does not hold.  Sybil and eclipse resistance remain
non-goals; this law bounds what a Sybil consumes, and does not prevent one.
\end{assumption}

\textbf{Refusal and backpressure.}  At a bound, a transition takes one of three forms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \emph{backpressure}: the transition waits for capacity in resource order, and nothing is
lost;
\item \emph{refusal}: it fails with an error that names the bound and changes nothing, and the
sender keeps its copy;
\item \emph{drop}: it is discarded and counted.
\end{itemize}
A drop is allowed only where the law names it (best-effort relay, the application inbox).  It
is never allowed for an honest frame or message at a capacity bound.

\section{The accounting law}\label{sec:accounting}

\begin{law}[Edge accounting]\label{law:edge}
Every table $T$ has a per-edge floor $f_T$, a pool $\Pool_T$ and a local share $L_T$, with
node-wide bound
\[
B_T(s)=\Rg(s)\, f_T+\Pool_T+L_T,\qquad \Rg(s)=2(F+s+1),
\]
so a deployment that raises the successor count $s$ (\issue{774}, \issue{777}) raises the floors
it reserves, $2f_T$ per successor, and nothing else.  Let $\use_e$ be the number of items
introduced by edge $e$, and $o_e=\max(0,\use_e-f_T)$ its pool items.  The \emph{claimants} are
the current edges with $o_e>0$ or a pending item over their floor, and the class $\delta$ of
departed edges, whose items all count as $o_\delta$; let $n\ge1$ be their number.  A transition
that adds an item introduced by $\pi$ is admitted iff:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\pi=e$ is current and $\use_e<f_T$ (the edge's floor); or
\item the pool has room, $\sum o<\Pool_T$, and the claimant is within its fair claim,
$o_\pi<\lceil\Pool_T/n\rceil$; or
\item $\pi=\lambda$ and $\use_\lambda<L_T$.
\end{itemize}
Otherwise it is refused or, where the law says so, it waits.  When an edge departs, its items
stay in $T$, in the departed class, until they leave by $T$'s own law.  No item is ever dropped
to make room: a claimant above its fair claim after $n$ grew keeps what it holds and gains
nothing until it falls below.
\end{law}

\begin{theorem}[Isolation]\label{theorem:isolation}
Under Law~\ref{law:edge}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $|T|\le B_T$ always;
\item a transition introduced by a current edge $e$ with $\use_e<f_T$ is never refused, however
many origins, edges or departed edges any other party controls;
\item an over-floor edge is refused only once it holds its fair claim or the pool is full, and
it is guaranteed $f_T+\lceil\Pool_T/n\rceil\ge f_T+\lceil\Pool_T/(\Rg+1)\rceil$ items as the
other claimants' items expire, with $n-1$ of them;
\item a party holding $k$ current edges, and any number of departed ones, occupies at most
$k f_T+\lceil(k+1)\Pool_T/n\rceil$ items once its excess has expired, and never more than
$kf_T+\Pool_T$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1)~At most $\Rg$ edges are current, each holds at most $f_T$ items within its floor, the pool
holds at most $\Pool_T$, and the local principal holds at most $L_T$.
(2)~The floor test reads $\use_e$ alone, and only $e$'s own transitions change it.
(3)~A claimant below $\lceil\Pool_T/n\rceil$ is refused only with a full pool, which then holds
some claimant above its claim; that claimant gains nothing and its items leave by $T$'s law, so
the pool drains to the fair claims, with $n\le\Rg+1$.
(4)~A party's claimants are its $k$ edges and at most the departed class.
\end{proof}

Accounting to the origin, as the removal store did~\cite{RemovalStability} and the replay
table's quota does, fails part~(4).  An origin costs nothing, so a party controls unboundedly
many, and the only bound left is the node-wide total.  The edge is the unit that is both
authenticated and bounded in number.  The quota per origin stays: it is a rate within an edge,
not a capacity.  Edge identities are free too, but replacing an edge moves its items to the
pool, which Theorem~\ref{theorem:isolation} already counts: rotating edges fills the departed
class, which is one claimant.

\textbf{Who overflows honestly.}  In an overlay of mostly browser nodes, the honest overflow is
on relay edges: a browser reached through a seed sees every origin the seed forwards to it on
that one edge.  Without the fair claim, a party with $k$ edges could take the whole pool and
refuse every honest relay edge for the table's horizon.  With it, a relay edge keeps
$\lceil\Pool_T/n\rceil$ however many edges the party holds.  The alternative of charging only
streams whose origin is the delivering edge, and leaving relayed streams to the origin quota,
reopens \issue{907}: a relayed final-destination stream is exactly the stream a free identity
creates, and the quota is per origin.

\section{Replay streams (\issue{907})}\label{sec:replay}

A receiver stream $s$ admits a transaction only while its proof is live,
$t<ts+ttl$, with $ttl\le\mathtt{MAX\_TTL\_MS}=H$ and $ts\le t_{\mathrm{admit}}+\sigma$.

\begin{definition}[Horizon]\label{def:streamhorizon}
Let $x_s=\max(ts+ttl)$ over the transactions $s$ admitted, each with $ts\le t_{\mathrm{admit}}
+\sigma$ and $ttl\le H$.  The stream is \emph{spent} at $t$ iff $t\ge x_s+\sigma$.  So a stream
of default-lifetime traffic is spent about $10$\,min after its last transaction, and none later
than $H+2\sigma$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Eviction is sound]\label{lem:evict}
A spent stream can be forgotten without reopening replay.  Every transaction it ever admitted is
then expired at this node's clock, even one stepped back by $\sigma$, so a stream opened afresh
at $\mathsf{First}$ refuses them as dead proofs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every admitted transaction expires by $x_s\le t-\sigma$.  Liveness is checked before replay, so
it is refused at the proof, whatever the window holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{law}[Stream admission]\label{law:streams}
The receiver table is a table under Law~\ref{law:edge}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item A \emph{new} stream is introduced by the edge that delivered its first transaction, and a
relay-inbox delivery by $\lambda$.
\item A stream leaves the table only when it is spent, by Lemma~\ref{lem:evict}.  A spent stream
is retired lazily, when a new stream needs its slot.
\item The record of a stream stores $(\pi(s), x_s)$, so a restart recovers the charges.
\end{itemize}
Parameters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $f=32$ new streams per edge, $\Pool=4096$ and $L=1024$, so
$B(3)=328\cdot32+4096+1024=15\,616\le16\,384$, today's capacity, and $B(s)=B(3)+64(s-3)$;
\item the per-origin quota is unchanged.
\end{itemize}
A new stream past its bound is refused with \texttt{TransactionReplayStreamCapacityExceeded}, as
today.  The refusal now lasts until a slot is spent, at most $H+2\sigma$ (100\,min, about
10\,min for default-lifetime traffic), and it reaches only an edge past its floor and its fair
claim.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[No permanent lockout]\label{prop:nolockout}
A party with $k$ edges and any number of origins can refuse new streams only through those $k$
edges, and only once the pool is full.  For every other current edge, a new origin is refused
once that edge has itself introduced $f+\lceil\Pool/n\rceil$ unspent streams.  Every refusal
ends within $H+2\sigma$ of the last admission on the streams that fill the bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{theorem:isolation}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:evict} for the end of a refusal.
\end{proof}

A relay or seed that forwards many distinct origins can fill its own floor legitimately; its
overflow then holds a fair claim of the pool, $\lceil\Pool/n\rceil\ge13$ at worst and $4096$
alone, and past it the honest cost is a refusal on that edge until its streams are spent.  The sender table holds this node's own streams, so it is local.

\section{The removal store, re-keyed}\label{sec:removal}

Law~6 of~\cite{RemovalStability} charged each remove to its sender's origin.  We re-key it
under Law~\ref{law:edge}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item an operation or a sync is charged to the edge that delivered it, and this node's own
writes and repairs to $\lambda$;
\item the holder class goes away: a predecessor or successor is an edge like any other, so its
floor is the same.
\end{itemize}
Parameters: $f=128$ removes per edge, $\Pool=16\,384$ and $L=7\,168$, so
$B(3)=328\cdot128+16\,384+7\,168=65\,536$, today's total, and $B(s)=B(3)+256(s-3)$.  Theorem~\ref{theorem:isolation} then closes
the Sybil residual that \issue{907} was to close: one mechanism serves both tables.

\section{Received frames (\issue{934})}\label{sec:frames}

Let lane $(e,\ell)$ have advertised credit $A$ and released count $R$.  Its liability is
$\liab=A-R$: the frames the receiver may still have to hold, in flight or held.  Today
$\liab\le W=16$ per lane, but credit is cumulative and never shrinks, so $\sum\liab$ is bounded
only by the connection count.

\begin{definition}[Reclaim]\label{def:reclaim}
Two frames are added on the priority lane, both cumulative and idempotent:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\mathsf{Shrink}(\ell,L',g)$, receiver to sender: ``from generation $g$, your limit on
lane $\ell$ is $L'$''.
\item $\mathsf{Returned}(\ell,c,g)$, sender to receiver: ``I committed $c$ frames of $\ell$, and
from now on I commit at most $\max(c,L')$''.
\end{itemize}
Until the $\mathsf{Returned}$ arrives, the receiver counts the lane's liability at its old
$A$.  After it, the liability is $\max(c,L')-R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{law}[Hard frame bound]\label{law:frames}
Every lane has a floor $g_\ell$: $g_{\mathrm{prio}}=2$ for the priority lane (control, and the
reclaim and demand frames), and $g_\ell=1$ for each of the three data lanes, so the first frame
of a burst on any lane never waits a round trip.  A fresh generation grants only the floors.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item A data lane with queued frames and no credit sends a one-frame $\mathsf{Wanted}(\ell)$ on
the priority lane; a receiver that admits a lane's floor frame also grants it pooled credit at
once, so a burst's second frame waits at most the round trip its first frame already took.
\item The receiver grants a lane credit beyond its floor only from the node's frame pool:
\[
\textstyle\sum\liab\le B_F(s)=\Rg(s)\,(g_{\mathrm{prio}}+3g_{\mathrm{data}})+\Pool_F .
\]
\item Demands are served first come, first served.
\item A lane that received no frame for $T_{\mathrm{idle}}$ while holding pooled credit is shrunk
to its floor.
\end{itemize}
Parameters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\Pool_F=256$ frames (16\,MiB, the soft limit of today);
\item $T_{\mathrm{idle}}=$ two observed round trips;
\item $B_F(3)=328\cdot5+256=1\,896$ frames, 118.5\,MiB, and $B_F(s)=B_F(3)+10(s-3)$ frames.
\end{itemize}
This is independent of how many connections are open.  Data-lane floors of $0$ would lower it to
57\,MiB, at the cost of one round trip before the first frame on any idle lane: on an onion loop
(\issue{843}) that is up to one round trip per hop whose lane went idle, on circuit setup and on
the first cell after a pause.  We keep the floor at $1$, and the onion data plane pays nothing.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[Frames]\label{prop:frames}
Under Law~\ref{law:frames}, for an honest sender:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\sum\liab\le B_F$ in every interleaving, with shrink, return and demand frames reordered
and duplicated;
\item no honest frame is refused: a frame sent under credit is within the liability counted for
it;
\item a lane with queued frames is never blocked forever while the receiver keeps consuming
(progress);
\item an idle lane holds at most its floor after $T_{\mathrm{idle}}$ plus one round trip, so it
starves no active lane, and its first frame after the pause is sent at once;
\item control traffic never waits for the pool.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(1)~Pooled credit is granted only when the counted liability fits, and the counted liability
never falls below the true one: a shrink takes effect only once its $\mathsf{Returned}$ confirms
what was committed.
(2)~A frame committed under $A$ is counted until released.
(3)~A blocked lane has a $\mathsf{Wanted}$ queued.  The pool refills as frames are released and
idle lanes are shrunk, and service is first come, first served.
(4)~The shrink frees the lane's pooled credit.
(5)~The priority floor is reserved per edge.
These are the model-checking obligations of the implementation, over the existing credit model
with the new frames.
\end{proof}

This is a wire change: a total cutover, with no versioning before 1.0.  Byte-denominated credit
(\issue{937}) composes with it, by counting $\liab$ in bytes, and is not required here.

\section{Reassembled messages (\issue{932})}\label{sec:reassembly}

\begin{law}[One charge per message]\label{law:onecharge}
The first chunk of a message, with header $[0,n]$ and data length $d_0$, reserves
$m=n\cdot d_0$ (validated $\le$ \texttt{max\_message\_bytes}) once, on the inbound mailbox
ledger: the edge's budget, the lane's share and the shared pool.  It takes that reservation as
an \emph{arrival}, so it waits in resource order and is never refused for capacity.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Later chunks of the message must have length $\le d_0$, and they charge nothing.
\item The reassembly buffer and the completed message live inside that one reservation.
\item The mailbox transition at completion is a move, not a new charge.
\item The reservation is released when the message's processing completes, or when the
reassembly expires.
\end{itemize}
The separate reassembly byte budget and its refusal ($\mathsf{Rejected}(\mathsf{Capacity})$)
are retired.  The id and slot caps stay, as validity bounds.
\end{law}

\begin{proposition}[No honest message refused]\label{prop:noref}
Under Law~\ref{law:onecharge}, no honest chunked message is refused or dropped for capacity, and
no wait can deadlock.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The only charge is the first chunk's, which waits as an arrival before it holds a lane ticket.
The ordering laws of \texttt{ResourceOrderedQueue} cover it, and the capacity it waits for is
released by events that do not wait on it.  The transition that used to re-charge, and could
only be refused, now moves bytes already reserved.
\end{proof}

The cost is direction~1 of \issue{932}: a message holds its full size for its whole reassembly.
This is bounded by the edge's budget and by the reassembly expiry, so a peer that announces and
then stalls holds only its own budget.

\section{Hot paths (\issue{938})}\label{sec:hot}

These are performance obligations, kept apart from the security laws above.

\begin{obligation}[Admission complexity]\label{ob:complexity}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Each arrival, release and cancel in \texttt{ResourceOrderedQueue} costs
$O(\log n)$ in the waiters: waiters are indexed by resource, and one ledger snapshot is taken
per pass.
\item Each received frame takes $O(1)$ lock acquisitions, and none of them on a node-global
mutex: the node load is an atomic counter.
\item The node load has hysteresis: advertisements are deferred above $H_{\mathrm{hi}}$ and
resume below $H_{\mathrm{lo}}=H_{\mathrm{hi}}/2$.  Deferred links are kept in a set, so a
release at the limit wakes no one, and crossing $H_{\mathrm{lo}}$ wakes each deferred link once.
\item Each peer runs one admission watcher, not one task per send.
\item Unread admissions are indexed by peer.
\end{itemize}
They are verified against the sync-storm matrix, before and after each change.
\end{obligation}

\section{The barrier exemption (\issue{927})}\label{sec:barrier}

\begin{lemma}[One frame per lane]\label{lem:oneframe}
If every path that hands a frame to core does so through its lane's FIFO, with at most one frame
in hand-over per lane, then a lane that waits behind the reassembly barrier waits at most one
reassembly frame's service, $\le2$\,s.  That is below the control deadline of 15\,s.
\end{lemma}

The predicate \texttt{lane\_waits\_for\_reassembly} is ``the lane carries logical data''.
Chunks travel only on their message's lane (the lane-mismatch refusal), and control messages
are never chunked, so the predicate is exactly ``the lane can carry chunks''.  In that form it
is the barrier's definition, not an exemption.

\textbf{Decision.}  Keep the predicate, renamed for what it is.  Remove the ablation toggle
\texttt{ProtectionLayer::BarrierControlExemption} and its legacy feedback-loop witness.  This is
a subtraction, under two obligations checked in the implementation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item every hand-off path obeys Lemma~\ref{lem:oneframe}, the pre-admission drain and the
detached submissions included;
\item no control message can exceed one frame.  This is asserted at compile time from the
widest control message.
\end{itemize}

\toprule
Table & Bound & At the bound\\
\midrule

reassembly & inside the mailbox reservation & first chunk waits (backpressure)\\
replay streams & $\Rg(s) f+\Pool+L$ by edge, fair pool & new stream refused, $\le H+2\sigma$\\
removal store & $\Rg(s) f+\Pool+L$ by edge, fair pool & write refused, unacked\\

\bottomrule

\textbf{What a free identity consumes.}  An origin alone consumes nothing beyond its quota on the
edge that carries it.  An edge consumes its floors.  The pool is shared, and is the most that
any number of free identities can take together.
